# When a player can't get past a spot: how shipped games handle it (the lead, 11-07, for Matt)

Trigger: Sally (a child) needed 37 min and 187 falls on Easy 1-1. Matt: "what happens if somebody's just not able to do it ... are those
people we just say that we lose?" The answer from shipped games is no: the good ones give everyone a way through, in layers.

## What shipped games do
- **Nintendo Super Guide** (New Super Mario Bros. Wii, then NSMB U and Donkey Kong Country Returns): after 8 deaths on one level (5 in
  NSMB U), a green block appears. Hit it and the game plays the level for you; you can take control back. Using it dims the save's stars:
  finishing is never blocked, but the record is honest.
  (mariowiki.com/Super_Guide; kotaku.com/your-guide-to-nintendos-gameplay-helping-super-guide-5422253)
- **Crash Bandicoot 2 and 3** (Naughty Dog): quiet, automatic help. After repeated deaths in a section you get a free protective mask, and
  chasers and boulders slow down. The player is never told.
  (blog.activision.com/crash-bandicoot/archives/go-ahead-and-jump; cogconnected.com, N. Sane Trilogy difficulty)
- **Celeste Assist Mode:** a menu the player opens themselves. Game speed down to 50%, invincibility, infinite stamina, skip a chapter;
  mix and match. In 2019 its wording changed from "reduce difficulty" to "fit your specific needs".
  (vice.com, Celeste assist mode change and accessibility; newnormative.com, Celeste's Assist Mode)
- **Super Mario Odyssey Assist Mode:** for young players. Falling into a pit doesn't cost a life (you're put back), there's extra health,
  and arrows show the way.
- **Yoshi's Crafted World Mellow Mode:** a kids' setting. Yoshi can flutter-fly much longer, takes less damage, and secrets are easier to
  find.

## The pattern (four layers)
1. **Teach it well and keep the curve gentle.** It prevents most of the trouble (our Easy fix, first).
2. **Quiet help** (Crash): small, unannounced help after repeated failure at one spot. A slightly wider landing, a longer leaf float,
   a little more jump forgiveness. It keeps a child's pride.
3. **Offered help** (Super Guide, Odyssey): after N failures at the same spot, the game offers to get you past. The player chooses.
4. **A kids' mode** (Mellow, Assist): chosen up front. Falls never cost, the floating is generous, and the way is shown.
Finishing is never blocked. Some games mark the record honestly (Super Guide's dimmed stars). None of them blocks the story.

## The lead's recommendation for Sloths (for Matt to decide with his own research)
- Layer 2, quietly, on Easy only, after 3 falls at the same spot. Use the data: Sally's fall points show where it would fire.
- Layer 3 after 5 falls at the same flag, on both settings. Dot offers: "Want a hand? I'll show you a way." Then a vine or leaf bridge
  appears for that one gap, or Dot carries Pokey to the next flag. The player taps yes or no.
- The petals and stars are kept, with a small honest mark (like Super Guide's). Never a lost life or a lost stage.
- The recorder logs every time help is offered, taken or refused, and where. The spots where help gets used most are the ones the
  designers fix.
- Layer 4 (a kids' mode) only if the data shows Easy plus layers 2 and 3 still isn't enough.
