## Like Super Mario on the NES

Iwata

So you began making Super Mario 3D Land in the mindset of an archeologist, but how was it once you actually got started?

Hayashida

What made a deep impression on me was how the staff said it was like making the Super Mario Bros. game for the Nintendo Entertainment System.

Iwata

In what way?

Hayashida

Well, I think it had many meanings.

Miyamoto

An easy example is how you can firmly stomp on enemies like you did in 2D. That realistic feeling in Super Mario Galaxy or Super Mario Sunshine of being a little off has changed to a more symbolic expression.

Iwata

In other words, you extracted the fun elements of Super Mario on the NES and imbued this game with them.

Miyamoto

Exactly. And when it came to making corrections, we looked back at the processes we went through in the days of the NES.

Iwata

You might say that you wanted the newest game to strongly reflect the point of origin of the series.

Hayashida

Yes, that's right.

Iwata

Tezuka-san, you were in charge of 2D Super Mario games. The 3D Super Mario games have always been Miyamoto-san's sphere, while you were in a position slightly removed from them. What made you become actively involved this time?

Tezuka

I never said much with regard to the 3D Super Mario games so far, but I sensed there were a lot of 2D Super Mario elements in this game, so I had things to say. I was like, "Let me chime in!" (laughs)

Iwata

I heard last time in "Iwata Asks" that when you played the prototype, you offered some harsh advice.

Tezuka

Yes. I thought it best to be clear up front.

Iwata

How did that feel, Koizumi-san?

Koizumi

Well…I thought, "At last, we motivated him!" (laughs) This time, we were putting aside the game element we carried on since Super Mario 646 of searching and were trying to reconstruct the game with the original Super Mario elements. I think perhaps that is why Tezuka-san couldn't help but feel as if it flowed like a 2D Super Mario game.
6. Super Mario 64: The first 3D action game in the Super Mario series. Released for the Nintendo 64 system in September 1996.

Iwata

Instead of having the player wander around the course looking for a Star like in the Super Mario Galaxy series, you have the player advance toward a goal pole, which reflects the origins of Super Mario. When I heard that from Tezuka-san, I thought, "Huh?!"

Miyamoto

On the other hand, when I heard it, I was like, "Oh, yes!" and was ready to go all out! (laughs)

Iwata

Joy and satisfaction were the core of the various points to Tezuka-san's advice. For example, going back to collect scattered Coins was "ungenerous" now that running around 3D space was normal.

Miyamoto

If I may add something regarding Coins, I have had various attitudes toward them myself. When we were making Super Mario Galaxy, at first we had it so the Coins would fly to the player-character of their own. But that felt impersonal, so we changed it so players have to go get the Coins. That's how I rationalized it to myself.

Iwata

After all, the Coins only appear because you want to get them.

Miyamoto

That's right. But in this game, you head for a goal, so if they were scattered back the opposite direction, gathering them would be a nuisance. What was correct in the past wasn't necessarily right this time. And it was changed by me admitting my responsibilities in the matter.

Koizumi

This time there's a timer, so the nature of the game is different. Collecting Coins would have interfered with the speed and tempo of the game.

Miyamoto

A lot changed with regard to the timer as well.

Hayashida

Yes. Tezuka-san said it was too short, so I lengthened it. I think that was another reason that it didn't feel generous.

Miyamoto

In particular, Tezuka-san doesn't trust how you're supposed to go sideways in this game, so he goes all around and does extra actions while he plays, so he was taking longer to clear the levels. (laughs) Well, we do want people like that to play, too, so thinking about it the original time was too short.

Iwata

As someone who had been away from 3D Super Mario for a while, what attracted you about this game, Tezuka-san?

Tezuka

Tanooki Mario, I think. I was the one who made Tanooki Mario for Super Mario Bros. 3, but I think this game displays Tanooki Mario's features much better than in 2D. When you flip enemies, the tail spins right around, but in Super Mario Bros. 3, all you could do was smack left and right. This time you can wipe them out in a full 360-degree circle!

Iwata

Tanooki Mario is perfect for a 3D world.

Tezuka

Yes. I was like, "Good job using something we made so long ago!"

Iwata

Our professor of archeology rediscovered Tanooki Mario!

Hayashida

Thank you! (laughs) Tezuka-san actually isn't that good at playing 3D Mario games, but this time he played through to the end, which pleased me incredibly.

Miyamoto

And that made you think 3D Super Mario is fun, right?

Tezuka

Oh, yes. (laughs)

Iwata

Hayashida-san, having someone who wasn't accustomed to Super Mario in 3D start by playing the 2D-like area and then suddenly find themselves enjoying 3D was exactly what you wanted!

Hayashida

Yes. I saw my first example of success with Tezuka-san! (laughs)

Iwata

Before the game even went on sale!

Koizumi

A couple guys in their 50s and nearing their 60s are having a blast with it, so there's no need to worry!

Everyone

(laughs)
