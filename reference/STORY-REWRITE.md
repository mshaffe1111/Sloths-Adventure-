## World 1: Emerald Canopy

### Act card

*Ten realms lay between Pokey and the Highland Fortress. Each one had a guardian, and every guardian answered to the General.*

### Leaving home

*The squirrels who lived under the old vine had put up a sign years ago, the day Pokey sat on that vine for two days without moving. It still said THE SLOTH, with an arrow pointing up.*

*They'd been watching him leave for two days now. He was almost out of sight.*

**Squirrel:** Hey! Sloth! Where are you going?

**Pokey:** North.

**Squirrel:** North! At that speed? See you next year!

*Somewhere behind the squirrel, a lot of smaller squirrels laughed.*

**Pokey:** I'll write.

### The first petal

*The first petal was stuck to the bark of a kapok tree, pressed flat, at about the height of someone being marched past. Pokey peeled it off with one claw and held it in both hands.*

**Pokey:** She pressed it flat. With a soldier on each arm.

**Dot:** Pokey! Pokey, there you are, oh, thank goodness. Don't move. Don't you move. Do you know how many pink things there are in this forest? Do you have any idea? I followed the petals for two days, and one of them was a hibiscus, which is a completely different pink, and I went all the way around a hill for it, a big hill, and—

**Pokey:** You said you'd be straight back.

*Dot's wing went to the pouch at her side.*

**Dot:** I know what I said. I know what I said. And I said don't you go anywhere without me, and here you are. Going.

**Pokey:** Not very fast.

**Pokey:** Did you see her? Is she okay?

**Dot:** She's okay. She's okay. They took her all the way up to the Highlands, Pokey, to the fortress, the big one up in the clouds, and she walked the whole way with her chin up like she was on her way to a party. She thanked them for the march. She thanked every one of them.

**Pokey:** Did they see you?

**Dot:** One of them looked right at me and said, "Is that a hummingbird?" And a different one said, "Where?" And by then I was already in a bush. I stayed in that bush an hour. It was a nice bush.

**Dot:** Okay. Now listen. They put her in a tower. And there's this netting, all the way around the mountain, iron thorns, from the ground up into the clouds, and there's exactly one gap in it. About as wide as a folded letter. Notes go out. Nothing goes in. I tried to carry one in. Twice. Same thorn, both times.

**Pokey:** So I can't tell her I'm coming.

**Dot:** She knows you're coming.

*Pokey didn't say anything to that.*

**Dot:** She's already gotten three notes out through it. I've got all three. This is the first one.

**Dot (reading):** "Pokey. If you have this, you found the petal. I put it low so you wouldn't have to look up. I saw you run. I didn't know you could do that. Don't you listen to a word he said. And don't stand in the rain and think about it. Follow the petals. Love, M."

*Pokey read it again himself, slowly, the part about running. Then he put it in the satchel.*

**Pokey:** Which way?

**Dot:** North. Everything's north. The petals go north.

*On a branch above them, something long shifted its weight. It had been told to look fearsome and had been doing it since breakfast. It was a warm morning, and it was tired.*

### Hidden note (in plain sight)

> Pokey, if you're reading this, you're already further than they think you'll get. They walk too fast to look down. I've been dropping things the whole way and nobody's noticed. Keep your eyes low.
>
> Sorry about the handwriting. I'm writing this walking.
>
> P.S. If you see Dot, tell her I saw her in that bush.
>
> Love, M.

### I am fine (halfway through the world)

**Dot:** I've got another one. I was saving it. You looked like you needed it.

**Dot (reading):** "Pokey. I'm fine. I have a window, a chair, a blanket that smells like mothballs, and a lieutenant who says sorry every time he brings the porridge. Nobody has been unkind. I am fine. Love, M."

*Far away, in a tower, a sloth sat up straight at a desk by a window. She had been sitting that way for an hour.*

**Pokey:** Let me see it.

*He ran a claw under the writing. The words sat on the line, evenly, from one edge of the paper to the other.*

**Pokey:** She falls off the end of the line. Always. She runs out of room and writes the last word on top.

**Dot:** Look, she even left margins. She had room for—

*Dot stopped.*

**Pokey:** Yeah.

*He folded the note along its own creases and put it in the satchel with the first one. It took him two tries to get the flap shut.*

### The guardian: Venom Fang

*Before the fight.*

**Venom Fang:** Ssloth. What are you doing on my branch?

**Pokey:** I'm going to get Meeca back.

*The snake looked at him for a long time, from the top of his head all the way down, and then back up, slower.*

**Venom Fang:** You're going to take her from him?

*Something under the snake's collar seemed to pinch. He shifted.*

**Venom Fang:** ...From the General. Up there. I was told to stop anyone who followed her. I pictured a soldier. A hawk, maybe. I did not picture this.

**Dot:** Pokey, he's long. He's really long. How long is he? I can't see the end of him. Where does he end?

**Venom Fang:** Further than you'd like. You'll have to get through me, ssloth. Take all the time you need. Someone has to.

**Pokey:** I usually do.

**Venom Fang:** I have been told to bite you. Hold still. It's quicker for both of us.

*After the fight.*

**Venom Fang:** Beaten. By a sloth. Not even a fast one. The slowest sloth in the whole rainforest.

**Pokey:** Wait till the neighbors hear.

**Venom Fang:** If anyone asks, I tied myself in this knot on purpose.

**Pokey:** Sure you did.

*The snake didn't untie himself. He scratched at the iron collar with the end of his tail, where he thought nobody could see.*

**Venom Fang:** Don't look at the collar. It itches. It always itches. She went north, toward the river. She thanked me too, on the way past. Nobody thanks me. The king at the river has a crown now. Don't laugh at it.

### Hidden keepsake

> A tiny iron key on a green ribbon. The teeth are worn smooth from years of being carried. Somebody kept it close.

### Hidden photo album: The Young Scholar

These are the General's younger years. Unchanged except the third caption, which was a little speech.

- **Top of the class.** He was first in his class and didn't seem to know it.
- **Graduation day.** He held the diploma as if it might fly away.
- **Three friends, one bench.** Three friends on one bench. Years later, two of them would sit on his council.
- **The first posting.** A border outpost, a map, and a lot of patience.

### What changed in World 1, and why

**The squirrels are new.** They set up "Wait till the neighbors hear" after the snake fight, so the line answers something instead of landing in the air. They also make the General's "He'll never make it" echo at home. Pokey's "I'll write" is him brushing it off with a joke, because that's what he does when it stings. Cut them if they don't fit.

**Dot coming back late.** She promised "straight back" in the prologue and turns up two days later, which is her fear coming true in the very first scene. Pokey's "You said you'd be straight back" isn't mean. It's just true, and it hits her where she lives. She hits back with "don't you go anywhere without me," because scolding is how her fear comes out. His "Not very fast" is the first time we hear him use a joke to get them both past it.

**Pokey asks about Meeca first.** In the old script he asked whether Dot got away from the soldiers. After two days of not knowing, the first thing he'd ask is whether Meeca's okay.

**Food is gone.** The old World 1 had Meeca's note telling him to eat, and Pokey eating a leaf because she said so ("That isn't eating, that's apologizing to a leaf"). The story notes liked that joke, but it breaks the rule you set: after the kidnapping, nobody's thinking about eating. Her first note now says what Meeca would really be worried about. She knows he'll blame himself for being slow, so she tells him she saw him run and not to listen to the General.

**The handwriting pays off twice.** The note dropped on the march is messy ("I'm writing this walking"), so when the tower note comes in neat, with margins, the player has seen her real handwriting. Dot trails off when she realizes what the margins mean, and Pokey's "Yeah" says the rest.

**The snake follows your direction.** "What are you doing on my branch?" gets "I'm going to get Meeca back," then disbelief, then the slip ("You're going to take her from him?") that only makes sense once the player knows who the General is. Dot's teapot story is gone (the notes called it out, and it was a joke about Dot in the middle of a threat). In its place she panics about how long the snake is. After the fight: "Beaten. By a sloth." Then "Wait till the neighbors hear," with no apology for the knot. The snake softens on his own afterward: the collar, the clue, and the warning about the crocodile's crown, which sets up World 2.

**Fewer hissing letters.** The old snake doubled every s ("Ssstand ssstill"). It reads as clutter in a subtitle box, so now only a word or two per line carries it.

**British and courtroom words are gone.** "Post," "I want that on the record," and "Wonderful."

**One fix after you read it.** The old script had Dot say Meeca "got three notes out before they closed it," which would mean the gap is shut and no more notes can come. But notes keep arriving all game. It now says Meeca has already gotten three notes out through the gap, so Dot can keep flying back for new ones.

## World 2: Sunlit Riverbank

### The village

*The Sunlit Riverbank was wide, brown, and in no hurry. A village of sloths had dug its houses into the bank in a long row, and every front door had the same hand-painted sign: PLEASE BE PATIENT.*

**Pokey:** Huh. I could live here.

**Dot:** Don't you dare.

*At the top of the bank, the village crier, Gruntlewick, was reading out a flood warning. He had started on Tuesday. The flood had come and gone since. He was on the word FLOOD.*

**Dot:** And that's the mailman. With the parcel. He's been taking that parcel to number nine since before the rains started. It says URGENT on it, in red. I offered to carry it for him. He said thank you, and held on tighter.

*The mailman and Pokey passed each other on the path. They nodded. It took a while, and neither of them minded.*

**Gruntlewick:** Young man. You've come a long way. Are your folks behind you? They'll want to know where you've gotten to.

**Pokey:** Somewhere back there. I think they stopped for lunch.

**Gruntlewick:** Ah. Then they'll catch up.

**Pokey:** Any day now.

*Dot had been in the middle of a sentence. She left it there and went and looked hard at a reed.*

*Pokey touched the petal on his wrist.*

*Out on the water, something long and dark slid toward the bank without making a ripple. On its head, slightly crooked, sat a crown of pink petals.*

### Hidden note (in plain sight)

> Pokey, we crossed the river today. The soldiers wouldn't touch the water, so the crocodile carried us over, three at a time. He has a crown now. I made it out of the petals I had left, so there won't be many for a while. You'll find the way.
>
> Don't swim.
>
> P.S. He's nicer than he looks. Don't tell him I said that.
>
> Love, M.

### The raft (halfway through the world)

*The village had promised Pokey a raft. They started building it the morning he arrived. By the afternoon, they had agreed on what kind of wood.*

*Pokey sat on the bank and watched them.*

**Dot:** Pokey. Your foot's tapping.

**Pokey:** It is not.

**Dot:** It is. Look at it. I've known you your whole life, and I have never once seen that foot move on its own.

*Pokey looked down at his foot. He put his hand on it.*

**Pokey:** Is this what it's like for everybody else?

**Dot:** Every single day.

*Dot sat down next to him, which for Dot meant hovering a little above the ground.*

**Dot:** I went back to the netting last night. There was a new one. It's not a good one, and I need you to stay calm. Which you will. You always do. It's the rest of us who— Here.

**Dot (reading):** "Every guardian wears an iron collar with thorns on the inside. They scratch at them when they think nobody's looking. I don't think they chose this. Be kind to them. But please win. Love, M."

**Pokey:** The snake kept scratching his neck the whole time. I thought he had a rash.

**Dot:** It isn't a rash.

*Down on the bank, a sloth carefully fitted the first log of the raft into place, stepped back, and looked at it for a long time.*

**Pokey:** She wants me to be nice to the ones trying to bite me.

**Dot:** That's her whole thing. It's also, a little bit, her whole problem.

*Pokey watched them fit the second log. His foot started tapping again. He held it still with his hand.*

### The guardian: Crocus Rex

*Before the fight.*

**Crocus Rex:** HALT! You stand on the bank of Crocus Rex, King of the Sunlit Riverbank! The toll is one traveler, and today, my friend, you are it.

*The crocodile leaned down and squinted.*

**Crocus Rex:** ...Wait. You're the one following her? You? I've watched logs float past faster than you, and they weren't even trying.

**Pokey:** Nice crown.

**Crocus Rex:** Don't look at it. It's crooked. The little keeper made it for me on the way across, with a soldier on each arm, and it is crooked, and it is staying crooked.

**Pokey:** It suits you.

**Crocus Rex:** Does it?

*A beat. Then, louder:*

**Crocus Rex:** No. NO. Nice try. It is a matter of professional pride that I bite something first.

*After the fight.*

**Crocus Rex:** Beaten! I, Crocus Rex, King of the Sunlit Riverbank, beaten by a SLOTH! The village will never let me hear the end of it. They'll make a sign!

**Pokey:** They're pretty slow. You've got a few years.

**Crocus Rex:** My crown! Where is my... oh. There.

*He put it back on. It was crooked.*

**Crocus Rex:** Fine. You may have the river. The General need not know. And tell her it was never crooked. Tell her I said so.

**Pokey:** I'll tell her.

**Crocus Rex:** Now go. Before I'm ordered to be unreasonable.

### Memory: the waiting

*Pokey remembered the day they met. He'd been crossing a single vine for two days. Below him, a squirrel was nailing up a sign. It said THE SLOTH.*

**Meeca:** You've been on this vine two days. Is it a good vine?

**Pokey:** Very good. I'm nearly halfway.

**Meeca:** Halfway to what?

**Pokey:** The other side.

**Meeca:** And what's on the other side?

**Pokey:** I've lost track. It's still a good vine.

*Every other cub who came along that branch had said "Hurry up" or "Can you move?" She sat down next to him instead and unwrapped a leaf with mango in it.*

**Meeca:** Then I'll wait. I brought breakfast.

**Pokey:** Won't you be bored?

**Meeca:** Nobody's ever let me look at anything this long.

*She waited six hours. When he got to the end of the vine, she was still there, and she had saved him the last piece of mango.*

### Hidden keepsake

> A silver locket, engraved with a single name: Meeca. Inside there is nothing but a pressed petal, still faintly pink.

### Hidden photo album: The Hero of the River

The General's younger years again. Two captions changed.

- **The flood.** A cub was swept off the bank. He didn't stop to ask whose it was.
- **The medal.** They gave him a medal. He kept it in a drawer.
- **Planting the future.** Every year after, he planted one tree where the bank had washed away.
- **A signature.** Peace is mostly paperwork, he used to say. He was good at both.

### What changed in World 2, and why

**The pudding is gone.** The old World 2 was built around a village dinner: Meeca's note said skip dinner and stay for the pudding, and the collar news landed while Pokey had his mouth full. It's the same problem as the leaf in World 1. Pokey's only thought is getting to her. So the village is building him a raft instead, at village speed, and for the first time in his life Pokey is the one waiting on someone slow. His foot taps. "Is this what it's like for everybody else?" is him getting a taste of what he puts everyone through, and Dot's "Every single day" is her loving him and telling him the truth at the same time.

**"Any day now."** When the crier asks if his folks are behind him, the old Pokey said "I expect so," which is British and a little flat. "Any day now" is the sarcasm covering the hurt, and Dot walking off to stare at a reed is her guilt. Neither of them says a word about it, which is the point.

**"I could live here." / "Don't you dare."** That's Dot being afraid he'll stop. It also gives the patient village a second job: it shows the life Pokey could have if he gave up.

**One fewer note.** Dot used to bring a note at the start of World 2 about the crown and the pudding. The crown is already covered: the snake warned him about it, and Meeca's note dropped at the river crossing explains it. That note also explains why the petal trail thins out for a while (she used them on the crown), and it sets up the croc being softer than he acts.

**The croc follows your direction.** He's never heard of Pokey and laughs at him ("I've watched logs float past faster than you"). Pokey's "Nice crown" and "It suits you" can be read as dry or as sincere, and the croc can't tell which, which is his whole problem. After the fight he's grand about losing and worried the village will make a sign, and Pokey's "They're pretty slow. You've got a few years" turns the village gag on him. No "I give it because you were polite," since Pokey wasn't. The crown request stays, because that's his soft side.

**No more seasons.** The mailman's parcel has been on its way "since before the rains started," not since the spring.

**The memory barely changed.** It was already good. The squirrel sign now gets nailed up in the memory itself, since World 1 introduces the sign.

## World 3: Mossy Grotto

### Into the dark

*The Mossy Grotto was lit from above by glowworms. They hung from the ceiling in long threads, and each one hummed a slightly different note.*

**Dot:** I don't do dark, Pokey. I do daylight. I'll do a little dusk if I have to. This isn't dusk. This is the inside of a boot.

**Pokey:** Stay close. I'm dim, but I'm steady.

**Dot:** Dim and steady. I'll take it. I've worked for less.

*Pokey stopped to listen to the glowworms, and the glowworms noticed. One at a time, over his head, they turned up, until he stood in a warm yellow light that hadn't been there a minute before.*

*Between the stalactites hung the old thorn vines that Meeca's grandma always said were asleep. One of them moved.*

*On the floor, in the mud, were footprints. Each one was as wide as Pokey was tall. They went once around the two of them, in a perfect circle, and stopped.*

**Dot:** I'm looking at them with my eyes closed.

**Pokey:** That doesn't make them smaller.

*Far back in the dark, something shifted its weight. Whatever it was, it could stand perfectly still, too.*

### Hidden note (in plain sight)

> Pokey, it's dark in there, and I can't do anything about that. But Grandma told me once that the glowworms only light up for people who stand still and listen. You're the best I know at standing still.
>
> P.S. Ask Dot what I put in her bush.
>
> Love, M.

### All ferns look alike (halfway through the world)

**Dot:** Okay. I have to tell you something, and I need you to not look at me like that.

**Pokey:** Like what?

**Dot:** Like that. Remember my bush? When they marched her past it, she emptied her apron pockets into it. Grandma's things. She keeps them in her apron, she always has, she doesn't like leaving them in the house by themselves. And then there were boots everywhere, so I hid them. In ferns. A lot of ferns. All the way to the mountain and back. Very fast.

**Pokey:** In ferns.

**Dot:** It was a good plan. The ferns were the problem. All ferns look alike. That's the thing about ferns.

**Pokey:** Which ferns?

**Dot:** Ferny ones. Around here, ish. If you crouch down next to the right one, it rustles. Or that might be the wind. I've gotten very good at ferns. I can tell you which ones are rude.

*So the search for Grandma's things began. A sloth crouched beside a fern, and a hummingbird hovered beside the sloth, and the two of them said "Hello?" in very small voices.*

### The guardian: Shadow Prowler

*Before the fight.*

**Shadow Prowler:** Word came down the river that the snake lost to a sloth. I did not believe it.

*Two amber eyes opened in the dark, a long way apart.*

**Shadow Prowler:** I still do not. You will not hit what you cannot find, little sloth. I am the dark between the threads. I try to be.

**Pokey:** I can wait.

**Shadow Prowler:** Everyone says that. Then they hurry. Everyone hurries. Nobody has ever lasted me past the second hour.

**Pokey:** I never learned how to hurry.

*After the fight.*

**Shadow Prowler:** Two hours, and you did not look at the sky once. I got tired first. That has never happened to me.

**Pokey:** Two hours? Try two days on a vine sometime.

**Shadow Prowler:** Meeca went north, toward the clouds. The thorns on our necks are called the Iron Pact. Ask the others how it began. I am not permitted to say.

### The horn

*They came out of the Grotto at dawn, the way you come out of a long sleep: slowly, not quite sure of the time.*

**Dot:** We did it. You did it. I was there, and I was loud, and I think that should count for something.

**Pokey:** You were really loud. It helped.

*To the north, past the last of the trees, a dark point stood on the horizon, narrow and straight. The Highland Fortress. It looked no closer than it had the day before.*

**Pokey:** She's up there.

**Dot:** And the weather lives up there too. I've been trying not to say it.

*The Prowler had followed them out as far as the ledge, keeping to the shade.*

**Shadow Prowler:** North, past the falls. Follow the wind, not the path. And, sloth. When the horn sounds, do not stand near me.

**Pokey:** What horn?

*The note came down out of the north. It was so low he felt it in his feet before he heard it, and back in the Grotto the glowworms went out, one after another, all along the ceiling.*

*It rolled on and on, like thunder. Pokey took hold of the ledge with both hands.*

*The collar on the Prowler's neck turned the red of a coal. His eyes went flat, as if someone far away had put a hand over them.*

**Shadow Prowler:** Run. Run, little one. I cannot—

**General Iron-Claw (from the north, through the collar):** Prowler. The ledge.

*The Prowler's claws came down on the ledge. He fought them every inch of the way, and he wasn't strong enough.*

**Shadow Prowler:** I am sorry. I am so sorry.

*The ledge went the way a dropped plate goes. Dot screamed Pokey's name the whole way down. Pokey didn't say anything at all.*

### The tower: the map

*The General had ordered the pins by the thousand. One for every night the sloth slept somewhere new. The sloth slept a great deal.*

*The pins had run out the week before. Now there were forks standing in the map, in a row, where the last pins should have been.*

**General Iron-Claw:** Forty-four.

**Lieutenant Pell:** Sir, I'm sorry to interrupt. I'm also sorry to report that the mess hall is out of forks.

**General Iron-Claw:** Spoons.

**Lieutenant Pell:** Sir, the spoons are for the soup. I'm sorry. People will notice. Sir. Everyone will notice.

*Pell went away and came back with a tray of spoons.*

**General Iron-Claw:** Four fingers to the tower. He hasn't gone around anything. Not the river. Not the Grotto. He walks straight through.

**Lieutenant Pell:** That's very foolish of him, sir.

**General Iron-Claw:** Yes.

**General Iron-Claw:** Go to bed, Lieutenant. Leave the lantern.

**Lieutenant Pell:** Yes, sir. Good night, sir. I'm sorry about the spoons.

*When the door had closed, the General took a spoon from the tray and drove it into the map beside the last fork, bowl up.*

**General Iron-Claw:** Forty-five.

### Hidden keepsake

> A tarnished medal on a faded blue ribbon. Someone scratched a line on the back with a pin: "For the next cub."

### Hidden photo album: The Statesman

- **A speech to the valley.** They cheered him. He looked slightly alarmed by it.
- **The banquet.** He raised a glass to his fiercest rival, and meant it.
- **The Original Council.** Ten of them around one table. They argued about everything, for twenty years.
- **The study.** After every long day he went home and read. The books outnumbered the medals.

### What changed in World 3, and why

**Where Grandma's things came from.** This is the big one, and it fixes a hole. The old script had Dot say Meeca asked her to hide Grandma's things "before the soldiers came." In our prologue there's no time for that. The birds go quiet, the soldiers are there, and Dot leaves after them. So now, when the column marches Meeca past the bush Dot is hiding in (the bush from World 1), Meeca empties her apron pockets into it. She carries Grandma's things in her apron because she doesn't like leaving them alone in that empty house, which is her whole wound in one small habit. Then Dot, with boots everywhere, stuffs them into ferns "all the way to the mountain and back," which is also why a keepsake turns up in a fern in every world, and why the General says at the very end that everything of hers turns up in a fern. Meeca's World 1 note ("tell her I saw her in that bush") and this world's note ("Ask Dot what I put in her bush") set it up. Tell me if you'd rather it go a different way.

**The Prowler has heard of him.** He's the first boss who's heard the rumor, that the snake lost to a sloth, and he doesn't believe it. That's the start of word spreading ahead of Pokey. Pokey's "Sorry. It's the only trick I have" is gone. Now it's "Two hours? Try two days on a vine sometime," which brags and calls back to the vine memory in World 2.

**The General's voice through the collar.** The old line was "You wait well, sloth. Let us see how you fall," which is a villain speech. Now it's an order: "Prowler. The ledge." Three words, and the Prowler has to obey them. That's colder, and it's how the General talks.

**The map got a fix.** The General used to say Pokey hadn't gone around "the swamp" or "the Reaver," but those are World 6, three worlds away. Now it's the river and the Grotto.

**Smaller things.** Dot's "if there are snacks" is gone (food again). Her "I would like that counted" sounded like a courtroom, so now it's "I think that should count for something." The narration says "Meeca's grandma" instead of "Grandma," so nobody thinks she's Pokey's. Pell is Lieutenant Pell, not "Officer." The old album said the council was five friends, but the Queen says it was ten, so now it's ten.
