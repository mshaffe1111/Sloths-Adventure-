## The five headlines

1. **The stage builder's idea of how far and how high Pokey can go doesn't match the game.**
   - It rates the single jump about 10 px, and the bounce about 14 px, higher than the 30 fps game delivers.
   - It rates a single jump's distance 20–50 px shorter than it really is.
   - As a result, the double-jump lessons can be crossed with one running jump, and the main bounce lesson's walls sit 1–16 px beyond a bounce alone.
   - "The mushroom bounce" was the most-named "hardest" thing in the survey.
2. **A child on Easy was stuck for 37 minutes** with 155 put-backs in one section.

   - Jump help is off.
   - Nothing changes after repeated failure, where the benchmark is to step in after about 5 failures.
3. **The end of the stage changes the rules without saying so.**
   - All 21 lives lost were in the treetop sections at the end.
   - The builder reports that no fall in 1-1 costs a life.
   - The deadly drop under the treetops is painted as the same forest floor seen everywhere else.
4. **Several play-data numbers can't be right.**
   - Double jumps read exactly 2 per run for 8 of 9 players.
   - A 4,000 px section shows a median time of 5 seconds.
   - End sections show 0 seconds.
   - The page announced the length before asking about it.

   Fix the measuring before tuning anything.
5. **The data mostly shows two practised people.**
   - 33 of 42 surveys come from them.
   - The six testers' very first runs took 162, 177, 213, 253, 612 and 2,231 seconds.
   - The only two players who didn't answer "Yes" to playing the next level are the two slowest.

## Findings, ranked by impact on players

### 1. Easy can trap a child forever: it keeps every forced double jump, help is off, and nothing escalates

**What the game does**
- On Easy, a fall puts Pokey back on safe ground with nothing lost (FEEL.md §8). That's like Super Mario Odyssey's bubble rescue, but free.
- Easy also lays narrower gaps and gives longer coyote time.
- But Easy keeps the same sections, and the 136–140 px rises "taken with double jumps" are in them on both settings (STAGES.md §1, table 3a). A single jump tops out at about 123 px, even with the 26 px climb-up. So on Easy every rise still demands a double jump.
- On touch, a double jump is a second, separately recognised flick inside about 0.6 s of air time. "Jump help", which makes that second flick far easier, is **off by default, including on Easy** (FEEL.md §4, §10).
- Nothing reacts to repeated failure:
  - the only escalation is an extra-lives offer after 3 Game Overs;
  - Easy has no Game Overs, because falls are free.

**The evidence**
- Player I (Easy, one run):
  - 2,231 s, against an Easy median of 206.
  - 187 put-backs, 83% of all 224 in the whole test; 155 of them in `w1-log-chain` (floating logs, two 136–140 px rises).
  - In the survey: hardest = "The tall steps (double jump)" and "Bounce, then double jump"; Dot's tips helped? "No"; play the next level? **"No"**. The only "No" in the test.
- The other slow player, H (612 s and 444 s), answered "Maybe" twice. Every other answer was "Yes".
- Player A mentions "rocks move in to cover holes the player would fall into". If that's an adaptive aid, it didn't reach the rises.

**What strong games do** (BENCHMARKS.md §8)
- They step in after repeated failure in one place:
  - 5 failures in New Super Mario Bros. U, Donkey Kong Country Returns 3D and HD, Super Mario 3D Land and Yoshi's Woolly World;
  - 8 in the older New Super Mario Bros. Wii.
- They climb a ladder. Super Mario 3D Land gives invincibility at 5 deaths and a skip at 10.
- Their easiest modes remove the failing skill rather than repeat the test. Woolly World's Mellow Mode gives Yoshi wings and continuous flight; Odyssey's bubble carries Mario out.

**Suggested change**
- **WHAT:** count failures per section, and climb a ladder:
  - at 3, Dot *shows* the move (flies the arc);
  - at 5, the obstacle changes: a step appears under the rise, or a mushroom, or a short leaf hover is granted;
  - at 10, offer "Dot carries you to the next flag".
- **WHAT:** on Easy, switch Jump help on by default.
- **WHAT:** consider giving Easy a short leaf glide from 1-1. The glide exists in the game; it just unlocks at 4-1.
- **WHY:** the one child in the test is the one player who said "No" to the next level. A tenfold overrun on the first stage is the strongest drop-off signal in your data, and Easy as built cannot help with the one skill that failed.

**How you'd know it worked**
- The most put-backs in one section in one run drops from **155** to about 15.
- Finished runs longer than twice the median drop from **3 of 44** to 0.
- First-time players on Easy answer "Yes" to the next level.

**Confidence:** high. **Fact** that the loop exists and why; the trigger counts are **taste** with strong precedent.

### 2. The builder's reach numbers don't match the game, so the lessons don't test what they teach

This is new, and it explains pass-1 #3 and #5.

**What the game does**
- **The single jump in the physics file.** FEEL.md §3 shows the game's physics file working out a single jump's length as 179.7 px. That's the distance Pokey's *middle* travels, worked out with the smooth formula, at top speed, with the jump pressed exactly at the edge.
- **The double-jump gaps.** The lessons lay "double-jump" gaps of 195–203 px, described as "wider than a single jump reaches" (STAGES.md #8). That fits the 180 px figure.
- **The builder's heights** (STAGES.md §0) are formula values too: jump about 108 px, double about 185 px, and bounce plus double about 258 px. The 258 is the formula bounce (181 px) plus the formula double (77 px).
- **The real game steps at 30 fps.** That makes every launch peak lower than the formula says:

  | Launch | Formula | 30 fps game |
  |---|---|---|
  | Jump | 107.8 px | 97.2 px touch, 97.8 px held |
  | Bounce | 181.3 px | 167.6 px |

- **Three things make real jumps longer than planned.** Pokey is 44 px wide. The game catches him on edges and lifts him onto ledges up to 26 px above his feet. And his 214 ms coyote time lets a jump start up to 6 ticks after he runs off the edge, carrying him further.

**My simulation:** a single touch jump from a full run on 1-1 Normal. Widest level gap crossed, in px:

| When the jump registers | Strict reading | Middle | Generous |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 tick (33 ms) early | 181 | 187 | 195 |
| exactly at the edge | 190 | 197 | 205 |
| 1 tick late | 200 | 206 | 214 |
| 3 ticks late | 209 | 215 | 223 |
| 5 ticks late (last coyote tick) | 218 | 225 | 233 |
| standing still at the edge (no run) | 151–173 | 157–179 | 165–187 |

How to read the table:
- *Strict* means his middle must be within 15.8 px of the far edge when he comes down, with no climb-up or nudge.
- *Middle* means any part of his body over the far edge counts as he comes down, plus the 26 px climb-up.
- *Generous* adds the 8 px nudge as well (both rules are in FEEL.md §1 and §5).
- All three assume he leaves the ground the moment his middle passes the edge. If he stays on until his whole body is off, add about 16–22 px.
- A flick takes at least 43 ms of finger travel, plus up to 33 ms to the next tick, plus the phone's own touch delay. So a flick *started* at the edge usually registers 2–5 ticks late.

**Result: double-jump gaps that a single jump crosses**
- A running player crosses the 195–203 px "double-jump" gaps with a single jump under every reading once the jump registers one to three ticks late, which is normal for a flick. Under the middle and generous readings, one tick late is enough.
- A cautious player who stops at the edge can't cross them.
- So the double-jump lesson tests whether you run and when you flick more than whether you double jump.
- The 227 px gaps in the treetops do need the double, except under the generous reading with a very late jump.
- On Easy, the treetop gaps are about 202 px, so on Easy *no* level gap in 1-1 forces a double jump on a running player; only the rises do.

**Result: bounce walls just beyond a bounce alone**
- A bounce alone lifts Pokey's feet 167.6 px above the mushroom top. The mushroom is 30 px tall, and the climb-up works on faces up to 26 px above the feet. So a bounce alone reaches walls about **224 px** tall.
  - `w1-hop-bounce` (116 of 138 layouts, the main bounce lesson) uses walls **225–240 px** tall. They miss by 1–16 px: Pokey's head rises above the wall top, and then he falls back. With the formula bounce (181 px), the same arithmetic gives 237 px, which is probably how the walls were sized.
  - `w1-mushroom-bank-teach` and `w1-mushroom-lift` (200–220 px banks) *are* reachable with a bounce alone, which corrects my pass 1.
- **The survey:** "The mushroom bounce" was named hardest 8 times out of 19 answers, more than everything else combined. The section ranking can't see it, because a missed bounce just lands on the floor (0 put-backs in 59 entries).

**What strong games do** (BENCHMARKS.md §6, §13)
- A lesson is a place where only the new move works, and where it clearly works.
- Generated games that feel designed (Spelunky, Dead Cells) keep the generator's rules tight and every piece hand-tested.
- Their reach numbers come from the real game.

**Suggested change**
- **WHAT:** have the builder take its reach numbers from the 30 fps simulation the team already has (FEEL.md's "30 fps sim" figures). Include Pokey's width, the catch and climb-up rules, and a jump that starts as late as the coyote window allows.
- **WHAT:** give every lesson a clear margin:
  - a "must double jump" obstacle is a **rise** of 136 px or more (as in `w1-bank-double-teach`, today in only 5 of 138 layouts), or a gap of about 260 px or more on Normal. Easy's gap rule (0.65 of reach) can't fit a forced-double gap, so on Easy use rises.
  - a "one bounce" target sits at least 15 px under the real bounce reach, so 205 px or less for a floor mushroom.
  - "bounce, then double jump" belongs in the Combine slot, where `w1-bounce-double` already teaches it cleanly.
- **WHY:** one change fixes both lessons and every future world. At the moment the tools certify lessons that teach something else.

**How you'd know it worked**
- The builder reports the share of "double" gaps a single 30 fps jump can cross. Today that's all 195–203 px gaps; it should be 0.
- "Hardest: the mushroom bounce" falls from **8 of 19** answers.
- Put-backs per entry in the double-jump lesson are about **0.32–0.45** today, excluding the child. After the change they should come from real double-jump misses, which the fixed counter will show.

**Confidence:** high for the heights (FEEL.md's own numbers). Medium-high for gap reach: the table brackets the unknown rules, and even the strictest reading crosses 200 px with a one-tick-late jump. **Fact.**

### 3. The treetop finale changes the rules without saying so, and looks like the safe ground

Pass-1 #2 and #15, strengthened.

**What the game does**
- STAGES.md §2: "Every gap is a soft fall... none costs a life." The builder counts only gaps on the route (`gen2.falls`).
- But the floating logs and boughs of the last third stand over open air. A fall off their edges or ends is a life on Normal.

**The evidence**
- All **21 lives lost were "pit" falls in treetop sections**:

  | Section | Lives lost | Entries |
  |---|---|---|
  | `w1-log-chain-rise` | 5 | 8 |
  | `w1-mushroom-chain-back` | 5 | 11 |
  | `w1-mushroom-chain` | 4 | 7 |
  | `w1-log-chain` | 3 | 19 |
  | `w1-canopy-rungs` | 2 | 4 |
  | `w1-canopy-rungs-level` | 1 | 20 |
  | `w1-high-walk-up` | 1 | 8 |

- That's 0.54 lives per Normal run, and none anywhere else in the stage.
- In the strips, the space under the treetop logs (strip B second row, right side; strip A's finale) shows the **same painted forest floor** of fallen logs, ferns and pale mushrooms that sits behind the safe parts of the stage. There's no visible drop, mist or darkness, and no Dot warning (as far as the files say). In-game mushrooms carry a glowing ring with an up arrow, so those can be told apart; the deadly drop can't be told from a safe one.
- Catch boughs exist only under canopy *gaps* (FEEL.md §8). There are none under rises or at the ends of logs.

**What strong games do**
- "Cheap shots and surprise attacks are almost never fun": that's Nintendo's lesson on *treating the player fairly* (BENCHMARKS.md §6). Danger is shown before it costs you.
- Odyssey, Galaxy and Wonder turn pits into rescues on their assist settings (§5).

**Suggested change**
- **WHAT:** on 1-1, either make treetop falls put-backs on Normal too (your double-jump gaps already are), or make the rule visible. Three ways to do that:
  1. paint deadly drops as drops (mist or deep shadow, not ground);
  2. add catch boughs under rises and log ends;
  3. have Dot say once, entering the canopy, that it's a long way down.
- **WHAT:** have the builder count every edge a fall can go over, and apply its tighter 2,300 px checkpoint rule there.
- **WHY:** the stage's hardest part is also its only deadly part, its art says it's safe, and the tools say no life can be lost. Nobody is looking at the spot that ends runs.

**How you'd know it worked**
- Lives lost per Normal run fall from **0.54** to about 0 (if made soft), or the builder lists the deadly edges per section.
- Time in `w1-log-chain`, now 1.9 times the plan, comes down.

**Confidence:** high that the mismatch exists and where. Medium on how alike the two floors look on a phone screen; I only saw the strips. **Fact.**

### 4. Fix the measuring before tuning: several numbers in the play data can't be right

New.

**The evidence**
1. **The double-jump counter.**

   | Player | A | B | C | D | E | F | G | H | I |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | Double jumps per run | 1.94 | 2.0 | 2.0 | 2.0 | 2.0 | 2.0 | 1.0 | 2.0 | 2.0 |

   (G: 2 double jumps in 2 runs, one of them unfinished.) Every one of these players finished runs whose layouts force more:
   - `w1-hop-bounce` alone (116 of 138 layouts) needs two bounce-plus-double moves;
   - every Showpiece except `w1-canopy-rungs-level` and one version of `w1-mushroom-chain` has two to four rises "taken with double jumps";
   - `w1-log-stair-test` (75 layouts) adds more.

   Most layouts need at least 4. The child, who spent 37 minutes in double-jump sections, also reads 2. The counter is capped, or it counts some other fixed event.
2. **Section times that are physically impossible.**
   - `w1-leap-stones` is 4,086–4,451 px long, at least 14 s at top speed. Its median time is **5.1 s**.
   - The End sections read **0.0 s** (`w1-quiet-arrival`, `w1-sunlit-log`).
   - "Entries" also run higher than runs (59 entries of `w1-hop-bounce` in 47 runs), so re-entries are being counted.
3. **The length question was answered after the length was announced.** The testers' page says "One level, about 3 minutes", and "How long did it feel?" came back "About right" **42 of 42** times.
4. **Settings and saves aren't recorded per run.**
   - Player B wrote that "once the float move is unlocked the petal jumps feel right; on a first play of stage 1-1 most players will not have the float yet". That suggests at least some of B's 1-1 runs had the leaf glide, which a first-time 1-1 player can't have.
   - Jump help, control mode, steering hand and orientation per run aren't in the data either.
5. **The section ranking can't see failures that aren't falls**, such as missed bounces.

**What strong games do.** Nintendo's lesson *Watching Others Play Your Course*: watch closely, and ask testers about difficulty spikes and confusion (BENCHMARKS.md §6).

**Suggested change**
- **WHAT:** before changing the game, fix the counter and the section timing.
- **WHAT:** log attempts per obstacle: bounces per mushroom, double-jump presses per rise, jumps that fell short.
- **WHAT:** log settings and unlocked skills per run.
- **WHAT:** ask "how long did it feel?" without announcing the length first.
- **WHY:** every other finding here is measured with these numbers.

**How you'd know it worked:** add a data check that each run's double-jump count is at least the minimum its layout needs, and that no section time is below its minimum crossing time. Both fail today.

**Confidence:** high. **Fact.** Do this first: it's cheap.

### 5. The play data is mostly two practised people, and the first-time picture is much harder

Pass-1 #9, strengthened.

**The evidence**
- Players B, D, E and F are one person, and G played on B's phone. That one person gave **21 of the 42** surveys; A gave 12; so **33 of 42 come from two people**.
- **First-ever run of each person**, counting G separately:

  | Player | A | B | C | G | H | I |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | First-run time (s) | 213 | 177 | 253 | 162 | 612 | 2,231 |

  The median is 233 s. **Two of six took over ten minutes.**
- The headline median of 204 s is mostly repeat plays.
- **The only non-"Yes" answers to "Would you play the next level?" came from the two slowest players**: H ("Maybe" twice) and I ("No").
- "Did you know what to do?" got "Mostly" 18 times out of 42, including 10 of A's 12, even after many runs.

**What strong games do.** The Mario team's help systems exist because first-timers fail in ways practised testers don't. **Suggested change**
- **WHAT:** run 10 or more first-time players, including 3 or more children (record their ages), on the same 3–5 fixed layouts.
- **WHAT:** record video, or use the touch recordings the testers' page already makes ("it records where your fingers touch and what Pokey does").
- **WHY:** the current numbers can't tell "the stage is right" from "two people have learned it".

**How you'd know it worked:** you get a first-run median and spread you can act on, and a next-level "Yes" rate among first-timers.

**Confidence:** high. **Fact.**

### 6. The double jump needs a clean lesson and a visible "ready" signal

The rest of pass-1 #3, plus #12.

**What the game does**
- The real double-jump lesson for most players turns out to be `w1-log-stair-test` (Challenge, 75 of 138 layouts): rises of 136–140 px. It works: 21 entries, 0 put-backs, on its planned time.
- The "Intro: double jump" slot uses gaps (finding 2).
- Nothing in FEEL.md shows the player whether a second jump is still available. Only the double jump's 14 rad/s somersault marks it.
- Dot's tips went unnoticed: "Didn't notice them" 12 of 14.

**What strong games do**
- Super Mario Run's Easy Mode shows a bar that fills while the finger holds, so you can see your jump's strength (BENCHMARKS.md §2).
- The Mario games show, rather than tell: Luigi plays the level for you; Odyssey puts arrows on the ground.

**Suggested change**
- **WHAT:** make the double-jump lesson a rise over soft ground in every layout (`w1-bank-double-teach`, or move `w1-log-stair-test` earlier).
- **WHAT:** give Pokey a visible "second jump ready" cue, such as a glow on his leaf or satchel that goes out when it's used.
- **WHAT:** have Dot *demonstrate* the first time, flying the double-jump arc, instead of a text tip.
- **WHY:** a move the player can't see the state of is a guess, especially for a child doing two flicks in 0.6 s.

**How you'd know it worked**
- "Always" answers to "Did you know what to do?" rise from **24 of 42**.
- First-timer put-backs at the first forced double fall.
- Dot noticed by more than **2 of 14**.

**Confidence:** medium-high. **Fact** that there's no cue; the cue's form is **taste**.

### 7. Petals lure players off edges, and the treetop "rest" sections aren't rests

Pass-1 #8, strengthened, plus new evidence.

**The evidence**
- Player A: "Jumped off cliffs while trying to collect the pink balls."
- Player B, twice: petals "on a log directly under another log"; "jumping kept landing on the upper log"; "Lost one life trying".
- Player F: petals "on a log that could not be jumped to or dropped onto... had to run off an edge and fall onto it".
- B also noted the petal lines "read" right only with the leaf glide, which a fresh 1-1 player doesn't have.
- Petal side roads like these sit in the two-level Fork roads (for example `w1-twin-mushroom-fork`'s logs about 195 and 380 px up) and in the four **bough "Rest" sections** (`w1-bough-bench-up`, `w1-high-walk-up`, `w1-high-walk`, `w1-bough-bench`, 67 layouts), which come just before the Showpiece. Each bough rest asks for:
  - a single jump across a gap: up to 85 px with a 50–80 px step up, or up to 142 px on the level versions;
  - plus an optional drop to a petal road 136–144 px below;
  - in the zone where falls can kill (`w1-high-walk-up`: 1 life lost in 8 entries).

**What strong games do**
- Nintendo, *Mario's Limitations*: don't put coins where Mario can't get them. "If you were to go after the four Coins, you'd fall into the hole. But as we've discovered, most humans will go after them anyway."
- Nintendo, *Coins*: place coins "to lure Mario toward places I want him to jump to" (BENCHMARKS.md §9).

**Suggested change**
- **WHAT:** add a builder check that every petal can be reached with the moves a fresh save has (no glide) and with the real touch jump. No petal line may lead off an edge that costs a life. A side road is entered at its start, by an obvious walk-in, or by a drop onto soft ground.
- **WHAT:** make the bough "rests" flat and safe, and put the petal side roads on the ground earlier.
- **WHY:** players follow petals. Petals that point off deadly edges, or at places the jump can't reach, teach players that the game's hints can't be trusted.

**How you'd know it worked**
- Petal complaints (today from A, B and F) drop to 0.
- No lives are lost in bough rests (today 1 in 8 entries).

**Confidence:** high. **Fact.**

### 8. Taps are read as punches in a stage with nothing to punch

Pass-1 #7, strengthened.

**What the game does**
- Any short tap becomes a punch, and a still finger becomes a mega punch after 550 ms (FEEL.md §7).
- A second finger that lands while the other thumb steers fires a punch at once, and cancels it only if it moves 12 px within 100 ms.
- 1-1 has no creatures.

**The evidence**
- 488 punches were read, 161 of them cancelled; there were 0 creature hits.
- The cancelled ones are mostly second-finger jump flicks that started as punches: G had 114 punches with 102 cancelled, in one finished 162 s run plus one unfinished run; E had 88 with 55.
- The child had **167 punches, none cancelled**, about 4.5 a minute. Those are single-finger taps and short flicks read as punches when the finger lifts. FEEL.md lists a short or slow flick up (a rise under 12 px, or a peak under 0.35 px/ms) as one that gets read as a punch.
- A punch also slows Pokey (×0.85 for about 0.28 s). The bigger cost is a jump that didn't happen.

**What strong games do**
- Platform norms are close to the game's: a long press is 400 ms on Android and 500 ms on iOS (BENCHMARKS.md §11).
- Praised phone platformers give each finger one job (§3).
- Children 7–10 make "holdover" touches seven times as often as adults, and their gestures are recognised 81% vs 90% of the time (§12).

**Suggested change**
- **WHAT:** read punches and mega punches only when something punchable is near, as your kick already does (within 260 px). At minimum, do this on stages with no creatures.
- **WHY:** every punch in 1-1 is a misread. For a child, some of them are missed jumps.

**How you'd know it worked**
- Punches per minute on 1-1 fall from about **2.5** (4.5 for the child) to about 0.
- In the touch recordings, put-backs within a second of a punch read disappear.

**Confidence:** high. **Fact.**

### 9. A first stage drawn from 138 layouts can't be polished, and it's long for a first level

Pass-1 #6 and #10, softened.

**What the game does**
- Each Play takes the next of 138 shuffled layouts (STAGES.md §1).
- 47 runs covered 43 layouts, so no layout has been seen enough to judge.
- First stage length is 174–230 s by the builder's own model.

**The evidence**
- Excluding the child, lives per entry by Showpiece range from 0.05 (`w1-canopy-rungs-level`) to 0.63 (`w1-log-chain-rise`). That's with only 4–19 entries each, too few to tell layout from player.
- Time against plan ranges from 1.0 times to 1.9 times (`w1-log-chain`).

**What strong games do**
- Super Mario Run's first course is fixed and has a 60 s limit. Its replay variety comes from remixing the coins (pink, then purple, then black) on the same course.
- Super Mario Bros.' 1-1 budgets 160 s of real time.
- The median phone session is 5–6 minutes (BENCHMARKS.md §3, §7).

**Suggested change**
- **WHAT:** for a player's *first* 1-1, use one layout or a small tested set (3–5) that passed a first-timer test. Keep the shuffle for replays and later stages.
- **WHAT:** consider remixing petals for replay variety, as Mario Run does.
- **WHAT:** restrict 1-1's Showpiece to the forgiving ones (the canopy rungs, with their catch boughs) until data says otherwise.
- **WHY:** you can only polish what you can replay. Every new player's first impression is currently a different, untested stage.

**How you'd know it worked:** the spread of first-run times narrows; per-layout results become possible.

**Confidence:** medium. **Taste**, with strong genre precedent. Your survey ("About right", fun 4.56) disagrees, but it was answered by practised players after the length was announced.

### 10. Touch has one jump height, but don't change it blind

Pass-1 #4, advice changed.

**What the game does**
- Every touch jump counts as held for 220 ms, so a flick peaks at 97.2 px and a long hold at 97.8 px. Celeste's range runs from 18% to 100% of full height (BENCHMARKS.md §0).
- The petal complaints in finding 7 are partly about wanting a short hop.

**The risk.** If height came from how long the finger stays down, every natural "flick and lift" would get a lower jump: 62–75 px at a minimum of 80–130 ms. Every layout is built for about 97.

**Suggested change**
- **WHAT:** first enforce the petal and layout rules (finding 7).
- **WHAT:** then use your touch recordings to measure how long fingers stay down after a jump flick.
  - If most stay down past 220 ms, test Super Mario Run's and Leo's Fortune's approach: flick and lift for a short hop, flick and hold for full height or a short hover.
  - If most lift early, keep one height.
- **WHY:** it's a real gap against the benchmarks, but the cure could hurt more than the disease.

**How you'd know it worked:** fewer notes like B's and F's; more first landings on the surface the player aimed for (from the recordings).

**Confidence:** high on the mechanics. **Taste** on whether to change.

### 11. Few layouts get a twist, and the pool's unused third has the same cause

### 12. Pacing: three new moves in under a minute, then almost no downtime

New.

- The first four sections are always Start, then lessons on the jump, the double jump and the bounce: about 45–55 s of new moves back to back.
- The first Rest comes at section 6–9, roughly 70–120 s in.
- Rests take 4–6 s each (median times; `w1-meadow` is 12 s), so roughly 5–9% of the stage.
- The builder caps "nothing to do" at 2–4 s (STAGES.md §2, FEEL.md §10).
- Nintendo's lesson, *All about Pacing*: "Sometimes it's better to leave an area completely empty... downtime creates contrast." The research on generated platformers builds stages from rhythm groups separated by rests (BENCHMARKS.md §6, §13).
- **WHAT:** give each new move a short practice before the next arrives (jump lesson, practice, double lesson, practice). Add one real breather, a canopy "view" moment, before the treetop finale. The stage is called "Into the Canopy"; the reveal is the natural rest.
- **Confidence:** medium. **Taste.**

### 13. Feel: input, camera and touch

- **Input delay is the reason for the generous coyote time, and that's fine.**
  - Phone touch delay is 55–123 ms on 2013 phones. A flick needs at least 43 ms of travel. The game reads input at the next tick (0–33 ms).
  - So a jump registers roughly 100–200 ms after the finger starts.
  - The 214 ms coyote window pays that back. But the builder must then assume late jumps (finding 2).
- **Frame rate.**
  - The Adreno 619 phone in landscape ran a median of 25 fps (10th percentile 19.5); H's Samsung Internet a median of 26.4; the iPhone 28.4.
  - The iPhone changed quality level 56 times in 16 runs, about 3–4 visible sharpness changes per run.
  - Jump height moves only ±4% with frame rate, so the issue is smoothness, not fairness.
  - **WHAT:** on the iPhone, after a step down, don't step back up within the same stage.
- **Camera.**
  - Upright 412 × 915 shows about 1.1 s of path ahead at full run; sideways 915 × 412 about 2.7 s. Both meet or beat Super Mario Bros.
  - Player C's "picture shifted when climbing up onto logs" is the vertical catch-up after landing on a higher log (the camera eases at 6.5/s, a time constant of about 0.15 s). Worth a look on a phone; I couldn't play it.
  - Player D's logged "camjump" (138 px in one frame, on Easy) may be the camera snapping on a put-back.
- **Landscape.** B felt landscape was "less responsive for jumping and moving" on a phone that held 30 fps both ways, so frame rate isn't the cause. Compare B's touch recordings upright against sideways.

**Confidence:** medium. Mostly **fact** from the files; I couldn't feel any of it.

## Missing entirely

Techniques strong games use that this game, as documented, doesn't have:

1. **Help that escalates where the player keeps failing.** Super Guide after 8, later 5; Super Mario 3D Land at 5, then 10; Woolly World at 5; Crash's quiet extra masks and checkpoints.
2. **A "show me" or "skip this bit" option** (Luigi or Super Kong playing for you; Odyssey's arrows; Celeste's chapter skip).
3. **A hover or float on the easiest setting** (Mellow Mode's wings; Leo's Fortune's hold to hover). Pokey's leaf glide exists but unlocks at 4-1.
4. **A visible jump state**: "second jump ready", or Super Mario Run's jump-strength bar.
5. **Variable jump height on touch** (Super Mario Run's hold for higher). Measure first (finding 10).
6. **A fixed, hand-tuned first stage**, with replay variety from remixed pickups (Super Mario Run's pink, purple and black coins).
7. **A visual language for deadly vs safe drops.**
8. **A builder check that every pickup is reachable** with the moves a fresh save has, and never leads off a deadly edge (Nintendo's *Mario's Limitations*).
9. **Telemetry of attempts, not just failures**: tries per mushroom, presses per rise. Settings and unlocked skills logged per run.
10. **Children-specific input handling**: ignore taps with nothing to punch; on Easy, read any upward movement in the air as the second jump (your Jump help already does this; it's just off).
11. **Real downtime**: an empty breather. The builder's 2–4 s cap rules it out.

## They may be right

Choices outside the usual pattern where the evidence supports the team:

- **The generous coyote time and jump buffer** (214–300 ms, against Celeste's 100 ms). On a phone, the flick plus touch delay plus the tick eats most of it; no tester complained about missing ledges. The cost is only that the builder must plan for late jumps (finding 2).
- **Air control and jump timing.** Air steering at 0.65 of ground acceleration is *exactly* Celeste's `AirMult = .65`. Pokey's 0.63–0.67 s in the air matches Madeline's 0.68 s. The core feel is in good company, and the fun scores (4.56 Normal, 4.50 Easy) agree.
- **Soft pits on the ground.** It's Odyssey's bubble rescue made free, and it suits a first stage. No life was lost on the forest floor in 47 runs, and A liked "how rocks move in to cover holes".
- **The builder's time model.** It predicted a median of 188 s (Normal) and 201 s (Easy); the actual medians were 204 s and 206 s. That's within 8%, unusually good.
- **The stage's shape.** It rises from the forest floor "Into the Canopy" on four hand-made outlines. That is exactly the Dead Cells approach: a designed skeleton, hand-made pieces, constrained assembly.
- **The bounce mushrooms read well.** In the stills, the glowing ring and up arrow clearly mark a bounce.
- **The same layouts on Easy**, with narrower gaps and wider landings. It assists the same content, as Odyssey and Celeste do.
- **The quiet End section**: a cool-down after the climax.
- **Upright framing**: 1.1 s of road ahead at full run, more than Super Mario Bros.
