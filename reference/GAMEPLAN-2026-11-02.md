## 3. Retirement (rewritten 2026-11-02 with Matt: "sometimes you just will have bad employees... But if you think that you've done everything right and you set the employee up to have success, that's when you cut ties. And maybe three's a little quick... ensuring that the employee understands the roles and has enough time to adjust.")

**Immediate, no ladder (trust and safety):**
- Hiding or misreporting (a rough spot not named, a check claimed but not run, a fact stated that it didn't check). Law zero.
- Spending Hugging Face money without Matt's approved list; pushing to main or opening a release PR on its own.

**Everything else goes up the ladder. A helper is cut loose only at the top, once the lead has done everything right:**
1. **Fix the setup first (the lead).** Before judging the helper: was the brief clear, the role file and method current, the asks batched,
   the tools there, the notes file in use? Change whatever the lead's setup made likely (LEAD-PLAYBOOK 8d). Written in the helper's notes.
2. **Coach (a clear talk).** Tell the helper plainly: what the bar is, where it fell short (with the proof), what good looks like, and
   that this is a formal step. It restates the role and the gap in its own words; the lead corrects a wrong restatement.
3. **Time to adjust.** It keeps working for at least one more full job (or one working day) with the fixed setup. Its notes record the
   coaching so memory compression doesn't erase it.
4. **Final warning.** If the same gap shows again with the setup fixed, the lead says so in writing: one more and you're retired.
5. **Retire.** Only if the gap shows again after all of the above. The lead writes in HELPERS.md what was fixed, what was coached, and
   why it still failed, so the call can be checked.

Self-caught, self-reported slips count as points on the scorecard and as a reason for step 1, never as a step toward retirement.
Nobody is retired or rotated for a long memory: every helper keeps notes (reference/notes/README.md) and carries on after compression.
A strong helper is never protected from the ladder; a weak one is never cut before it.

**Judgement, not a counter (Matt, 2026-11-02):** "errors will happen. You make errors. I make errors. It's about having it own the errors
and stop making the same errors... be sure that when you retire, it's because of the right reason, because it's just not a fit. Because
getting training up to speed again is harder sometimes than actually working with somebody. It's your job to understand when it's worth
putting the time and effort into somebody that you believe in versus knowing that no matter how much time and effort you put into it, it
won't ever work right." So: a new error that the helper owns, fixes and learns from is normal work, even after a warning. A warning is
about the SAME error coming back, or errors hidden or shrugged off. Good work after a warning counts. Before retiring, the lead weighs
what the helper knows (its notes, its track record, the cost of training a new one) against the evidence that it won't ever work right,
and writes that judgement in HELPERS.md.

## 4. Onboarding: every helper states the contract before it works
The laws and lessons print automatically at session start. Every brief now opens with:
"Before any work, reply with: (1) your job in one line, (2) the method file you'll follow, (3) the three lessons most likely to bite
this job, (4) what gets you sent back and what gets you retired." The lead reads it. A wrong or vague restatement is corrected
before a single token goes into the job.
Honest note: an AI saying "I understand" isn't proof. The scorecard is the proof. The restatement is cheap, though, and it catches
a misread brief in the first minute instead of after an hour.
