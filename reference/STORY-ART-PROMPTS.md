# Gemini prompts for the story rewrite

New pictures the rewritten story asks for. Every other scene reuses art the game already has. For each prompt, attach one existing story picture as the style reference (the village plate or a Pokey story sheet works well), so the new art matches.

Sheets of poses go on a flat magenta background (#FF00FF) so they can be cut out. Full scenes are wide (16:9) with no characters in them unless the prompt says so, because the characters are drawn on top in the game.

---

## Prompt 18: the squirrel family and their sign (World 1, and the vine memory in World 2)

> In the same soft painted storybook style as the attached picture, draw a sheet of cutout characters on a flat solid magenta background (#FF00FF), each one separate with space around it, no shadows on the background. A family of rainforest squirrels: a chubby, cheeky father squirrel with a big fluffy tail, and four small squirrel kids. Poses: the father cupping his paws around his mouth and shouting up at someone, the father laughing with his belly shaking, the father hammering a nail into a small wooden sign, and the four kids in a row laughing and pointing up. Also draw, separately, the hand-painted wooden sign they nailed to a branch. It says THE SLOTH in wobbly white letters, with an arrow pointing up. Friendly and teasing, not mean. No text other than the sign.

## Prompt 19: the village building the raft (World 2, halfway)

> In the same soft painted storybook style as the attached picture, a wide 16:9 scene with no characters in it: the muddy bank of a slow brown river beside a sloth village dug into the bank, round doors with little PLEASE BE PATIENT signs. On the bank, a raft that is barely started: two logs lashed together with vine rope, a pile of logs waiting beside it, a coil of vine rope, a stone hammer resting on a stump, and a few wood shavings. Warm afternoon light. Leave the lower third fairly open so characters can stand there. No text except the tiny door signs.

## Prompt 20: Dot holding on over the gorge (World 4, the start of Act II)

> In the same soft painted storybook style as the attached picture, a wide 16:9 illustration. Pokey, the orange-and-cream sloth with a brown satchel, hangs by one arm from the very end of a long vine over a deep misty gorge, the broken edge of a cliff ledge above him. A tiny emerald-green hummingbird, Dot, has hold of one of his claws in her feet and is pulling upward with her wings beating so hard they blur. She is far too small to lift him and she is trying anyway. Pokey looks down into the mist, scared but calm. Long white mist below, falling rocks in the distance. Emotional, not funny.

## Prompt 21: Dot covered in flour (the ending)

> Using the attached Dot hummingbird sheet as the character reference, redraw Dot completely dusted white with flour from beak to tail, little puffs of flour coming off her, on a flat solid magenta background (#FF00FF), each pose separate with space around it. Poses: hovering, cheering with wings up, puzzled with her head tilted, and sad with her wings drooping. Same size and shape as the reference, only floury.

## Prompt 22: Pokey's parents going out into the storm (World 6 memory, World 10 confession)

> In the same soft painted storybook style as the attached picture, a wide 16:9 illustration at night in a rainforest storm. Seen from behind, two grown sloths, a mother and a father, are going out along a long far branch into the wind and rain, holding on, heads down. In the foreground, a small round nest woven into a fork of branches, and over its rim just the two small hands of a sloth cub holding on tight. We never see the cub's face or the parents' faces. Lightning far away, heavy rain, deep blues and greens, one warm glow from the nest. Sad and quiet, not frightening.

## Prompts 23-25: the sequel tag after the credits (Matt, 2026-10-30: A + B)

## Prompt 23: the storm comes back (night)

> In the same soft painted storybook style as the attached picture, a wide 16:9 illustration at night. A storm is rolling in from the east over the rainforest canopy: heavy dark clouds lit from inside by distant lightning, rain starting to slant. In the foreground, high on a mossy branch, Pokey the orange-and-cream sloth sleeps curled up and peaceful, a very long knitted rust-and-cream scarf wound around his neck four times with its end trailing far down off the branch and swaying in the wind. He has no idea the storm is coming. Calm in the foreground, menace in the distance.

## Prompt 24: the empty branch (morning)

> In the same soft painted storybook style as the attached picture, a wide 16:9 illustration the morning after a storm, washed pale light, leaves still dripping. The same mossy branch from the night before, now empty. Growing from the branch where the sloth slept is a single long black thorn that does not belong to the rainforest, glossy and sharp, with a faint cold blue sheen. Caught and hanging from the black thorn, swaying, is a very long knitted rust-and-cream scarf. No characters. Quiet, wrong, a little eerie, still gentle enough for children.

## Prompt 25: Meeca sets off (cutout sheet)

> Using the attached Meeca and Dot sheets as the character references, draw on a flat solid magenta background (#FF00FF), each pose separate with space around it: Meeca the young sloth (outfit exactly as in the reference: pink racing vest with 07 and headband) holding the very long knitted rust-and-cream scarf to her chest with both arms, eyes looking up and to the side, scared but deciding; the same Meeca with the scarf now wound around her own neck, standing tall with a determined small smile, one paw gripping her apron strap; and Dot the tiny emerald-green hummingbird hovering beside her, chest puffed out, ready to fly. Same proportions and colours as the references.
