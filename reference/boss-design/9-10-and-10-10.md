## 0. The one idea behind each fight

- **9-10, the Magma Drake: be patient with the fire.** The Monarch's warning ("He is bad at visitors. Be patient with the fire.") is
  the whole fight. His fire is never just the thing that hurts you. It is the way to him. In phase 1 it **lifts** you, in phase 2 it
  **builds your bridge**, and in phase 3 it **runs out**, and all you have to do is still be there when it does. Rushing his fire is
  what burns you, every time. He ends the way his story asks: not with a final punch, but with a visitor setting a cup down.
- **10-10, General Iron-Claw: he counts, and he holds on to everything.** Phase 1 is his counting (his steps are the attack *and*
  the warning). Phase 2 is his tough-guy shell (the council's table up on end, the council's chairs thrown at you, which become
  your stairs). Phase 3 comes straight after the argument he can't win ("It was a storm." / "The General does not lose twice."): he
  smashes out into the storm, and the fight becomes about **holding on**. The finale then asks you to **let go** with that same
  thumb, with no cut in between. The fight teaches you to hold on and the story asks you to let go. Only this game can do that.

Neither third phase is a creeping strip (LV-17). Each phase brings one new idea, and every idea comes from the story, not from the
shared template.

## 1. Rules this design is held to

- **The opening rule** (HANDOFF "Bosses", `bossFair.test.ts`): every opening has a clear tell and stays open at least as long as
  **reach + 0.8 s react + 0.6 s hit**, with reach measured from where the player is standing. Where reach can vary a lot, the opening is
  sized from reach the way the Sky Sovereign's are (`sovereignReach.ts`: `max(base, reach + 1.4 s)`).
- **Every hurt has a tell of at least 0.6 s** (most here are 0.9-1.0 s on Normal and 1.3-1.6 s on Easy). His body never hurts by touch.
  He never hurts you while he is open. Something happens at least every 6 s (`IDLE_MAX`). At least 90% of wind-ups start on screen.
- **Worlds 9-10 can be hard** (HANDOFF: difficulty climbs from World 5; nothing has to be beaten first try). Still never unfair.
- **The first time of every mechanic is taught**, and the first strike of a new hazard is harmless (LV-14).
- **Painted art for anything that hurts** (STAGE-RULES §4): lava, crust, flame, thorns. That is the art list in §6.
- **Phone:** touch only, 30 fps, both orientations designed in from the start. "Upright" is 390x844, "sideways" 844x390.
- **Story:** every line already in `storyBosses.ts` stays word for word. It is only moved to a mid-fight break, and §4 and §5 say
  where. Every new line is marked **(new, needs Matt's approval)**.
- **Dot:** she is with Pokey in World 9 and missing in World 10 ("Where's Dot?"). So the 9-10 tips are hers, and the 10-10 tips are
  Pokey's own thoughts, as in World 7. Tips are 8 words or fewer and only at a first-time mechanic.

## 2. What we took from the best platformer bosses

- **Rayman Legends:** the music levels (Castle Rock and the rest), where you can hear the beat the obstacles land on. **10-10
  phase 1** puts that rhythm in a voice: the General counts out loud, and the number tells you what is coming.
- **Ori and the Blind Forest:** the Ginso Tree escape, a climb with something rising behind you, where moving is the fight. **9-10
  phase 3** is our version (with 6-8's fair rising-water rules), so the hardest phase is about nerve, not hit points.
- **Celeste:** the confrontation with Badeline ends in acceptance, not a knockout, and that is what lands. **9-10's end** (a cup, not a
  punch) and **10-10's hand-off** (hold on, then let go) aim for that.
- **Oddmar** (and good phone platformers generally): big, slow, readable wind-ups; timing sized for a thumb; the arena does the
  talking, not a HUD. Every tell below is a body pose plus one painted change in the arena (a glowing pool, a raised chair, a flash),
  never a code-drawn arrow.
- **What only this game has:** the guardians are people with a want (the Drake wants tea and company; the General wants never to lose
  anyone again). Each fight's mechanic *is* the want. The Drake's fire is the thing he can't manage around visitors. The General's
  counting and holding on are the grief he can't put down.

**Two dependencies to flag:**
1. 9-5 Vent Riding must be on the shipped roll before 9-10 for phase 1's lesson to be "taught". The engine already does the lift, so it
   only has to be met once in a stage. If it isn't, phase 1's first breath teaches it (a harmless first flare and Dot's tip).
2. The "hold on" verb is not a stage mechanic yet. The finale already plays it (`storyFinale.ts`, the `hold` beat: both thumbs down,
   then lift one), but no stage teaches it. 10-3 and 10-4 must teach it when World 10 is rebuilt. Until then, phase 3's first thunder is
   a harmless teaching gust (§5).

What "hold" means in play (a proposal, to settle when 10-3 is built): on a climbable (vine, netting, wall ring), keeping any thumb down
keeps the grip in a gust, and an empty screen in a gust peels him off. On the ground, a thumb held still digs him in (he crouches, the
duck pose) and he slides slowly instead of being blown. Same gesture as the finale's "Hold on with both thumbs" (one thumb is enough in
play).

## 4. 9-10 — the Magma Drake: "Be patient with the fire"

### The arena: his hoard cave
The painted lair as it is (`lair-drake-calm` / `-rage`: a lava river, black basalt spires, heaps of coins, rusty mugs and teapots).
**He has not tidied** ("I did not tidy"): the floor is his hoard, cups everywhere, a teapot on its side. The play space:

- **Phase 1 (bank, pools, island):** Pokey's rock bank on the left; three round lava pools set in the floor between low rock shelves;
  the Drake's hoard island on the right. About 1,300 px wide. The pools are the vents: calm until he breathes into one.
- **Phase 2 (the river):** his thrashing sinks the shelves between the pools, and what is left is a lava river (about 450-700 px
  wide) between Pokey's bank and his island.
- **Phase 3 (the hoard stack):** the volcano behind rumbles, the lava rises, and the fight goes **up**: a zig-zag of basalt shelves
  heaped with his hoard (coins, mugs, teapots), each held by its own rock (STAGE-RULES: nothing floats), up to a top shelf where he
  keeps his one good tea set. He clings to the cave wall beside the climb.

### Phase 1 (100-66%): Heat (the fire lifts you)
**The new idea:** his breath is the ladder. He breathes **down** into the pool nearest Pokey. The pool flares (that hurts), then the
flare dies into a column of hot shimmer that lifts the leaf exactly like a World 9 vent (`ventLift`, the same numbers). His head is
up high and only reachable by riding his own heat.

| Attack | Tell | What it does | Way out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Breath down** (the main one) | 1.0 s: he rears, his spines glow (the painted `roar` frames, already glowing yellow), his head turns down to the pool nearest you, and that pool starts to bubble | the pool flares for 0.6 s (hurts in the pool and 40 px each side), then turns to rising shimmer for the whole opening | step off the pool's lip while it bubbles. **Opening:** he is out of breath ("huff... huff...", smoke from his nose, head drooping at shelf height + 300 px); ride the shimmer up with the leaf, glide to his head, hit it |
| **Tail sweep** | 0.8 s: the tail lifts and curls (`tail` clip) | sweeps his island and the shelf beside it at shin height | jump it. Never during an opening |
| **Hoard shower** (bad at visitors) | 0.9 s: he shifts nervously on his pile (`stomp`); glints show on the floor where the mugs and coins will land (3 spots, 4 on Normal later) | mugs and coins fall in arcs and clatter | stand between the glints. Funny, never long |

- **Opening size:** reach ≈ step to the column 0.5 s + rise about 300 px at 240 px/s 1.25 s + glide about 250 px 0.9 s, so about
  2.7 s; plus 1.4 s gives **at least 4.1 s**. Normal opens for `max(4.4 s, reach + 1.4)`, Easy for `max(6.0 s, reach + 1.4)`. The
  shimmer lasts as long as the opening. He always breathes into the pool between himself and Pokey, so reach stays short.
- **Hits:** a full combo on the head per opening. Normal needs about three rides, Easy two (the existing hit counts, spread
  evenly over the three phases).
- **Taught:** the first breath down is **at a pool Pokey is not in**, and he doesn't attack again until Pokey has seen the shimmer (or
  6 s pass). Dot's line on that first breath: **"Wait for the shimmer. Then open your leaf."** (new, needs Matt's approval; 8 words).

### Phase 2 (66-33%): Crust (the fire builds your bridge)
**The new idea:** his breath across the lava leaves a skin of rock. Fresh crust is red (it hurts), it darkens through orange to
black in about a second, black holds you, and later it cracks. The impatient player steps on red. The patient one walks out on black.

| Attack | Tell | What it does | Way out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **River sweep** (the main one) | 0.9 s: he swings his head along the river, spines glow, a breath drawn in (`breath` clip) | a long gout of flame across the river at chest height (the existing fire beam, 700 px), then a crust laid where it passed, from his island to Pokey's bank | duck it, or jump it (World 9 knows both). **Then wait for black** (1.2 s Normal, 0.8 s Easy) and walk out. **Opening:** out of breath again, head low at his island's edge, about 120 px up: a ground combo |
| **Leap crush** | 0.9 s: he crouches, tail stiff (`leap` prep, already painted) | jumps to Pokey's side of the river; lava waves both ways from where he lands (the existing `leapCrush`) | jump the waves; he jumps back on the next breath |
| **Tail sweep** | as phase 1 | | |

- **Opening size:** reach = cooling 1.2 s + walk about 420 px at 285 px/s 1.5 s + step and hit 0.4 s, about 3.1 s; plus 1.4 s gives
  **at least 4.5 s**. Normal `max(4.8, reach + 1.4)`, Easy `max(6.5, reach + 1.4)`. The crust holds until the opening ends + 1.5 s
  (time to walk back), then cracks from his end with a visible crack line (0.8 s tell). On Easy it never cracks while Pokey stands on it.
- **Falling in:** stepping on red is one heart and a hop back to the bank. Falling through cracked crust is one heart and the bank.
  Never instant game over.
- **Taught:** the first sweep of phase 2 is **aimed at the river, not at Pokey** (a harmless first strike), so the first crust is
  watched cooling from safety. Dot: **"Red is hot. Wait for black."** (new, needs Matt's approval; 6 words).

### Phase 3 (33-0%): Embers (the fire runs out)
**The new idea:** there is no glowing spot this phase. **His fire is going out**, and the fight is to still be there when it does.
The volcano rumbles, the lava starts to rise, and he scrambles up the cave wall beside his hoard stack. Pokey climbs the stack. Each
breath he throws at a row is **shorter and paler** than the last (orange, then yellow, then a grey puff), and after the last one he
slides down onto the top shelf and sits.

| Attack | Tell | What it does | Way out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Row breath** | 1.0 s Normal, 1.4 s Easy: his head turns to one shelf row; that row's rock glows warm | fire along that row, shorter each time | be on another row, or behind a basalt rib on that row (a painted rock that shields) |
| **Ember spit** (Normal only, late) | 0.9 s: cheeks glow, a cough | three embers arc onto the rows; glints show where | step aside |
| **The rising lava** | always visible, a glow on the shelves below | creeps only when Pokey stops; it never sits closer than one row (two on Easy), as in 6-8 (`risingTide.ts`) | keep climbing. A catch ledge under every hop (6-8's rule) |

- **Length:** about 4 breaths Normal, 3 Easy, so 35-50 s of climbing. No minimum fight length for bosses (HANDOFF), but this phase
  must not drag: the climb is about 6 rows, and a breath comes every 7-8 s.
- **Fairness here** is survival fairness, not openings: every row breath warned 1.0 s+, at least one safe row always open, lava never
  closer than one row. `bossFair.test.ts` needs a small extension: a phase with no openings, judged on hurts per minute and its tells.

### The end: "Bring tea. Two cups." (played)
His fire goes out. He slides onto the top shelf where his tea set is, grey and quiet, and sits. Pokey walks up. Then **the one
interaction of the fight that isn't a hit:** a prompt, **"Tap to set the cup down."** Pokey picks a mug off the hoard (the carry pose)
and sets it in front of him. Then the defeat scene plays word for word: "My fire. A hundred years I kept that going, and the first
visitor puts it out." ... "Bring tea. Two cups." Now that line follows something the player did.

| 66% (a 4 s break: he stops, confused, the shelves start to sink) | Drake: **"Where was I. The burning. Then a part about the moon."** Pokey: **"Take your time."** (both new, need Matt's approval; Pokey being literally patient is the fight's idea in two words) |

### Upright and sideways (9-10)
- **Phase 1** is a tall moment, best upright: the camera frames the pool, the column and his head together (head about 300 px up;
  the upright view shows roughly 700 world px of height at Pokey's minimum scale). Sideways, the camera eases back about 10% while
  Pokey rides, so the head never leaves the top of the screen.
- **Phase 2** is wide, best sideways. Upright, the river is the narrower one (450-550 px) and the camera keeps the crust's cool end
  and his head both in view (keyBox framing, as in the puzzle rooms).
- **Phase 3** is a climb, natural upright. Sideways, the stack is two columns of shelves wide, the camera follows up, and the row
  glow (the tell) is always on screen because it is on Pokey's row or the next one.
- The end beat and the prompt are centred on Pokey, so both orientations work.

## 5. 10-10 — General Iron-Claw: "Still slow, boy"

### The arena: the council hall, then the courtyard in the storm
**A conflict to settle first.** HANDOFF's World 5 notes already give "the old council's long table (ten chairs and Iron-Claw's head
chair)" to **5-10**: the Queen's garden fight plays in front of it (`w5arena-table.webp`) and the tea scene reuses it. The brief puts
the same table in 10-10. My proposal keeps both: **the Queen's lace table in her garden is where the council took tea; the original
council table, bare oak, stands in the great hall of the General's fortress**, where they governed (the photo book's "The Original
Council. Ten of them around one table."). He has kept the hall as it was. Ten chairs, nine of them the guardians' he made kneel, one
Thornbark's, plus his head chair. (Question for Matt in §8.)

- **Phases 1-2: the council hall.** A long stone hall (the war room plate's vaulted stone, banners with the paw crest, rain on tall
  windows, one hanging lantern). The long table runs most of the arena (about 1,200 px), walkable on top, about 140 px high (a hop up).
  Chairs stand along the near side (step-ups: the seat about 60 px, the back about 130 px) and the tall head chair is at the far end.
  Each chair back is carved with its guardian's sign: fang, crown, eye, feather, web, reed, stone, moth, flame, thorn.
- **Phase 3: the courtyard.** He breaks out through the hall's end wall into the storm courtyard (the painted `lair-general-rage`:
  iron netting between stone pillars, the tower with Meeca's lit window, banners, rain). The netting panels are climbable. A thorn
  hedge (the Pact's iron thorns) lines each end. This is where the finale already starts ("The General lay in the rubble in the middle
  of the courtyard").

### Phase 1 (100-66%): Eleven Steps (the count is the warning)
**The new idea:** he paces the council table counting out loud, **eleven steps there, eleven back** (exactly how he paced outside
Meeca's door: "Eleven steps there, eleven back. I counted them."). The number is drawn big over him, the way the game draws "DAZED!",
and it tells you what is coming:

| Count | What happens | Tell | Way out |
|---|---|---|---|
| any white number | a step: a shock runs along the floor 330 px ahead (the existing `floorWave`, rock look) | the number appears as his foot lifts, 0.6 s before it lands | be up on the table or a chair, or hop it |
| **a red number** | the club goes up. On the next number it comes down **on the table top** (`staffSlam`), bouncing anything on it | the red number + the club raised: 1.0 s before the slam | get off the table (a chair or the floor) before the next number |
| **"ELEVEN."** | he halts by a table end, stamps, and does a slow, stiff about-face (an old soldier's parade turn) | the count itself: you can see eleven coming from "seven" on | **Opening:** his head is bare and glows for the whole turn |

- On **Easy** the club is always on "FIVE" (red), so it lands on six, and the next number shows faintly ahead. On **Normal** the red
  number moves each lap ("THREE." one lap, "EIGHT." the next), so you have to read it, not learn it.
- **Opening size:** reach from the table top near that end is about 400 px max, 1.4 s + a jump 0.4 s, so about 1.8 s; plus 1.4 s gives
  **at least 3.2 s**. Normal `max(3.5, reach + 1.4)`, Easy `max(5.0, reach + 1.4)`. You always know which end he will stop at.
- **Taught:** Pokey's own thought as the count starts: **"He counts. So can I."** (new, needs Matt's approval; 5 words). The first lap's
  shocks are small (a harmless first strike).

### Phase 2 (66-33%): The Shell (the council lifts you up)
**The new idea:** the tough-guy shell, made literal. After "Not again." he **flips the council table up on its end** in front of him
as a wall. Fists bounce off it ("TOO TOUGH!" is already in the game). From behind it he **throws the council chairs** over the top.
**Every chair he throws lands and stays, and they are Pokey's stairs.** Stack them (carry one, set it down on another) to get high
enough to hit him when he peeks over to count you.

| Attack | Tell | What it does | Way out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chair lob** | 0.9 s: a chair rises over his head behind the shield; a red mark on the floor shows where it will land (Normal: two at a time later) | the chair falls and hurts on landing, then stays as a prop (at most 4 on the floor; older ones splinter away with a crack, off screen if possible) | stand off the mark. Then use the chair |
| **Club through the gap** | 0.65 s: the club's head shows at the shield's foot (`spearThrow`, low) | a club skids along the floor | jump it (Normal later adds a high one: duck) |
| **The peek** (the opening) | 0.8 s: his claws grip the top edge of the table, then his head rises over it | he counts you: **"...One sloth."** (new, needs Matt's approval). His head glows | **Opening:** hit the head from the top of the chair stack |

- **The heights:** the peek's head box is 270-330 px above the floor. The floor plus a double jump plus a jab reaches about 240, so
  **the chairs are needed** (STAGE-RULES §5b: every move it teaches is needed). One chair (about 56 px) plus a double jump reaches the
  bottom of it; two stacked reach it with a single jump.
- **Easy:** his own head chair stands by the shield all phase (he won't throw that one), so one step is always there. **Normal:** two
  chairs stacked (carry, set down on top: the carry rules hold, no jump while carrying, and the walk to the shield is flat).
- **Opening size:** he peeks every second volley. From the stack it is about 0.5 s to the head, so 3.0 s would be enough, but reach
  is measured from wherever Pokey is, so the peek is `max(3.0, reach + 1.4)`, capped by the next volley. A player with no stack built
  gets a long peek they can't reach. That is fine: it is fair, and it shows the target.
- **A small thing for adults:** he throws nine chairs. **Thornbark's chair (the thorn on its back) he never throws.** It was the one
  that said no. Nobody mentions it.
- **Taught:** the first lob lands **away** from Pokey, upright, right by the shield. Pokey's thought: **"His chairs. My stairs."** (new,
  needs Matt's approval; 4 words).

### Phase 3 (33-0%): The Storm (hold on)
Straight after the argument he can't win (story beats below), he charges **through the hall's end wall** and out into the storm
courtyard. The shell slams shut. This is the hardest phase, and its idea is the one the whole game has been about: **hold on when it
thunders.**

| Attack | Tell | What it does | Way out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Thunder gust** | the lightning **flash** first, then the count to the thunder (drawn on screen: "1... 2..."); the gust comes on the roll: 1.0 s after the flash Normal, 1.6 s Easy | a 1.2 s gust across the courtyard toward the thorn hedge | **hold**: on the netting, keep a thumb down. On the ground, hold still and dig in (slide slowly) |
| **Iron charge** | 0.9 s: he paws the stones and drops his shoulder (`charge` clip) | runs the length of the courtyard (the existing `ironCharge`) and **crashes into the netting** | climb the netting out of the charge line. The netting shakes when he hits it: **hold on** |
| **Club slam** (close, not on Easy at the same time as a gust) | 0.8 s: club raised | as phase 1's slam, waves both ways | jump |

- **The opening:** after the crash he is **DAZED!** at the foot of the netting, right under Pokey. Drop on him (a stomp) or drop and
  punch the glowing head. Reach from the netting is about 0.6 s, so `max(3.0, reach + 1.4)` Normal and `max(4.5, reach + 1.4)` Easy. (The
  existing `chargeMove` stun is 1.8 s; it grows to this.)
- **Normal's combo:** a charge can follow a gust closely (hold, then climb). Easy never puts a charge inside a gust.
- **The last daze:** the third crash cracks the battlement; the last hit drops him into the rubble in the middle of the courtyard as
  the thunder breaks.
- **Taught:** the first gust of the phase is harmless (it pushes, it doesn't reach the hedge). Pokey's thought: **"Thunder. Hold on."**
  (new, needs Matt's approval; 3 words). If 10-3 and 10-4 already teach the hold, this is a reminder.

### The end: the hand-off (played, no cut)
The General goes down in the rubble, the thunder keeps rolling, and **Pokey's thumbs are already holding on** to the netting or the
wall. The finale's **THE TOWER** starts right here, in the same place and the same rain: "The General lay in the rubble..." / "Pokey
had hold of the nearest wall with both hands." / **"Hold on with both thumbs."** / **"Now lift one."** The thumb the fight trained to
hold is the one the story asks to let go. No loading card or fade between them, and the defeat scene's four lines play after the song,
or fold into the finale where the story team decides. (Today the defeat lines come first. Moving them is a story call, §8.)

### Story beats mid-fight (10-10)
