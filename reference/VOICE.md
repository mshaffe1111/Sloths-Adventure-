# How the story sounds

This page is the rulebook for every line of dialogue and narration in the game. It came out of rewriting the prologue with Matt, line by line, and it's meant to be read before touching any story text. The Cast Bible says who everyone is and what happened to them. This page says how they talk, and why.

The story gets rewritten one world at a time. Matt reads each world before the next one starts.

## The one question

Before any line goes in, ask: why is this being said, by this character, right now?

If the answer is "to be funny," "to explain the plot," or "to end the scene neatly," the line isn't finished yet. People talk because they want something from the person in front of them, or because something is pressing on them and it comes out. Dot asks Pokey if he's been eating because she's been gone and she worries about him. Pokey says "It's only weather" because he's scared and won't say so. The General says "That one" because he doesn't see Meeca as a person yet, just the keeper.

## What we learned rewriting the prologue

**Let people talk the way people talk.** Real speech is loose. People repeat themselves ("I'm looking. I'm looking."), start over, trail off, and add words that don't carry information but carry the person ("Well. It seems to me..."). Earlier drafts kept trimming lines down to the shortest version, and that's exactly what made them sound written by a machine. "Of course I did. He trained for it." is better than "He trained for it." because the first three words are Pokey. Don't cut a line for word count. Cut it only if the character wouldn't say it.

**Every joke needs its setup in the scene.** Pokey's "it's still morning" only works because Dot just said "first thing in the morning." If the setup isn't there, the joke isn't a joke, it's a non sequitur. Before writing a punchline, find the line it's answering.

**Characters stay inside what's happening.** After Meeca is taken, nobody thinks about snacks, or eating, or anything small. The whole story shifts: Pokey's only thought is that he has to get her back. A joke during tension is only allowed when it's a character choosing to break it (Pokey asking the General if he's feeling okay), never the writer reaching for a laugh.

**Check where everybody is.** Dot is already on the branch, so she can't say she'll be early. She says she'll go get snacks. Before writing a line, picture the scene: who's standing where, what just happened, what each of them can see.

**Plain American speech.** No "post" (it's mail, or letters). No "I'd like it noted," "on the record," "Wonderful" as a reaction, or anything else that sounds British or like a courtroom.

**Nothing that reads wrong for the relationship.** No "honey," or any pet name that reads romantic. Dot is family to these two, not a sweetheart.

**Capitals only when someone would really shout.** Meeca's "LOOK" and Dot's "you KNOW I" earn it. Most scenes shouldn't have any. The old script had about sixty capitalized words; that's too many.

**Narration doesn't report what the picture shows.** It does what the camera can't: "Pokey's branch was usually last." It stays plain, concrete and in the past tense. No morals, no "little did he know," no stock similes. At most one dry aside in a scene.

**Habits to break.** The old script leaned on the same moves over and over, and they're what make it feel machine-written: jokes built on counting numbers, every voice sharing the same rhythm, every scene ending on a tidy button line, "very" (26 times) and "nobody" (12 times). If you could swap two characters' lines and nothing would change, rewrite them.

## The people

### Pokey

He's slow, and he's fine with it. In the prologue he's carefree, easygoing, a little literal, with a dry sense of humor that never hurries. He teases Dot gently ("Well, you're really late then.") and doesn't rise to anything. The jokes come out flat and short, aimed at himself or the situation, one beat and then he moves on.

Why he's like that: he lost his parents in a storm when he was a cub, and nobody ever told him how. A funny, easy kid gets left alone. A sad one gets questions he can't answer. So the jokes are how he keeps things light, and he's been doing it so long he doesn't know he's doing it.

When something real happens, the joke doesn't come. That silence is how the player knows. When he's scared he holds on to things with both hands (the branch, the nest, the satchel strap). He never says "I'm afraid of thunder." He says "You know how I feel about thunder," and "It's only weather."

After the kidnapping, he isn't carefree anymore. He thinks it's his fault, because he was too slow, and the General said so out loud. Everything he does in the game comes from "I have to save her." He keeps his manners, though; politeness is how he faces people even when they're scary ("Excuse me, sir?").

### Meeca

The keeper of the Heartbloom, and the one who holds everybody steady. She's warm and quick, a little bossy in the way a good host is bossy ("Everybody sit down. Sit, sit, I'll go get the tea"), and she treats Dot's fussing the way a granddaughter treats a worried aunt. "If I've told you once, I've told you twice" is her mixing up a saying, on purpose, and it stays that way.

Why she's like that: Grandma and Grandpa raised her, in the house with the garden. Her parents are never mentioned. Grandma died in the storm. Grandpa came home from the funeral and sat by Grandma's chair for a year, there but not there, while Meeca did everything she could to keep the house gentle and bring him back. Then one morning the door was open and he was gone. Meeca believes he walked out on her. She doesn't know he put on the war look and became the General. So she lost the woman who raised her, then the man who raised her left anyway, and she was alone with the garden and a very quiet house. She decided somebody had to keep everything gentle so nobody else would leave. She looks after everyone's feelings, and nobody looks after hers.

In the tower she starts working it out on her own, before Pokey ever finds the photograph. The soup she liked as a cub keeps showing up, made wrong. Someone stands outside her door every night, eleven steps there and eleven back, and never knocks. A blanket arrives when she's cold. She pays attention to everything, so by the end "Hello, Grandpa" isn't a surprise to her. It's something she's been holding. Her one angry scene is at him, for leaving her, and only after that does she take his hand.

She is never a damsel. Faced with an army she's indignant, not scared, at least not on the outside: "You're standing in my garden. And you're blocking my flower." When she's taken, she doesn't scream for rescue so much as give Pokey a job: "Follow the petals!"

### Dot

Dot is she. She's a hummingbird courier with the fastest wings in the forest and no sense of direction, and she's always late. She's the nurturer of the group, almost a second mother to Pokey. She's the one who notices he looks thin and asks if he's eaten. "Have you been eating?" is Dot's line, and it belongs to calm moments; once Meeca is gone, nobody thinks about food.

How she talks: long, breathless sentences that run on and interrupt themselves, explanations nobody asked for (the headwind, and then another headwind, a completely different one), getting louder as she gets more worked up. Then she stops dead when something actually matters. "That's not weather." When Dot goes quiet and short, it's serious, so that has to stay rare.

Why she's like that: she's terrified of being late, and the player won't know why for a long time. The night Pokey's parents were lost, her warning came too late. She's never told him. She's looked after him ever since. In the prologue, when the soldiers come, she's the one who keeps her head and goes after them, because she's the fast one, and she promises to come straight back. Her wing goes to the pouch at her side when she's frightened.

### General Iron-Claw

Short, formal, heavy. He gives orders, not explanations. "That one. She's the keeper. Bring her to me." He counts out loud ("One. Two. Three.") because counting is how he makes a loss feel like a plan. He calls Meeca "little bloom," the family's name for her, and it lands on Meeca before she knows why.

The guardians know exactly who he is. Before he was anybody's General he was Iron-Claw, the head of their council and their friend, and they said yes to his Pact because they couldn't tell a grieving man no. What they keep from Pokey is who he is to Meeca, and the collar holds their tongues. A boss can let something slip that only makes sense later ("You're going to take her from him?"), then catch itself.

He's cruel to Pokey in the prologue ("He's too slow. He was always too slow."), and that line has to stick, because Pokey carries it the whole game. He doesn't joke, and he doesn't say sorry, until the very end.

### Grandma

She's gone, so she only appears in memories, her letter, and once as something Pokey may or may not have seen in the rain. Gentle, dry, specific. "Dear." "My love." She teaches with a fern or a kettle, never a speech. Because she has so few lines, every one has to earn its place. In the prologue she says one thing: "Follow the petals, Pokey." Pokey isn't sure it was her.

### Lieutenant Pell

Over-polite, apologizes inside his own sentences, corrects himself mid-order. He's always Lieutenant Pell (the old script sometimes calls him Officer; those get fixed).

### The narrator

Plain, warm, past tense. Says what the camera can't. Never explains a feeling the scene already shows.

## What happened, in order

These are the facts every line has to agree with. It's a tropical rainforest: there's a rainy season and a dry season, and no winter, ever. Anything that says winter, a freeze or snow is wrong.

Years before the game, when Pokey was a cub, his parents went out along the far branches in a storm and never came back. Dot was supposed to fly the warning around the nests that the storm had turned early, and she went down the wrong river and was late. Nobody told Pokey anything except "it was a storm." Later he sat on a vine for two days, and Meeca sat with him for six hours. That's how they met.

Meeca was raised by Grandma and Grandpa in the house with the garden. Grandma kept the Heartbloom for fifty years. Grandpa, Iron-Claw, was the head of the forest's council of ten.

Ten years before the game, a few weeks before the storm, Pokey knocked on their door. Grandma let him in. Grandpa timed him coming up the path, fourteen minutes, and told him "Come back when you can protect her."

Then the storm came, and Grandma died in it. The help came late.

There was a funeral. Grandpa was there, in the photograph with everyone who loved her. Afterward he went up the hill to the old tree where she's buried and stood there a long time, and then he stopped going. For about a year he stayed in the house, by her chair at the window, home but not really there. Meeca kept the house warm and gentle the whole time, trying to bring him back.

Nine years before the game, one morning, the door was standing open and his chair was empty. Meeca believes he left her. What really happened: he went to each of the ten council members at night, crying, with the same bargain (no more waiting, no more late), and they said yes. That's the Iron Pact, and the thorn collars on their necks come from it. He put on the war look and became General Iron-Claw. He's carried the club for nine years.

The Heartbloom hasn't opened for Meeca since Grandma died. The morning the game starts, it begins to open, and the General comes for the keeper.

Things in the script that have to change to match: the Queen's "Then came a winter" in World 5 becomes the storm. Pokey's World 9 memory says "nine years ago, a few weeks before the storm" (it's ten), and has him going back after the storm to find the chair already empty, so it has to be a year later. Grandma's last letter, the one Dot carried and never delivered, gets checked against this when we reach Worlds 5 and 10.

## Boss fights

Matt's direction: the bosses look at Pokey and can't believe this is what showed up to fight them. The snake's attitude is "what are you doing here?" Pokey tells him, flat out, that he's here to save Meeca. The snake says he'll have to get through him first, and he can't picture something this slow beating something this powerful. When the boss loses, it can't believe it either: beaten by a sloth, and not just any sloth, the slowest sloth in the rainforest. Pokey answers with a one-liner that breaks the tension, something like "Wait till the neighbors hear." He doesn't apologize to anything he just knocked down. No "I'm sorry about the knot."

Why it works: the General said "He's too slow. He'll never make it." Every boss who thinks the same thing and loses proves the General wrong one more time, and the player carries that into the last fight.

So it isn't the same scene ten times, it changes as the game goes. Word spreads ahead of Pokey. The early bosses laugh because they've never heard of him. By the middle they've heard rumors and don't believe them (the Marsh Reaver's "The sloth is somehow still coming. I thought you would be taller." is already this). By the end they're nervous before he arrives. Pokey's one-liners grow along with it: in World 1 he's nearly as surprised as the snake that he won, and by World 9 he expected it. Each boss's disbelief comes out in its own voice (the croc's vanity, the Queen's manners), never one shared line.

The guardians keep their soft side. After Pokey wins and says his line, the beaten guardian softens on their own: a clue, the itchy collar, the small thing they wanted. That softness comes from them, not from Pokey being nice to them.

## The prologue, as approved

*Morning came down through the canopy one branch at a time. Pokey's branch was usually last.*

**Dot:** I'm sorry, I'm so sorry I'm late. I have letters. Important letters. I wanted to have them here this morning, first thing, I promised myself first thing in the morning—

**Pokey:** Well. It seems to me that it's still morning.

**Dot:** ...Yesterday morning. I meant yesterday morning.

**Pokey:** Oh. Well, you're really late then.

**Dot:** There was a headwind over the river, I hit it head on, and then there was another one right behind it, a completely different one, I don't even know where it came from, and you know I hate being late, you KNOW I— *(she stops and looks at him properly)* You look thin. Have you lost weight? Have you been eating?

**Meeca:** Dot. If I've told you once, I've told you twice. It's fine. It's just mail. Come here, both of you, come look. LOOK. It's opening!

**Pokey:** I'm looking. I'm looking.

**Meeca:** It's never opened for me. Not once. Not since Grandma. Everybody sit down. Sit, sit, I'll go get the tea, everybody gets tea, nobody's going anywhere—

**Pokey:** How long are we gonna sit here and watch it?

**Meeca:** All day, if it takes all day.

**Pokey:** I'm happy for you. I really am. But sitting here staring at it isn't gonna make it grow any faster.

**Meeca:** You once sat and watched a snail win a race.

**Pokey:** Of course I did. He trained for it.

**Dot:** Well, you can't watch a flower all day on an empty stomach. I'll go get snacks. Don't let it open without me.

*She was already lifting off the branch when the birds went quiet. Every one of them, all at once. Dot stopped in the air. And way off in the distance, up in the Highlands, thunder.*

**Pokey:** Did I just hear thunder? You know how I feel about thunder. *(hands already on the branch, both of them)* It's only weather. It's only weather.

*Meeca looked at his hands. Then, to be kind, she looked at the sky. Dot's wing went to the pouch at her side.*

**Dot:** That's not weather. Inside. Both of you, inside, right now. Meeca, leave the flower. Pokey, I need you to let go of the branch.

*(The flower closes, petal over petal, and its light goes out. Soldiers come in two straight lines, the one in front bigger than the rest.)*

**General:** That one. She's the keeper. Bring her to me.

**Meeca:** Excuse me? You're standing in my garden. And you're blocking my flower.

**General:** Come, little bloom. You're coming with me.

**Meeca:** ...Nobody's called me that since Grandma.

**General:** Grab her. We're leaving.

**Pokey:** Excuse me, sir? I think there's been some kind of misunderstanding here. *(a beat)* Are you feeling okay? You seem a little grumpy.

**General:** One. *(Pokey looked over at Meeca, and he moved faster than he had ever moved in his life.)* Two. *(He threw his arms out toward her, reaching for her hand.)* Three.

**Meeca:** Pokey! Pokey, help me! Follow the petals! Follow the petals, Pokey!

**General:** Him? Save you? He's too slow. He was always too slow. He'll never make it.

**Dot:** Pokey. Pokey, look at me. Somebody has to see where they take her, and I'm the fast one. I'll follow them, I'll find out where they're going, and I'll come straight back for you. Straight back. Do you hear me? Don't you go anywhere without me. *(And she was gone.)*

*Pokey ran. The soldiers weren't even hurrying, and they pulled away from him anyway. He tripped over the old root he said good morning to every day, and this time he didn't say anything to it. When he looked up, they were a line along the ridge. Then they were only rain.*

*He stood there alone in the rain. And for a second, by the closed-up Heartbloom, he could have sworn he saw someone. An old sloth in a gardening apron, looking right at him.*

**Grandma:** Follow the petals, Pokey.

*He blinked the rain out of his eyes. There was nobody there.*

**Pokey:** ...Was that Meeca's grandma?

*On his wrist, the faded petal Meeca had tied there years ago began to warm, and slowly, all by itself, it turned to the north.*

**Pokey:** Hang on, Meeca. I'm coming. I'm slow, but I'm coming.

## Things the prologue changes later on

Dot is she everywhere in the game, not he. The Cast Bible still says he and needs the same fix. It also says Grandpa vanished the same night Grandma died; he left a year later, after the funeral (see What happened, in order).

"Eat something" in World 1 (line 58 in the script doc) moves to Dot, and only where things are calm.

Grandma's ghost in the prologue uses the existing Grandma art, faded.

The World 3 memory about hiding the satchel is cut.

Every boss intro and defeat gets rewritten world by world to the boss-fight direction above. Pokey's apologies come out (the snake's knot, the Prowler's "Sorry. It's the only trick I have."), and the croc's "I give it because you were polite" changes, since Pokey isn't asking politely anymore.

The prologue goes into the game (src/game/storyIntro.ts) once the rewrite is approved as a whole.

## Still open

In the prologue Meeca says "Nobody's called me that since Grandma." If "little bloom" was mostly Grandpa's name for her, that line points away from him, which may be exactly right (it hides the twist), or it may want to be "since Grandpa," which would be a big early clue. Matt to decide.

Meeca's parents are never mentioned. Leave it unspoken, or give it one line?

What does Pokey remember of his parents? The suggestion is that he doesn't remember their faces, just small things, like rain on the nest or someone humming. Not decided.
