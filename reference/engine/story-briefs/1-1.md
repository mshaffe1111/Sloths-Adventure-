## Where Pokey is
The night before he lost Meeca. He let go of the branch "one claw at a time" and said "Hang on, Meeca. I'm slow. But I'm coming." The
petal on his wrist warmed and turned north. This is two days later. He has barely left: at the forest's edge the squirrels have brought
snacks to watch him go ("Are you leaving?" / "I've left." / "I'll write.") and laugh at how close he still is to home. Then he finds the
first petal, pressed flat to a kapok "at about the height of someone being marched past". Dot catches up (the hibiscus story). He is slow,
deadpan and kind, and quietly set on going. He doesn't make speeches.

## What the player should feel
- Start: small and close to home, a little funny. The forest's edge is still homely, and the squirrels' laughter is the joke. Gentle, safe ground.
- First lift: "I can actually do this." The first real step up, off the forest floor.
- The double-jump chain (the stage's moment): Pokey surprising himself. It pays off in 1-2's letter, where Meeca writes "I saw you run. I didn't know
  you could do that." This is the first time the player feels he is quicker than anyone thought.
- End: up in the high branches, on the petal trail, pointed north. Resolve, not triumph. The bigger views belong to later stages.
