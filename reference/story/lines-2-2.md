## Why
1. **pads.** Weight sinks them, leaving brings them back: the whole rule, in a child's words. It says STAND, never "don't stop" or "don't
   be still": stillness turning into the trap is World 5's lesson (the 11-11 ruling), and 2-2 must not spend it.
2. **stone.** World 2's theme is patience (the village of PLEASE BE PATIENT). "Sit tight", not "wait": that word stays out of Pokey's help.
3. **breathe.** The twist named the design's way; "on the up" is the move.
4. **lily / crown.** The pay-off of the hook: the first place in the pond he can be still, and it is Pokey who says it, deadpan, his
   signature move come home. Dot then gives the reason (the king wants only the best).
5. **king.** Crocus Rex "wants admiring" (stagePlan); his crown is the one Meeca made him (her note). The boss is still to come, so the line
   is about his vanity, not a threat.
7. **note.** Stage 2 of every world carries Meeca's note in plain sight (GAP-MAP; finds.ts `w{N}_note`), so 2-2's her-trail beat is the NOTE
   (past the second flag, by the water), not a mark at the exit. Its words are canon (STORY-REWRITE:206-216: "we crossed the river today... the crocodile carried us over... He has a crown
   now... Don't swim... P.S. He's nicer than he looks."). It pays off 2-1 (why he rides, not swims) and this stage's crown.

## Story faults in the design (for the lead)
1. **The crown's petals are NOT her pink petals.** Meeca used up her petals on the king's crown ("there won't be many for a while").
   2-2's goal flowers must be the pond's own and look nothing like her pink trail petals: lavender water hyacinth (native to the Amazon)
   on the pads, and the crown lily's white petals at beat 11. (The same rule as 1-2's bonus blooms.)
2. **Her note must be in the stage** (above). If the find is not laid, line 7 cannot play.
