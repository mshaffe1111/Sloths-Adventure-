# 2-1 Down to the River: the level as designed (DESIGN-19 final, from PICKS-19-22.md: PITCH-B's idea with PITCH-A's code fact; written 7:47 PM ET 11-14). DESIGN ONLY: no build until the World 2 kit, the lead's look, the tribute check (D58) and the engine packet (R-165.256)
**One idea:** "When the trees run out, the river is your road: wait for a log, ride it, and hop to the next before the water gets you." A player says: "the one where you swing down
out of the trees and ride logs to the village." World 1 made him move; World 2 moves HIM. The thumb only decides WHEN (board, hop, step off): a child's one-thumb game, and for the 12+
player, logs read in rhythm and a river that runs backwards. (Our own idea: a sloth, the slowest thing in the forest, gets his first ride.)
**The river, measured from code first** (origin/t-int-9 @ 1c7f6b954). Logs slide out from under one bank, ride the current and slide in under the other; they never stop (river.ts:4-5).
A log carried under a bank HANDS HIM UP onto it (engine.ts:1849-1853), so the reeds never hurt: **the danger is the water between logs** (PITCH-A's fact; the map's "step off before the
reeds" is scenery and a cue, not a hazard, unless an engine packet makes it one for every river). The teaching river costs no life, a splash puts him back on the bank (w2Stages.ts:6-7).
Rivers run with or against him; speeds 70-120 px/s (river.ts:134-147), against his run of 285 (physics.ts:13), so riding is never faster than running: the gift is not speed but being
carried while he rests. A log leaves the bank every (log + gap) / speed s (river.ts:160): 242 px at 70 px/s is 3.5 s, a beat he can count. A river is about 800 px bank to bank
(river.ts:152-153). He rides by position: the log moves his x (engine.ts:1848), so a jump off keeps none of the log's speed, where a ride's does (ride.ts:19, RIDE.keep): **ENGINE-WORTHY**
(rule 22): one rule for everything that carries him, decided before World 2's rides are laid.
**Difficulty:** Normal **target 9.0, PROPOSED** (PICKS-19-22.md, from PITCH-A): the slot formula, pasted: "target(w, s) = 10(w - 1) + T[s], T = [-0.5, 3, ...]: a first stage just under
the last world's end" (DIFFICULTY.md:57-58) gives 9.5, which TIES 1-9's rated 9.5 (:61); 9.0 is World 2's band floor ("World band [10w - 11, 10w + 1]", :56). **Easy ~5.4** (at most 0.6 x 9.0).
Hard because: three logs read at once, deep water from beat 4, a river that runs back, both ways at once. Easy: every river the splash-back kind, slower and wider logs (riverRecipes'
rows by setting), the knock louder. **Length** ~180 s designed; bots 2:00+. **Goal** reach the village (sprint), petals on the way. **Cast** none (nothing hunts him: World 2's
first breath). **Look** the canopy's edge thinning into sky, then the Sunlit Riverbank's painted river, logs and reeds (riverArt.ts), the village far off, its round doors at the end
(w2Stages.ts:10-11). Last of World 1: the Old Boa's splash rings downstream (1-9.md).

## Tributes (the lead checks a/b/c before any build, D58; every quote from a page opened this session)
**Frogger (Konami, arcade 1981; Matt's own favourite, MAP-REVISIONS §7).** a. Players loved its "non-aggressive yet challenging" play (Cashbox, 1981, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frogger):
a game for everyone. b. The road first, then "a river with logs, alligators, and turtles, all moving horizontally across the screen in opposite directions"; a frog is lost "riding a log,
alligator, or turtle off the side of the screen" (same page). c. Our river's edge is the water between logs, our road is the bank, and the first river costs nothing. d. Not copied: traffic,
frogs, homes, alligator jaws (2-5's), and not "the arcade game with the most ways to die" (Softline 1982, same page): we keep one.
**Jungle Hunt (Taito, arcade 1982; Matt's list says 1983).** a. Bill Kunkel (Electronic Games, 1983): "the classic formula for successful videogames: easy to learn, difficult to master", "an
undeniable kick the first few plays" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungle_Hunt). b. Vines first ("pressing the button when two vines are close enough together"), then "After the last vine,
the explorer dives into a crocodile-infested river" (same page): one trip that changes what you do. c. Beat 1: World 1's lianas carry him DOWN into World 2's river. d. Not copied: swimming,
the knife, crocodiles, captives, its right-to-left scroll.
**Bit.Trip Runner (Gaijin Games, Wii 2010).** a. GamesRadar: "all of the levels are meant to be played in rhythm"; "With the avoidance of each significant obstacle, the music becomes more layered
and enthralling" (https://www.gamesradar.com/bittrip-runner-review/). b. Commander Video "runs automatically" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bit.Trip_Runner): you only act, on the beat. c. The
river already keeps a beat (river.ts:160); the knock lets a child board on it, and the long log's knock becomes the village drum. d. Not copied: the auto-run, its music, the one-mistake restart.
**The tribute rule: PASS.** Series: three (Frogger, Jungle Hunt, Bit.Trip). Eras: 1981, 1982, 2010 (post-2000: Bit.Trip Runner). Pre-1990 classic: Frogger, Jungle Hunt; lesser-known: Jungle
Hunt, Bit.Trip Runner; famous few: none. Our own idea: the slowest animal's first ride, World 1's moves carrying him into World 2. What players loved: quoted for each (a).
