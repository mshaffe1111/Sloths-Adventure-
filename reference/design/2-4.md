**One idea:** "Swing out over the water, let go onto a passing log, ride it under the next vine and grab on: cross the lake without touching land." A player says: "the one where you
drop from the vines onto the logs and back up." **The hook** (B): his two ways of moving (1-6's liana, 2-1's log) meet in one moment, and the log turns from a floor into a launcher (the carry
rule, carryRule.test.ts:2). **The teach** (A): two clocks the eye reads at once, the vine's swing and the river's logs, and ONE press when they line up. 2-4 is World 2's breather after 2-3
(slot 3.5 < 4.5): a joy ride of air time, soft water first, the only question WHEN.

Every row, both settings: 100% of swings hold a let-go onto a log. **So the picks' teach rule (pass time >= swing period) is not what lands him: the GAP between logs is.** A let-go's landing
spreads over most of a log spacing, so short fast logs (`fast`, 1.4 s) land as often as long slow ones; only wider water between logs makes him wait for the clocks ("not yet... NOW").
**Up from the log** (same script, UP): standing on a log carried at the row's speed from 420 px before a liana hung over open water to 60 px past it, a held jump catches it on 76-99 frames of

## Tributes (quotes as the pitches cite them, from pages they opened; D58)
- **Bionic Commando** (Capcom 1987/88; Matt's #11). a. Loved: EGM: "having to swing instead of jump added unexpected depth to the platform genre"
  (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bionic_Commando_(1988_video_game)). b. Taught by taking the jump away. c. Ours: a stretch where swings and logs ARE the floor. d. Not copied: the arm, guns, no jump.
- **Gibbon: Beyond the Trees** (Broken Rules 2022; a phone game; A's). a. The Guardian: "short, simple and lovely to play". b. "Releasing the button at the right time" launches across the
  gaps (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibbon:_Beyond_the_Trees). c. Ours: one release per swing, on a phone, onto something that moves. d. Not copied: auto-run, the backflip boost.
- **Grapple Dog** (Medallion 2022). a. Kotaku: its swinging is "tricky but rewarding" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grapple_Dog). b. [S] fixed points first, moving ones later. c. Ours: vine to
  log (beat 1), then log to vine (beat 4), then both, no bank (6). d. Not copied: aiming, the hook.
- **The Jungle Book** (Virgin 1993/94; the warning): GamePro found it "pretty repetitious, centering around his ability to swing on vines" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jungle_Book_(video_game)):
  every beat here changes what the swing lands on (log, log to vine, no bank, a wider river, the raft).
**Different:** 2-1 boards logs from the bank; 2-4 boards from the AIR and leaves UPWARD. No pads (2-2), no line behind him (2-3), no beat (2-5).
**Pushback (design):** the picks' "every log's pass time >= the swing's period" teach rule is replaced by the flown drop share (the ruler fixed, not the data); "shorter faster logs past the
teach" became WIDER GAPS, the only knob that made him wait. **Learned:** fly the press over BOTH clocks' phases, never one log; a sim's per-swing reset cut windows in half (fixed, swingLog.ts).
