# 3-3 Footprints in a Circle: "Off to Bed", the level as designed (DESIGN-33-35 final, from PICKS-33-35.md:6-11: PITCH-33A's input (ring a chime stone) + PITCH-33B's resolution (the sleepy mushrooms send it home) + PITCH-33A's twist (be the bait); written 7:17 PM ET 11-19 by `date`). DESIGN ONLY: no build before its engine (NEW 1-2 below, each proven on a real stageTable row, level.md rule 23)
**One idea:** "Ring the chime stone and the prowler comes to look, right into the sleepy mushrooms: a puff of spores and it yawns off home to bed." A player says: "the one where you ring the
rock and the cats go to sleep." **The hook:** for two stages the prowlers were something to hide from; now Pokey sends them home, and nobody is hurt. A sloth never chases: he picks his
spot, reads the circle (their glowing pawprints in the moss), and lets the cave do it. One thumb: the punch he has had since 1-3 rings the stone (no aiming a throw on a phone). The
payoff (a big yawn, a sleepy pad off into the dark) lands every time. **The teach:** 3-3 teaches NOISE AS A CALL (3-1 taught DARK, 3-2 HANG UNDER; 3-5 turns noise into the danger).

**First appearances, each taught** (rules 6, 7): the call (beat 1: from a shelf it cannot see, on a 100% patch, so the first ring cannot fail); the round and hearing (beat 3: rung too
early it simply does not come: costs seconds); the bait (beat 6: Dot's line before it, "they come to where you land"). Nothing that hurts between a teach and its thing.
**What a miss costs:** a ring it did not hear: nothing. A call the cloud missed: it looks 4.5 s and walks back (a wait, never a heart). Only beat 8's third ocelot (Normal) can hurt: its
round passes the floor; seen moving within its engage, it tells (0.5 s) and strikes (one heart, callsOff). Easy: no third ocelot; ferns everywhere he stands.
**One ring per call** (the rule NEW 1 builds; a code fact, core.ts:344-346): a bell heard again by one already drawn resets its look (`s.look = 0`), so ringing every 4 s would hold it
in any cloud and the timing would vanish. The stone, once rung, hums (its ring frames) and cannot ring again until every animal it called has gone back to its round.

## Tributes (each opened by the pitcher who cites it; D58)
- **Lode Runner** (Broderbund 1983; via PITCH-33A). a. Loved: "the rather dim-witted enemies who by the end are more tools to be taken advantage of than opponents" (John Harris, Game Developer,
  "20 Difficult Games"). b. Taught: a guard walks toward you and the level does the rest [S for the first layout]. c. Ours: the prowler's curiosity is the tool; he places it, never runs.
  d. Not copied: digging, gold, being chased.
- **Pac-Man** (Namco 1980; via PITCH-33B). a. Loved: "the game explained itself when people saw or played it" (Iwatani's postmortem, Game Developer); "In scatter mode, the ghosts give up
  the chase for a few seconds and head for their respective home corners" (The Pac-Man Dossier). b. The hunters keep a rhythm you can read. c. Ours: the circles are the readable rhythm
  (their prints), and the circlers are SENT HOME. d. Not copied: eating them, the maze, the power pill.
- **Oddworld: Abe's Oddysee** (1997; via PITCH-33B). a. Loved: "careful negotiation of a bubbling stew of AI variables" (GamesRadar). b. Sligs "may be led astray by thrown bottle caps"
  (GamesRadar). c. Ours: the unarmed hero who wins with a noise somewhere else and the creatures' own habits. d. Not copied: death, possession, a thrown thing to aim.
- **Undertale** (2015; via PITCH-33B). a. Loved: "The important part was in choosing not to hurt" (Joel Couture, Game Developer). c. Ours: "drive off" means "send home to bed".
**Different:** 3-1 he makes light by keeping still; 3-2 he hides from what the light shows; 3-3 he ACTS on the dark's hunters for the first time, from a distance. 1-3's trap waited for its
animal; here he calls it. 2-9's beat is gone (no song): the clock is the patch's own. The hand build's punches go (new replaces old).
**Pushback (design):** (1) PITCH-33B's pebble lure would need a throw to aim on one thumb and a carried pebble (no jump, no climb: STAGE-RULES §1 Carrying); the pick's stone keeps the
punch he has. (2) The pitch's "a lure that misses times out (4.5 s)" is true, but the look resets on every ring heard (core.ts:344-346): without one ring per call the timing is free;
NEW 1 carries it. (3) Matt's "every hazard must matter" (STAGE-RULES §0.2): a 6.4/2.2 patch takes a called prowler 95% of the time, so the patch alone is a teach, not a test; the test's
bite is hearing (two rounds, 80%) and the combine's is the slow patch (70%). **Learned:** fly where a hunter STOPS, not where it goes: an 'alert' hunt halts 120 px short of his landing
(core.ts:576, engage x 0.8), so the first bait layout (patch 40 px before the landing) caught 0%.
