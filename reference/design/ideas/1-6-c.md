## TRIBUTES (research by web search, 2026-11-13; this session's network blocks page fetches, so each fact is from search results at the URLs cited)

**Pitfall! (Activision, 1982): the vine over the pit**
a. A vine swings by itself over a pit that is always on screen; you stand at the edge and press when the vine is overhead, Harry grabs it and rides across; push down at the farthest point of the swing and he lands safely. Loved for the one clean decision: the swing is free, the timing is yours (Joystik 1982: https://vgpavilion.com/mags/1982/12/joystik/pitfall). Crane wanted a Tarzan yell on the grab (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitfall!).
b. The hazard is always visible and still, the vine is the only moving thing: you watch one full swing before you commit. Moving pits on a timer come later (https://www.hardcoregaming101.net/pitfall/).
c. Beat 3: the edge press waits for the vine (engine.ts:2290), the floor below is soft moss, he watches it swing first; one tap catches, one tap lets go at the far point. A happy whoop (our own sound) on the first catch.
d. Not copied: Harry, the tar pit, crocodiles, the yell, the 2600 look. Our liana, our moss, our sound.

**Jungle Hunt / Jungle King (Taito, 1982): vine to vine**
a. Vines swing on their own; you press when two vines come close and he leaps across; a miss is a fall. The first scene uses swings of varying distances, so the timing keeps changing; some vines carry a gorilla, so you time the catch AND avoid it (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungle_Hunt; https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Jungle_Hunt).
b. It starts on the vines with near, regular gaps, then varies the distances, then puts a creature on a vine.
c. Beats 5 and 11: liana to liana with each next one a chainPhase ahead (the widest window, never in step); gaps of different lengths so each catch has its own timing; the creature on the vine is OUR vine snake (beats 6-7), and the grip choice is ours, not theirs.
d. Not copied: the explorer, the gorilla, the scenes (river, boulders, cannibals), the look. No people in World 1.

**Donkey Kong Jr. (Nintendo, 1982): creatures that travel the vines**
a. On the Vine Scene, Snapjaws climb the vines toward Junior; blue ones run down a vine and fall off its end, red ones keep patrolling up and down; one vine climbs at half speed, two at twice, but two means more exposure; fruit dropped on them beats them (https://mariowiki.com/Snapjaw; https://www.mariowiki.com/Vine_Scene; https://arcadesushi.com/tribute-donkey-kong-jr/; https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/DonkeyKongJr). Loved because the danger has a path you can read and Junior always has a move (change vine, wait, drop fruit).
b. The first threats are the simple ones that only pass (blue: down and off), the persistent two-way ones (red) come after.
c. Beat 6: the first vine snake only passes, down and off into the ferns (blue); beat 7 one comes down HIS vine and pushes him low, where he flies farther; the twist (beat 10) brings them UP as well (red). The thumb never needs a new move: up, down, let go. 1-4 owns the one-or-two vine climb; 1-6 takes only the creatures on the vines.
d. Not copied: Junior, Mario, the cage, Snapjaw looks, fruit-drop, the score screens. Our snake is a real Neotropical vine snake.

**Donkey Kong Country (Rare, 1994): ropes that never stop**
a. Swinging ropes sway back and forth without losing momentum and carry the Kongs over big gaps; they debut in Ropey Rampage, the game's second level, among storm-lit treetops; later ropes only start when grabbed (https://www.mariowiki.com/Ropey_Rampage; https://mariowiki.com/Ropes). Loved for the rhythm: the rope keeps time, you only choose the moment.
b. A walkthrough excerpt describes the level first having you climb a rope, then grab one that only starts swinging once you are on it, before chains over gaps (https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Donkey_Kong_Country/Ropey_Rampage; the first rope's exact place not confirmed).
c. Matt already chose this feel (STAGE-RULES.md:377-389, physics.ts:457-463): every liana keeps its own time and he only picks the moment; 1-6 adds the grip as the one thing a thumb can tune (bullet 13 measures it). Our swing is slower and calmer (2.85 s, 0.45 rad).
d. Not copied: the Kongs, barrels, Kremlings, the storm level, the music, the look.

**Nature check (for the story keeper):** the brown vine snake (Oxybelis aeneus) lives from Mexico through Central America into South America, sways like a wind-blown vine and is often mistaken for one; it eats lizards (https://reptilesofaz.org/?p=257; https://azgfd-hdms.s3.amazonaws.com/public/abstracts/Oxybaene.pdf; https://patagoniaalliance.org/?p=10367). Note for the lead: the game's "Golden Tree Snake" (PREDATORS.md:38, :73, the gliding flying snake) is Chrysopelea ornata of South and Southeast Asia (https://www.ecologyasia.com/verts/snakes/golden_tree-snake.htm), so it fails the Central/South American check; 1-6's vine snakes should not borrow its glide.
