1. **THE ONE IDEA:** "When the shadow comes, get in the green and hold still, and it flies right over you." (bush first, then moss, then a mossy liana.)
2. **Beat 1, the bush (safe teach, the story's own bush):** Dot, 800 px before (keeper's words; the guard says "In the bush, hold still"): a wide bush, a
   `freeFirst` stretch so the first screech and shadow cannot hurt; the moss tint creeps over him in 0.5 s and a soft calm sound plays. He just watches it pass.
3. **Beat 2, red light, green light:** three bushes ~480 px apart under one rhythm: move on calm, be in a bush and still by the end of the 2.0 s screech.
   The shadow always enters from the screech's side, so the player learns to read WHERE as well as WHEN. First hurt of the stage only after this row.
4. **Beat 3, "the leaves shake":** a bush with a petal just outside its far edge. Reach for it during the sweep and the bush rustles: seen (stillness.ts:67-68).
   Taught on a `freeFirst` stretch: the eagle screeches, circles back, no dive; Dot: "Still. Steady." Then it counts.
5. **Beat 4, moss is cover too (the step from bush to "anywhere in moss"):** a long mossy log and a moss bank on a trunk's foot, no bush; the moss tint
   and the bank are the same green, so the player SEES why he vanished. Bushes thin out; moss patches become the stepping stones.
6. **Beat 5, hold still on a hanging green (grows from 1-6):** a liana hung with moss and bromeliads (hanging cover). He hangs on it through a pass: the
   swing still carries him (the vine moving is not him), but climbing is moving. Dot: "Hold on. Calm."
7. **Beat 6, the greedy gap:** a golden seed in the open midway between two cover patches, reachable in one calm on Normal only by a player who left
   cover the instant the shadow cleared; Easy's calm is longer. The adult's test: leave cover early, not late.
8. **Beat 7, both ways:** the sweep's direction rolls per pass (`dir`, watcher.ts:64), so the shadow can come back the way he is heading; the edge it
   screeches from tells him which cover is "behind" the shadow and already safe.
9. **Beat 8, the rest:** a flag beside the biggest bush in the stage; Dot remembers hiding in it ("Was it a nice bush?", STORY-KEEPER.md:151).
10. **THE TWIST, "still is not stopped":** a gap with no ground cover at all, crossed only by riding two mossy lianas in a row. The screech comes while he
    is mid-chain: he holds still on the swing, the liana carries him across under the shadow, he lets go on the next calm. Stillness that still gets
    somewhere (Grandma: "Slow is not stopped, dear", storyMemories.ts:86; keeper guard b: still to stay safe, never to put off going on).
11. **THE FINALE, the eagle gives up:** one long watched stretch with shorter calms (WatchZone.calm per stretch, watcher.ts:29): bush, moss log, mossy
    liana pair, moss bank, last bush by the shrine. Two empty passes in a row and the eagle gives up (PREDATORS.md:131 "2 empty passes"), screeches
    once far off and leaves; the sky brightens, the shrine is clear. He wins by being calm, not by beating anything.
12. **The moment people talk about:** "I hid ON the swing": the shadow crossing a swinging Pokey, moss creeping over him mid-arc, and the eagle missing him.
13. **Trap to avoid:** waiting dressed up as stillness. Never make him sit through more than ONE pass at the same cover (every calm has a reason to move),
    never a gauge, never the words "slow" or "wait"; and pick ONE hide rule: the crouch hide (watcher.ts:159) and stillness (stillness.ts:67-68) disagree
    about a still, standing Pokey in a bush, and a player must never meet both.

## TRIBUTES (web search 2026-11-13; page fetches not used, facts from search results at the URLs cited)

**Metal Gear (Konami, MSX2 1987): hiding from eyes that only see what crosses them** (the "very good bush" is our cardboard box)
a. The only rule for being spotted is crossing a guard's line of sight, and the sight lines are narrow, so the whole game is reading where eyes point
   (https://kotaku.com/metal-gear-the-kotaku-retrospective-1828740753). The box hides Snake when he is STILL; moving in a sight line under it still gets
   him caught (https://gamingpastime.com/metal-gear-msx-review/). Loved because one simple, readable rule makes every room a small puzzle.
b. The first floor teaches sight lines with guards alone; the box comes later (found on floor 3) and is the answer to a room of three cameras where
   hiding is otherwise near impossible (https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Metal_Gear/B1_Floor_1, https://retronauts.com/article/445/the-anatomy-of-metal-gear-part-1-on-site-procurement).
   Rule first, tool second: our bush (beat 1) is safe AND the rule at once, because World 1 is for children too.
c. Phone: the thumb does NOTHING to hide. Let go of the stick inside green and the moss tint is the "box". Touching the stick in the sweep shakes the
   leaves. One rule, no button, readable on a 412 px screen because the eagle's shadow (not a thin cone) shows where the eyes are.
d. Not copied: boxes, soldiers, codec calls, the "!" alert, any name or art. Ours is a bush and moss in our forest, an eagle that is nature, not a guard.

**Super Mario Bros. 3 (Nintendo, 1988): Boo Diddly, red light green light turned round**
a. A ghost that freezes and covers its face while Mario looks at it and creeps after him when he turns away: the player controls the danger with a
   look, a tiny rhythm game (https://www.mariowiki.com/Boo_Diddly). Search found no designer quote on why; the appeal named here is ours.
b. First met in World 2's fortress, in close quarters where the player has room to discover the rule by turning (same source).
c. We flip it back the right way round for a sloth: the HUNTER moves, Pokey freezes. The tell for both sides is visible (the screech and the shadow's
   edge; the moss on Pokey), so the player always knows who is "looking", as Mario did.
d. Not copied: ghosts, faces hidden behind hands, any Mario name, art or sound. Matt's "predator shadow, red light green light" (MAP-REVISIONS.md:138) is the source.

**Nature check:** the harpy eagle (Harpia harpyja, Central and South America) hunts sloths and monkeys from the canopy; real sloths rely on stillness and
the algae in their fur (the moss tint is real biology). Bromeliads and moss on lianas fit a Central/South American forest.
