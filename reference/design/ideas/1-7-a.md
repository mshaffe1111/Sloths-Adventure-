1. **THE ONE IDEA:** "When the eagle's shadow comes, freeze in the moss and it can't see you."
2. **Arrive (calm, 1,300 px):** wet moss floor, ferns, bushes; a feather drifts down; far off, one screech with NO shadow (sound first, the way the
   watch always starts). He feels watched before anything can happen. Meeca's petal by the first bush.
3. **Teach 1, the bush (safe, first pass free):** Dot, 800 px before (level.md rule 6), keeper's line in spirit of "In the bush, hold still!". A bush
   every 460-540 px (one always on an upright screen, w1Sections.ts:384-389). He slides in, the moss tint creeps over him, a soft calm hum; the
   shadow slides over; "PHEW!". The first pass cannot hurt (freeFirst).
4. **Teach 2, same lesson, the leaves shake:** inside a bush he wiggles (walks) as the shadow passes, the bush shakes and the eagle screeches
   again, harmless this once. He learns the bush alone is not enough: STILL in the bush (stillness.ts:66-67). This is the bush lesson, not a 2nd twist.
5. **Practise, the moss banks:** low mossy banks between bushes (a `Cover`, stillness.ts:70-86); stand on one, stop, the tint comes in (0.5 s),
   the shadow passes. Feels: "I'm the forest." Bushes thin out as moss banks take over.
6. **Use, red light / green light:** a long moss-floor walk with the eagle's rhythm closer (calm shorter), petals strung low along it; he walks

10. **THE TWIST, the look-back:** the shadow passes, the calm hum starts... and the shadow sweeps BACK once (a second pass without a new screech,

## TRIBUTES (web search 2026-11-13; page fetches not tried, every fact from search results at the URLs cited)

**Red Light, Green Light (the playground game, Matt's idea) via Super Mario Bros. 3's Boo Diddly (Nintendo, 1988)**
a. The Boo is shy: while Mario faces it, it freezes; turn your back and it chases. Loved because the rule is one glance long and the tension is
   all in your own nerve; fans and wikis call it red light, green light (https://www.mariowiki.com/Boo_Diddly, https://www.mariowiki.com/boo).
b. Met first in the Ghost Houses with room to turn and test it; you learn by turning back and forth (mariowiki, above).
c. Ours is the mirror: the hunter looks, Pokey holds. The thumb simply lets go of the stick (no button for "freeze"); the tell comes in on
   Pokey (moss tint, hum) in 0.5 s, so a thumb that lifts on the screech is always in time. Taught in a bush, the safe version.
d. Not copied: the Boo, ghosts, Ghost Houses, any Nintendo art, name or sound.

**Metal Gear (Konami, MSX 1987): hide from a line of sight**
a. Guards see in a straight line in front; crossing it is the one rule; sneaking close unseen by the right route was the joy, rooms as
   puzzles (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal_Gear_(video_game), https://www.hardcoregaming101.net/metal-gear/).
b. You start with nothing, so the first rooms teach that being seen is the danger; crates and trucks are the cover
   (https://retronauts.com/article/445/the-anatomy-of-metal-gear-part-1-on-site-procurement).
c. Our sight is a shadow sweeping the ground (always on screen, never a hidden cone), and our cover is living: a bush, then moss. One rule
   (moving in sight = seen) like theirs; the first time is free.
d. Not copied: soldiers, guns, boxes, alarms, any base or military look (World 1 has no made things).
