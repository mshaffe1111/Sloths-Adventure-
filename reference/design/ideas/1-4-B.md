# 1-4 "Up the Kapok": idea list B (idea helper B, 2026-11-12 6:09 PM ET; Matt's "why two vines?" folded in 6:10 PM ET)

1. **THE ONE IDEA:** "Pokey climbs like a real sloth: on one vine he goes hand over hand, slow; on two, one per long arm, he flies up, and the kapok keeps giving him a reason to hurry." A slow sloth finding his own way to be quick is the story's "too slow" theme in his own hands.
2. **The foot (first climb, taught):** buttress roots, Meeca's petals walking up the bark; one short single vine straight up to a wide low bough with ground right under it; nothing can go wrong. Feels: "oh, this one goes UP."
3. **Rotting boughs (taught):** the first rotting bough sits over a sound bough one step below; it sags, creaks, shows its notched underside and drops after a beat; he lands on the sound one and loses nothing. Then "now you": two in a row to cross at a walk, no stopping. Feels: nerves, then "I can read that."
4. **Fruit down the trunk (taught, on ONE vine):** from a rest ledge beside the column he watches a laden bough shake, a shadow grow on the bark, a kapok pod tumble past and burst into white fluff (a soft knock, never a splat); the tell is on screen before every drop, sideways too. Then a single vine with a knot-hole to wait in: safe, but stop and go. Feels: "watch, wait, go."
5. **Two vines, taught as the ANSWER (Matt's question):** the next column drops pods too often to cross on one vine, and it has no knot-hole; beside it hangs a pair, a liana split around a knot (grown by the tree, not placed); arms spread, claws in both, he is through between two pods. He felt the problem first, so the pair is "oh, THAT's what it's for", not a trick. Dot gives one line.
6. **Fast lane or safe lane (grown):** the pair hangs right in the fruit column, the single vine climbs under a bough overhang out of it; petals reward the brave lane; both lanes always reach the top. Feels: choice and nerve.
7. **Rotting steps between vines (grown):** short rotting boughs are the only footing between vine stretches, each crumbling as he lands (required now, whole again on respawn); where a pair starts from one, the pair is how he leaves before it goes. Feels: flow, keep moving.
8. **Rest high up:** a wide sound bough, a bromeliad, Meeca's scratched petal in the bark, a flag; the camera shows the forest floor far below. Feels: pride, a breath.
9. **THE TWIST: the snake climbs too.** A Golden Tree Snake starts up the vines BELOW him: on a single vine it would catch him, on the pair he stays ahead to a laden bough, shakes its vine (1-3's handle), and the pods tumble down the column; the snake glides off to the next tree, nobody hurt. The fruit that knocked him off all level is his to drop, kindly.
10. **THE FINALE:** the crown stretch: pairs in the fruit lane, singles under shelter, rotting boughs as the only steps, one laden bough to shake to clear the lane for a sprint, and one more snake to send gliding; a flag at its start, and a miss costs one stretch only (every knock lands on the bough below).
11. **The moment people talk about:** he pushes up through the last leaves and is ABOVE the whole forest for the first time; the camera pulls all the way back at sunset, the last petal sits in a bromeliad on the crown, Dot goes quiet (her dead stop when something is real); a far-north speck of the tower on the skyline only if the story keeper agrees.
12. **The trap to avoid: the pair as a gimmick.** A pair where nothing hurries him is just a faster button, so the pairs stop feeling like the rainforest and start feeling like a power-up. Every pair answers a reason to hurry that he can see from below: a pod rhythm, a crumbling bough, a snake behind. Every pair grows the way lianas do, never as a labelled thing, and his pose shows both arms on it. A single vine is never "wrong", only slower and safer.
