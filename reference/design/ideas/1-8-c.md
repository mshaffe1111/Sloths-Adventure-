## TRIBUTE
### Jungle Hunt / Jungle King (Taito, 1982): the boulder hill, "jump the low, pass under the high"
a. The explorer runs up a slope while boulders come down: small rocks roll (jump them), big boulders bounce, so you jump, run under or duck them; big ones bounce higher, so timing is everything, and the bold jump scores most (200 vs 100 duck vs 50 run under) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungle_Hunt; https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Jungle_Hunt; https://www.mobygames.com/game/133; https://tasvideos.org/5636S, search result only). Loved for one glance, one answer, at a run.
b. It is the game's third scene, after the vines and the river (Wikipedia); I found no source on its first-boulder teaching, so none is claimed. Matt's own #5 (MATT-80S-ARCADE-IDEAS-2026-11-11.md:12) is this idea.
c. Beats 3-4 and 9: small nut rolls = hop; big pod bounces = run under; each taught alone and safe before the chase, then together. Thumb: tap to hop, do nothing to pass under; the pod's ground shadow gives its height at a glance on a phone.
d. Not copied: the explorer, the hill, cannibals, the rescue, the scoring, the look. Our branch, our fruit, a sloth.
### Pitfall! (Activision, 1982): the rolling logs that only cost points
a. Logs roll along the jungle floor; you jump them, and touching one costs points, never a life (the only hazard that cannot kill), so they set the rhythm of a run without ending it (https://vgpavilion.com/mags/1982/12/joystik/pitfall; https://discmaster.textfiles.com/view/1886/1000gamemanuals.iso/Atari 2600/Pitfall.txt).
b. The manual tells players to learn to jump the rolling logs and the holes first, then the harder skills; logs are on many screens (https://www.hardcoregaming101.net/pitfall/; manual above). Whether the first screen has one: not confirmed.
c. Beat 3: the first rolling nut is the "log": a bump nudges him back, no heart, so the hop is learned before anything hurts (level.md rule 7).
d. Not copied: Harry, logs, pits, crocodiles, the treasure count, the look.
### Crash Bandicoot (Naughty Dog, 1996), "Boulders": the chase
a. A giant boulder rolls after Crash and he must keep running or be crushed, with the camera in front so you see it coming; the first chase level in the series and a nod to Raiders of the Lost Ark (https://crashbandicoot.fandom.com/wiki/File:PLATINUM_RELIC_CRASH_BANDICOOT_N_SANE_TRILOGY_Crash_1_-_Boulders; https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Videogame/CrashBandicoot1996; https://medium.com/@varunbajaj/crash-bandicoot-designing-games-to-balance-challenge-and-fun-a-game-design-critique-225af9d0661a). Loved because the pressure is constant and readable: the threat is behind, the answers ahead.
b. It is one of the game's easiest levels and was moved earlier in development for difficulty balance (https://tcrf.net/Proto:Crash_Bandicoot/April_8,_1996_build): the first chase is gentle, the hard ones come later.
c. 1-8's collapse is a soft chase on purpose: slower than his run (230 vs 285), every beat passable in stride, a rest at each living fork, the camera leading ahead.
d. Not copied: Crash, the boulder, the toward-camera view, the temple, the music. A side-on branch that breaks, and nobody is crushed: a miss is a heart and the flag.
