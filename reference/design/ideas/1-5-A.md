# 1-5 "Where She Walked": idea list A (idea helper A, 2026-11-12 8:17 PM ET; brief reference/packets/2026-11-13/IDEAS-1-5.md)
Code facts are from origin/t-int-2; 1-4 facts from origin/t-14-design.

1. **THE ONE IDEA:** "At every split the squirrels paint two ways, up over the creaky limbs or down where Meeca walked, and the way I pick is the way the forest lays out ahead of me."
2. **Arrive at the crown, going down:** he starts where 1-4 left him, high in the kapok at dusk, and works DOWN (stages.ts:11). A warm-up on sound boughs, then the first of Meeca's marks scratched on bark with Dot's line that these are hers. The marks are the goal (stages.ts:41: 10 on Normal, 8 on Easy), so he learns to look for her marks before any sign shows up.
3. **Teach over/under; it can't fail:** the first split fits one upright screen (level.md rule 8). Two big leaves hang from a trunk, painted with berry juice: THIS WAY ↓ with a petal on it, THAT WAY → with a cracked limb on it. Signs go on living leaves or bark, never a post (no made things in World 1). The over road is ONE creaky limb that drops him onto the under road 240-260 px below (w1mix.ts:1065), and a straight vine climbs him back up. Lesson: "a fall from over lands you under, never in a pit."
4. **Why pick OVER (THAT WAY, the squirrels' road):** it's shorter and quicker, and from up there you SEE what's ahead, including the "something shiny, far away" (stages.ts:35's puzzle beat; the seed can only be reached from over). The risk is creaky limbs (rot read by shape, taught in 1-4: design/1-4.md:17). A limb that goes costs him the shortcut, never a heart.
5. **Why pick UNDER (THIS WAY, where she walked):** this is where her folded petals lie, pressed low at marching height (STORY-KEEPER.md:153), along the creek's lily pads and reeds (stages.ts:26), with a snake dozing on a bough over the trail and a tendril he shakes down to move it (1-3's drop trap). The footing is sure, with more to read and more ground to cover, and it carries the petals and Dot's story lines. No breakables down here: there's no W1 ground-rot art yet (stages.ts:24, R-165.30).
6. **Card road, the first real pick:** after a rest, the second pair of leaf-signs, and the pick lays the WHOLE next stretch: over gets a creaky-bough run, under gets the creek. It is not today's fork (w1mix.ts:1049-1104), which is two bursts that rejoin within seconds. Meeca's marks sit on the banks before and after the split (w1mix.ts:1104), so no pick costs him the goal.
7. **Practise both, braided:** one screen with both roads and vine ladders between them. The snake is on the under road and a pair of creaky limbs is on the over road. The clever player climbs over the snake and drops back down past the rot, so the roads become tools, not lanes.
8. **The trail chase:** dusk deepens, the petals get fresher (Dot, stages.ts:27: "These petals are fresher. She came this way not long ago.") and the stretches get longer and tighter. The chase is him gaining on her trail, never soldiers on screen and never limbs collapsing behind him (that is 1-8's).
9. **THE TWIST:** a leaf-sign turns in the breeze on its stalk and now points the other way. The squirrels' sign says over, but Meeca's fresh mark is scratched on the under road. He learns to trust where she actually walked over where the squirrels guessed. Her road finds the stage's best folded petal; the sign's road still rejoins (rule 5: nothing strands him).
10. **THE FINALE:** one long last split with no signs at all, only her marks. Over is creaky limbs in a row, the only footing, so he keeps moving. Under is the creek with a dozing snake and its tendril. He reads her marks to choose and can switch by vine halfway. Both roads come down to the forest floor at the creek bank, where a petal folded tight lies on a root. Dot: "She's making them last." A quiet end, facing north.
11. **The moment people talk about:** "The top branch broke under me and I landed right on her trail, and a folded petal was waiting where I fell." Under every over-road limb that can drop him, the landing spot on the under road holds a petal, so the fall itself pays.
12. **One trap to avoid:** a coin-flip fork, where both roads are the same length with the same pay, or the pay sits up high. Today's fork puts its petals on the high road only (w1mix.ts:1089, the designer's ruling (i) 11-05), against the story's "petals low at marching height; the trail, never a prize up high" (STORY-KEEPER.md:153). Over must pay in speed, view and the seed; under in petals and her story. Otherwise players stop reading the signs.
