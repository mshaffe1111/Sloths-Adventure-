1. **THE ONE IDEA:** "The branch is breaking behind you and its nuts roll back at you: hop the rollers, run under the bouncers, never stop."
2. **Why nuts roll at him (the logic a child gets):** as the branch breaks behind him his end sags, so everything loose on it rolls DOWNHILL toward him from
   ahead, the way the branch falls. Low rollers: round palm nuts. High bouncers: heavy Brazil-nut pods that hit knots and hop.
3. **Beat 1, the calm start (teach the low nut, safe):** before the collapse sets off, one palm nut rolls lazily down the still branch. Dot, 800 px before:
   "Nuts on the branch! Hop over them." Touching it here only nudges him back (Pitfall's log costs points, not a life); the chase has not started.
4. **Beat 2, teach the high one (safe):** still before the start line, a pod bounces down off a knot in big arcs; he walks under while it is up. A ghost
   footprint of where it lands is the tell (the shadow it casts on the bark). Missing it is a nudge, not a heart.
5. **Beat 3, it begins:** he passes the start x, a deep crack behind, the collapse sets off 520 px back. Never look back: the sound and the cracking bark
   300 px ahead of the front tell him how close it is. First stretch: open branch, no nuts, just run (he feels he is faster: 285 vs 230).
6. **Beat 4, two heights at pace:** single nuts, then a low and a high spaced so their rhythm reads from one upright screen: hop, run, run under, hop.
   Each nut is heard as it drops onto the branch ahead (a "thunk" before it is on screen) so an upright phone never surprises him.
7. **Beat 5, World 1 asks for what it taught (1-1 to 1-6):** a break he jumps (gap), a double-jump break (leap), a self-swinging liana over a fallen
   section (1-6; swing), a creaky limb that gives under him (1-5), a snake on the bough he hops (1-3); the old chase pieces, re-laid as gen2 beats.
8. **Beat 6, a breath:** a flag in a knot-hollow where the branch meets a living trunk; the collapse stops at the trunk (CHASE.hold 1.6 s on restart is
   the same breath). Dot: "Nearly there. Keep going!" Never a stillness beat in a chase (keeper guard b).
9. **Beat 7, nuts among the call-backs:** a low nut rolling down a liana's landing, a pod bouncing over a creaky limb: two old things at once, never
   more than two questions on one screen (level.md rule 8).
10. **THE TWIST, two limbs (the taste of lanes):** the branch forks into an upper and a lower limb side by side; nuts roll down one limb at a time and
    switch. He reads which limb is clear and changes limb (bounce up from a mushroom, 1-2; drop down) while the collapse eats both. Two heights of nut
    become two heights of path: World 2's river owns real lanes (Q1), this is one stretch.
11. **THE FINALE, the branch lets go:** the last stretch runs faster (+60), the nuts come in a short run of hop/under/hop, then the end of the branch
    tears free; the final leap (slow motion, engine.ts:745) onto a liana that swings him to the boa's tree and the shrine (1-9 begins there).
12. **The moment people talk about:** the last leap in slow motion as the whole Long Branch drops away into the green below, nuts and all.
13. **Trap to avoid:** reading time. At 285 px/s and about 270 px seen ahead upright, a nut that enters the screen gives under a second: every nut is
    heard before it is seen, rolls slower than he runs, and the low/high read is shape AND height (round vs. pod), never colour alone. Also: no blind
    hazard from behind (the Crash camera complaint), and Easy is never harder in any number.

## TRIBUTES (web search 2026-11-13; page fetches not used, facts from search results at the URLs cited)

**Donkey Kong (Nintendo, arcade 1981): rolling barrels you jump**
a. Jumping was rare in games then; the team let the hero jump OVER the rolling barrels as the answer to them, and it set the platformer template
   (https://tcrf.net/Prerelease:Donkey_Kong_(Arcade), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donkey_Kong_(1981_video_game)). Loved: a clean read (it comes down
   the girder at you), one button, and the barrel's slope makes the danger's path obvious.
b. The barrels are the first screen: they roll down sloped girders, so the player sees them coming far off and learns the jump on the first one.
c. Ours rolls DOWN the sagging branch for the same reason: the slope shows its path. The thumb taps jump; the first nut, before the chase, only nudges.
d. Not copied: barrels, the ape, girders, ladders, the hammer, any name, art or sound.

**Jungle Hunt / Jungle King (Taito, arcade 1982): boulders at two heights**
a. Boulders of different sizes bounce at different speeds and heights, so the player jumps or ducks at the right moment; small ones are jumped, large
   ones bounce high and can be run under (https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungle_Hunt, https://www.arcadeclub.co.uk/games/jungle-hunt/). Loved: reading
   size and arc in a split second, at a run.
b. The boulder scene comes third, after the vines and the river, so players meet it already fluent in the jump; search found no detail on its first boulders.
c. Low nut = hop, high pod = run under (no duck: a duck in a chase costs speed). Shape and height tell them apart; the landing shadow tells where a pod hits.
d. Not copied: the explorer, the boulders' look, the cannibal scene (never), any name, music or art.

**Pitfall! (Activision, 1982): logs that cost points, not a life**
a. Rolling logs are the one hazard that never kills Harry, only costs points, and stopping lets them gain on you
   (https://vgpavilion.com/mags/1982/12/vi/conquering-pitfall, https://www.hardcoregaming101.net/pitfall/). Loved: a gentle hazard that still has rhythm.
b. The gentlest hazard is the commonest: players learn the jump on logs long before a mistake can end the run.
c. Beats 1 and 2: before the chase starts, a nut or pod only nudges him back, so the child learns both heights with nothing to lose (level.md rule 7).
d. Not copied: Harry, the logs, scorpions, crocodiles, any art or sound.

**Crash Bandicoot (Naughty Dog, 1996): the boulder chase**
a. A chase where the threat is always behind: one path, constant pressure, the tension of never stopping
   (https://all-things-andy-gavin.com/tag/character-action-games/, https://medium.com/@varunbajaj/crash-bandicoot-designing-games-to-balance-challenge-and-fun-a-game-design-critique-225af9d0661a).
b. It came fourth, after three ordinary levels; a fruit arrow at its start pointed the new way, and following the fruit avoided the obstacles. Its known
   flaw: the camera hid what was ahead (https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Concepts/Running_Towards_the_Screen).
c. We keep the pressure and drop the flaw: side-on, the danger behind is HEARD (cracks), the danger ahead is SEEN and heard; petals on the run's line
   lead the safe path, as Crash's fruit did.
d. Not copied: the boulder, the run-at-the-camera view, the bandicoot, any name, art or music.
