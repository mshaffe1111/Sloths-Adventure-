# 1-6 "First Lianas": idea list A (idea member A, 2026-11-13 3:36-4:06 PM ET; brief reference/packets/2026-11-13/IDEAS-1-6.md)
Facts checked in code: every liana swings on its own clock, never pushed (src/game/physics.ts:457-498, Normal 2.85 s ±0.45 rad, Easy 3.3 s);
on it up/down climb, left/right only turn him, letting go is the one choice and he leaves with the vine's speed AT HIS GRIP (physics.ts:459-463);
the next liana of a chain is phased for the widest leap window, never in step (chainPhase, physics.ts:789); chains are up to 560 px apart
(CHAIN.dx, physics.ts:521); a golden sweet-spot glow already shows when to let go (engine.ts:887-892). Note: reference/design/1-4.md and 1-5.md
are not on t-int-3 (only 1-2.md, 1-3.md), so the bar was read from 1-3.md and level.md.

1. **THE ONE IDEA:** "Wait for the vine to swing to you, ride it, and let go when you glow; hold low to fly far."
2. **Beat 1, the safe first swing (teach):** Dot, 800 px before: "Lianas swing all by themselves. Hop on, then let go when you glow!" One liana over a
   soft mossy dip (no hurt; a miss lands him on moss and a vine-ladder walks him back up). The far ledge is wide and low: the first swing cannot fail.
3. **Beat 2, jump from the ledge when it comes (Pitfall's lesson):** one liana swinging toward and away from a ledge. Jumping when it swings away
   drops him onto moss again; he learns to wait one swing and jump as it comes TO him. Still nothing that hurts.
4. **Beat 3, the first chain:** two lianas over a shallow thorn patch (thorns taught in 1-2), off-step clocks. He feels the rhythm: catch, ride,
   glow, go. First hurt in the stage, with a rest ledge after it.
5. **Beat 4, low flies far (the turn):** a far ledge only reachable by climbing DOWN to the liana's foot before letting go (bigger speed at a low grip);
   a fruit hangs at the foot as the hint. Next to it a near, high ledge that wants a HIGH grip (a low grip overshoots into the next thorns).
6. **Beat 5, the vine snakes arrive (safe):** a thin green vine snake asleep coiled at the TOP of a liana, far above his grip. Dot: "A vine snake!
   They're shy. Stay low and swing on by." It never wakes. Nature: the brown/green vine snake (Oxybelis) is a real Central and South American canopy snake.
7. **Beat 6, the snake slides down (Donkey Kong Jr.'s lesson):** an awake vine snake glides down a liana on its own slow clock, pauses at the foot,
   glides back up. He times the catch or climbs up past it on the down-swing; kind fix: shake a still vine first (1-3's "off the snakes glide")
   and it slips off to the next tree, unhurt.
8. **Beat 7, creaky limbs between swings:** the landing between two lianas is a creaky limb (taught in 1-5, MAP-REVISIONS.md:57; may be required): land, wait for
   the next liana's swing, go before it gives. Required ones are whole again on respawn.
9. **Beat 8, the story rest:** a broad bough with Meeca's mark and a petal: "She swung across here. Grandpa taught her." The gorge opens below.
10. **THE TWIST:** let go on the BACK swing. A ledge sits up and BEHIND the liana; he turns (left/right) and lets go as it swings back. Everything
    so far went forward; now the vine's return is the move.
11. **THE FINALE, the gorge:** four lianas (Easy: three, plus one rest ledge) over the river gorge, each on its own clock; one carries a sliding
    vine snake, one needs the low-grip fling across the widest gap, the landing in the middle is a creaky limb. The far bank shows the view north.
12. **The moment people talk about:** hanging at the very foot of the longest liana, the vine snake gliding down toward him, waiting for the glow,
    then flinging across the whole gorge.
13. **Trap to avoid:** dead air and blind leaps. 2.85 s a swing x a dozen lianas is a lot of waiting, so never make him miss a whole swing on a
    ledge for nothing (place him so the next liana reaches him within about half a swing); and a 560 px chain is wider than an upright phone
    (412 px): the next liana and its landing must be on the upright screen from where he decides (level.md rule 8), or the camera must lead.
14. **Building blocks:** EXISTS: liana self-swing, chains and windows (physics.ts:498, :789, :725); sweet-spot glow (engine.ts:887-892);
    snake + sleeping snake (src/game/gen2/worlds/w1Cast.ts:28-29); shake-a-vine snake trap (w1Arc13.ts:35, :220); creaky limb and rope/vine
    (NODE-KEY rows k "Crumble", r "Rope and vine: liana taught 1-6"); thorn floor (1-2). NEW: a vine snake that rides a liana on a clock (a creature
    moving along a moving vine), its painted art (Hugging Face), a soft moss "miss floor" for the teach beats, the gorge/river backdrop, the
    back-swing ledge layout in the generator's reach check.

## TRIBUTE
### Pitfall! (Activision, 1982): the swinging vine over the pit
a. A vine swings by itself over tar pits, quicksand and crocodiles; you run, jump as it comes overhead, ride, and release at the far end of the
   swing. Loved for the timing (run to meet the vine, not chase it) and Harry's smooth swing animation (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitfall!).
   A leap at a vine swinging away usually misses into the pit (vgpavilion.com/mags/1982/12/joystik/pitfall).
b. Taught by the screen itself: some pits have a vine, some don't; you meet the vine early and learn "jump when it's overhead, push down at the
   farthest point" (manual text, discmaster.textfiles.com/view/1886/1000gamemanuals.iso/Atari 2600/Pitfall.txt; vgpavilion.com/mags/1982/12/vi/conquering-pitfall).
c. 1-6 beat 2: jump from the ledge when the liana swings TO you, over a soft moss miss; thumb: tap Jump as it comes, tap again on the glow.
d. Not copied: Harry, the crocodiles, tar pits, treasures, the screen-by-screen jungle, the art or sounds. Mechanic and timing feel only.
### Donkey Kong Jr. (Nintendo, 1982): vines you climb and the things that slide down them
a. Junior climbs vines; Snapjaws crawl up and down them, the blue ones sliding fast down and off; dropping fruit knocks them off
   (mariowiki.com/Vine_Scene; mariowiki.com/Donkey_Kong_Jr._(game)). Loved because the vine is a lane you share with a threat: up, down or switch.
b. The first stage (Vine Scene) is the vines themselves with few Snapjaws at first, more as the round goes on (mariowiki.com/Vine_Scene).
c. 1-6 beats 5-6: the first vine snake asleep at the top, then one gliding down a liana on its own slow clock; his thumb climbs (up/down)
   to stay out of its way or waits a swing; the kind answer is shaking it off, never hurting it (World 1: clever and kind).
d. Not copied: Junior, Mario, the cage, Snapjaws' look, the stage layout or music. Our snakes are shy rainforest snakes that glide off, unhurt.
### Donkey Kong Country (Rare, 1994): ropes that swing on their own
a. Swinging ropes move on their own; you jump on, ride, and jump off carrying the swing (mariowiki.com/Swinging_vine). Loved for the rhythm and
   momentum of chaining rope to rope over gaps. Our self-swing already follows it (STAGE-RULES.md:380-382, Matt).
b. The ropes debut in the SECOND level, Ropey Rampage, not the first: the first level teaches run and jump, the second swings among treetops
   (mariowiki.com/Swinging_vine; strategywiki.org/wiki/Donkey_Kong_Country/Ropey_Rampage). No source found on its exact teaching beats (named plainly).
c. 1-6 beats 1-3: one liana alone, then one from a ledge, then a chain of two, each on its own clock; thumb: one tap to catch, one to let go,
   with the glow as the beat.
d. Not copied: the Kongs, Kritters, barrels, the rope art or music, level names. Self-swinging lianas and release timing only.
