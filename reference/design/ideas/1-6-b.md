# 1-6 "First Lianas": ideas, list B (idea member B, 11-13 3:40 PM ET; brief reference/packets/2026-11-13/IDEAS-1-6.md)

1. **THE ONE IDEA:** "Let go at the top of the swing, and the vine that isn't swinging is a snake."
2. **Arrive, the safe first swing:** a shallow dip of soft ground, one liana over it. Dot, 800 px before (level.md rule 6): "Jump to a liana, then jump again as it swings forward!" A miss drops him a hop down onto moss and he walks back up a root step. He feels: "oh, it carries me."
3. **Two lianas, same safe dip:** liana to liana. The chain swings as a wave (`chainPhase`, physics.ts:789), so the next one is always coming toward him when his own reaches the front. He learns the rhythm, not a speed pick: wait one swing, and it comes round again (2.85 s Normal, 3.3 s Easy, physics.ts:471/489).
4. **Up and down change your reach:** a liana whose low end sweeps through a fruit arc (bananas-on-a-rope feel); straight down climbs (STAGE-RULES), and lower on the vine swings further out. Same dip, nothing hurts. This is the lever the rest of the level pulls.
5. **First real drop:** the dip becomes a thorn floor (1-2's thorns), two lianas, a wide sound landing. Easy: a mossy ledge under the middle catches a miss.
6. **Swing onto rotten wood:** a liana lands him on a rotting bough (taught in 1-4); it starts to go, so he jumps straight on to the next liana. Feels: momentum carries through, no stopping.
7. **Meet the vine snake, safely:** off the route, over the bank, among three swinging lianas hangs a fourth "vine" that does NOT swing. It turns its head. Dot: "Excuse me, that vine has eyes." Shy, it slides up into the leaves. No risk, just shown.
8. **The snake hangs in the chain:** in a chain over the dip, one hanging line is a vine snake, still. He must not jump to it. The clever way: climb DOWN his liana so it reaches past the snake to the real liana beyond (beat 4's lesson). Nobody is hurt; he never touches it.
9. **Creaky limb rest (1-5's piece):** a breather limb over a bank, where a fall only drops him to the floor, before the twist.
10. **THE TWIST: snakes come down the lianas.** A vine snake slides slowly DOWN the liana he holds (the DK Jr. vine climbers). He reads it: let go early to the next liana, or climb up and over while it pauses. At a liana's foot a snake glides away (every snake ending is the glide), so waiting is always safe: being patient is the kind answer.
11. **THE FINALE: the gorge.** Five lianas in a wave over thorns, one still snake hanging between two of them (climb down to reach past), one snake sliding down the fourth (let go early), a rotting bough in the middle, then the shrine bank. Easy: a sound bough instead of the rotten one, wider catch (`grabX` 76, physics.ts:490), no sliding snake on the fourth.
12. **The talked-about moment:** in the finale, the line he is lining up for opens its eyes, and the player realises they were about to jump onto a snake. Fair, because beat 7 showed the tell: lianas swing, snakes hang still.
13. **Trap to avoid:** a snake that looks like a liana in a still shot is unfair unless the tell is always readable. It must never swing, it is a different green with a visible head, and the whole question (his liana, the snake, the real next liana) fits one upright screen from where he hangs (rule 8: about 425 px of stage upright). And never a blind leap off-screen.
14. **Building blocks:** the self-swinging liana EXISTS (physics.ts:398-560 `vineAngle`, `swingSetup`, `swingClearance`; `chainPhase` :789; landing `swingLanding` :629); straight up/down climb EXISTS (NODE-KEY row "liana (taught 1-6). **Nature check:** the vine snake is real (*Oxybelis*, brown and green vine snakes of Central and South America): thin, vine-coloured, hangs still from branches. Shy and slow-sliding is storybook. Lianas are real.

## TRIBUTE

### Pitfall! (Activision, 1982): the vine over the pit
a. One vine swings over a pit, a bog or a crocodile lake; you jump to catch it and ride it across. It turned a crossing into a pure timing question, and players loved how smooth it moved for its day. It sold over 4 million copies. ([Wikipedia](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitfall!), [JoyStik 1982](https://vgpavilion.com/mags/1982/12/joystik/pitfall), [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2013/08/02/the-pitfalls-of-atari-development-a-retrospective-on-the-industry-s-first-platformer))
b. David Crane put a swinging vine over the alligators the first time players met them; only later did they meet alligators without a vine. The safe tool came first, then the risk. ([Wikipedia](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitfall!))
c. 1-6 does the same: the first liana hangs over a soft dip (beat 2), and only at beat 5 does the dip become thorns. Thumb: one jump onto the liana, one jump to let go, with the next liana always in view.
d. Not copied: no explorer, no crocodiles, no tar pits, no screen-by-screen jungle. Our forest, our lianas, Pokey.

### Donkey Kong Jr. (Nintendo, 1982): climbing vines and the things that come down them
a. Junior climbs vines while trap-jaw creatures come down at him; grabbing two vines at once climbs faster. Some creatures pace their vine; others drop off when they reach its end. Its climbing became a lasting staple. ([Super Mario Wiki: DK Jr.](https://mariowiki.com/Donkey_Kong_Jr._(game)), [Vine Scene](https://www.mariowiki.com/Vine_Scene), [Data Driven Gamer](https://datadrivengamer.blogspot.com/2020/01/game-144-donkey-kong-junior.html?m=1))
b. The first round, the Vine Scene, teaches it all on one screen: water at the bottom, vines and platforms going up, and the creatures arrive a few at a time so you learn their paths. ([Vine Scene](https://www.mariowiki.com/Vine_Scene))
c. The twist (beat 10): a vine snake slides down Pokey's liana; he reads it and either lets go to the next liana or climbs past while it pauses. Like the creatures that drop off a vine's end, our snake glides away at the liana's foot, so nobody is hurt and waiting is always safe. Thumb: up/down to climb, jump to let go.
d. Not copied: no ape, no plumber, no cage, no trap-jaws, no fruit dropped on enemies. Ours are shy snakes, and Pokey avoids, never attacks.

### Donkey Kong Country (Rare, 1994), "Ropey Rampage": a rope level built on one mechanic
a. Its second level is built around swinging ropes between treetops and over gaps; players praise it as the first level to take a single mechanic and slowly flesh it out. ([StrategyWiki](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Donkey_Kong_Country/Ropey_Rampage), [Super Mario Wiki: Rope](https://mariowiki.com/Rope), [level ranking](https://simplestreviews.blogspot.com/2024/10/donkey-kong-country-level-ranking.html))
b. The rope first appears in that level and is the whole level's idea, building from simple single ropes to longer chains. I did not find a source for where the very first rope is placed, so that is not claimed here. ([Super Mario Wiki: Ropey Rampage](https://www.mariowiki.com/Ropey_Rampage))
c. 1-6 is the same shape: one liana, then two, then reach, then danger, then snakes, then the gorge. Our lianas already swing on their own clocks (STAGE-RULES.md:380-382, Matt: DKC), so the player catches a rhythm, never a still rope.
d. Not copied: no apes, no bananas as a currency, no level names, music or treetop art. Our lianas between living trunks (level.md: no rope in World 1).
