# 1-4 Up the Kapok: idea list A (idea helper A, 2026-11-12 6:20 PM ET)

1. **THE ONE IDEA:** "Race the wind up the inside of the giant kapok: find the twin lianas, climb with all four paws, and beat the falling pods." Why two vines (Matt's question): the wind sets a beat (gust, then ripe pods let go a moment later), and the pair is how a sloth beats that beat; one liana is two hands hand-over-hand, steady; a pair is arms on one, feet on the other, the way real sloths climb, so it reads as Pokey, not a power-up.
2. **Roots (arrive):** buttress roots, the hollow's door at ground level, Meeca's petals drift INTO the trunk on the updraft; first single vine straight up to a solid bough, no danger. Feels: "we're going UP."
3. **The pair, taught safe:** a single vine and a twin pair side by side to the same bough; the petals ride the updraft up the pair. He grabs it, shoots up at double speed with all four limbs. Feels: "whoa." Nothing is chasing yet, so the pair is a gift first, not a test.
4. **Rotting boughs, taught soft:** a dark, mossy bough creaks, sags, crumbles; under it, one short drop onto a solid bough, nothing lost. Then "now you": a rotting bough is the only step between two vines. Whole again if he falls.
5. **Pods, taught where he isn't:** a gust howls, leaves shiver at the top of the screen, a shadow grows, and a kapok pod drops down the OTHER column while he is safe in a knot-hole alcove. Then one comes down his column with an alcove beside him: duck, it bounces past. A knock only drops him to the bough below, never to the roots.
6. **Grow: speed matters:** a long climb with alcoves spaced so a single vine needs two gusts between them (you sit out one in the middle) and the pair makes it in one. Both are fair; the pair is faster and carries the petals. The player WANTS it because it saves a wait, not because the game forces it.
7. **Grow: no camping:** the alcoves up here are rotting wood: they hold through one gust, then crumble. Now the slow vine leaves him on a crumbling alcove when the next gust comes; the pair gets him to solid wood in time. The pair has become the smart choice, earned, not ordered.
8. **Rest window:** a knot-hole opens to the outside: the whole canopy below, an eagle circling far off (not here, just foreshadow), Dot catches up. Breath before the top.
9. **THE TWIST:** one "liana" of a pair is the boa hanging still (tongue flicks, it doesn't sway with the gust while the real vines do). Grabbing it is a short drop, nobody hurt; the clever, kind answer is to shake the real vine beside it so a pod drops near it and the boa slides off into the dark, freeing the pair (1-3's "shake, and off they go", met vertically). Teaches him to watch how things move in the wind.
10. **THE FINALE:** the crown shaft: twin vines in short broken stretches, rotting boughs as the only steps between them, gusts coming faster, rotting alcoves, a boa-vine to read; he switches single to pair to bough to pair on the wind's beat and bursts out the top.
11. **MEMORABLE:** out of the hollow into sunset above the canopy, the kapok's ripe pods burst into drifting white fluff like snow, and Meeca's petals are caught in it, all blowing north. The view he climbed for.
12. **TRAP TO AVOID:** a pod that sends him back to the roots, or one with no readable gust warning, turns a climb into a rage quit; and a level that is just "hold up" in a straight shaft is dull: keep side steps between columns, boughs to stand on, and checkpoints every stretch. Never make the pair a toll on every screen, or it becomes the gimmick Matt fears.
