**One idea:** "Be the bait: the boa strikes at what moves, so make it strike where YOU choose, and let the forest beat it." A player says: "the one where you tease the big
snakes, freeze in their faces, and set the eagle on the Old Boa." (Both pitchers, blind; PICKS-19-22.md. Our own idea: the stillness that HID him in 1-7 becomes the move that BEATS a hunter.)
**The boa, measured from code first, then FLOWN** (origin/t-int-9 @ 1c7f6b954). It never walks (world1.ts:53); it watches 280 px both ways (:52) but a posted boa gives NO "?" first:
it goes straight from rest to its rear when he MOVES within 150 px (engage, :55; core.ts:355). The rear is 0.8 s (:57; Easy x1.3, :11); the strike reaches 128 px, aimed where he
WAS when the rear began (:59; core.ts:245-247); its head stays out 0.8 s (:60). Beaten by: a trap (chased off for good), the eagle's roost (lure: chased off for good, but only while
it is after him, in its rear or strike: lure.ts:5), stillness IN its rear (it flees 4 s and comes back: :67, core.ts:444-447), a punch while its head is out (dazed 2.5 s: :68). It fears
the eagle: the eye passing within 70 px of it chases it off for good (core.ts:221-236; W1_EAGLE sight, world1.ts:84). A still sloth outside 150 px is ignored for ever: the rear is the
only door, so baiting it IS the level. **The freeze, flown** (reference/specs/sims/boaFreeze.ts: the REAL engine for Pokey via realPlay.ts, the REAL kit for the boa via castPlay.ts,
fed as engine.ts:4663-4676 feeds it): from a full run (285 px/s) he is under the still speed 3 frames after the thumb lifts (8 px slide). **Normal: the thumb may lift up to 7 frames
after the rear starts (0.23 s); at 8 frames he is hit. Easy: 19 frames (0.63 s).** Creeping in with taps (1-6 frames on, 3-6 off) wins every time on both settings. Widened: a 0.9 s
rear gives 10 frames, **1.0 s gives 13 (0.43 s)**, 1.1 s gives 15; Easy at 1.0 x 1.3 gives 26 (0.87 s). 0.23 s is under a touch player's reaction, so world1.ts:56's own aim ("fits
inside the rear with room to react") is not met. **RULED (the lead, 7:47 PM ET 11-14): BOA.tell 0.8 -> 1.0 s for every boa in every world** (13 frames on Normal; Worlds 7 and 8 re-run by their bots, rule 22); the creep stays the expert's move.
**Difficulty:** Normal **target 9.5**, pasted: "1-8 7.5 (the chase), 1-9 9.5" (DIFFICULTY.md:60-61), World 1's hardest. Hard because every boa is a read (the wrong answer costs a heart),
the freeze is a nerve test inside its reach, a creaky limb takes freezing away, and the last stretch has two hunters. **Easy ~5.5** (at most 0.6 x 9.5 = 5.7, DIFFICULTY.md:55): the boa's
EASY row (rear x1.3, cool x1.5, world1.ts:11), calm 0.35 s (stillness.ts:22), the eagle's free first pass (1-7), "read it" with two boas. **Length** ~170 s designed; bots must read 2:00+, or a
second "read it" set goes in (more pieces, never padding). **Goal** petals (the kit's count). **Cast** boas (12 as designed: one in any sight except beats 3, 9 and 12, where each still faces him alone) and the harpy eagle (L and S only).
**Look** old mossy boughs high over a river glinting below (World 2 is down there), shed skins, the roost snag, the cannonball tree behind (1-8's end). Nobody is hurt or eaten: a beaten
boa slides off its bough and pours away; the Old Boa drops into the river with a splash and swims off downstream.

## Tributes (the lead checks a/b/c before any build, D58; every quote from a page opened this session, PITCH-B.md "Pages opened")
**Mario Bros. (Nintendo, arcade 1983; Matt's own favourite on the Atari 7800).** a. Computer and Video Games (1989): "incredibly good fun", the Atari 7800 version "slightly better"; its heart
is the window that closes: "If the player allows too much time to pass after doing this the enemy will flip itself back over and recover" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Bros.).
b. The first foes go down to one bump; a later one "can only be flipped when it is touching a platform" (same page): one condition added at a time. c. Beat 4: stop short, it misses, its
head is out 0.8 s, bop it, 2.5 s to pass; taught with the landing outside its strike. d. Not copied: kicking, the POW block, pipes, plumbers, flipped foes.
**Oddworld: Abe's Oddysee (Oddworld Inhabitants, 1997).** a. GameSpot: "the ideal platformer, balancing its action and puzzle elements perfectly to make the game intelligent, engaging, and,
best yet, fun"; its AI "goes a long way towards making you feel as if you're interacting with an actual world and its inhabitants" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oddworld:_Abe%27s_Oddysee).
b. Each creature has one readable rule ("Sligs will shoot on sight, but cannot see through dark areas", same page), and a hero who cannot fight wins by using it. c. The boa's one rule (it
sees what moves) is said once and then only shown; every boa is a small one-screen puzzle with a flag before it. d. Not copied: possession, chanting, guns, the factory.
**Limbo (Playdead, 2010).** a. Its makers: "it was fun and scary before the spider came in, and it just got worse, or better, when the spider came in" (https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/hanging-in-limbo).
b. Testers stared at a trap that shared the spider's screen, so it was moved; "the spider's actions would eventually cause this trap to drop to the ground and become a weapon against the
spider" (Carlsen, GDC Europe, told on https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limbo_(video_game); the Gamasutra original would not open [S]). c. One hunter met again and again, closer each time,
always beaten by the forest, never a weapon; the Old Boa's fall into the river is our spider fleeing. We keep the tool ON the boa's screen (a child, not a puzzle veteran). d. Not copied:
the dark, death, legs cut, the boy, the spider.
**The tribute rule: PASS.** Series: three (Mario, Oddworld, Limbo). Eras: 1983, 1997, 2010 (one post-2000: Limbo; post-2005 too). A pre-1990 classic: Mario Bros.; lesser-known: Abe's
Oddysee and Limbo; famous few: one at most (Mario Bros. is Mario's family; PICKS counts none). Our own idea: stillness that hid him becomes the move that beats a hunter. What players loved:
quoted for each (a). Not Pitfall!, Donkey Kong, Crash or Donkey Kong Jr. (1-6 to 1-8's leads).
## Nature check (left for the story keeper)
Boa constrictors "are ambush predators, so they often lie in wait for an appropriate prey to come along" and "can sense heat via cells in their lips" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boa_constrictor):
the posted boa and "a cool, still sloth" are real. Harpy eagles live mostly on sloths, and "Additional prey items reported include reptiles such as iguanas, tegus, snakes" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harpy_eagle):
a boa wary of the harpy is fair; a roost that scares it off is storybook, and so is the boa's fall into the river (boas do swim). Brown-throated sloths' fur "often supports algae growth,
sometimes giving them a greenish tint" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown-throated_sloth): why a still sloth reads as moss.
