**The pads, measured from code first, and the hop FLOWN** (origin/t-int-9 @ 1c7f6b954). A sinking pad is a beat brick 'gone' (beatBrick.ts:11) whose count runs only while he stands on it
and runs back when he leaves (ride.ts:9, :100-112); once sunk it stays down 0.6 s after he leaves (RIDE.padRise, ride.ts:22-23), then rises through its warning. The same brick on the stage
clock sinks on a beat by itself; Easy stretches every brick on a clock together (beatBrick.ts:4). Not wired: nothing calls newPad / stepPad outside ride.ts. Lily art exists (drawLily,
riverArt.ts:119-120). **Flown** (reference/specs/sims/hopAir.ts, the real engine): a tap hop is 9 frames in the air (0.30 s), 83 px at a run, 30 px up; a held jump 20 frames (0.67 s),
184 px, 98 px up. So a child's pad-to-pad (land, decide, tap: about 0.2-0.4 s on the pad) needs a pad that holds 1.0 s: the **proposed counts** (data, flown by the bots before listing):
small pad 1.0 s (its shiver from 0.5 s), big pad 2.0 s (shiver from 1.2 s), pads one tap hop apart (under 83 px edge to edge) in the teach, up to a held jump (184 px) after; the pond's
beat row 3.0 s (up 1.8, shiver 0.6, under 0.6); Easy x1.5 on all of them (the kit's stretch).

## Tributes (the lead checks a/b/c before any build, D58; every quote from a page opened this session)
**Wonder Boy (Escape / Sega, arcade 1986; Matt's #8).** a. Clare Edgeley (Computer and Video Games) praised its "simple, addictive gameplay", "not as complicated but just as playable" as
Super Mario Bros. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wonder_Boy_(video_game)). b. "The player must remain aware of the vitality meter, which constantly runs down at a steady pace and can only be
refilled by collecting food throughout the level" (same page): the food is on the path, so the meter teaches itself. c. Each pad is a little meter that drains only while he stands on it
(ONE pad, never the whole level), the shiver is its bar, and the petals sit on the pads, where he must not linger. d. Not copied: the meter bar, the skateboard, the hatchet, eggs, the caveman.
**Super Mario Bros. 3 (Nintendo, 1988; Matt's #15; the one famous game).** a. Mean Machines' Matt Regan: a "truly brilliant game" that tested the player's "brains and reflexes", its graphics
"incredibly varied" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Mario_Bros._3). b. Its donut lifts shake when stood on and drop soon after [S: mechanic only; no page I could open describes them].
Its levels set a pattern, then break it. c. Beats 1-4 set the pattern (his weight sinks a pad); the twist breaks it (the pond's own beat sinks a row, weight or not). d. Not copied: donuts,
power-ups, Mario, the look.
**Shovel Knight (Yacht Club Games, 2014; Matt's #82: "8-bit look, DuckTales-style pogo"; the lesser-known, post-2000 pick).** a. "The checkpoint system was praised": GameSpot and Shacknews
"wrote that the amount of checkpoints made the game fair and encouraged them to keep playing", and "As an optional challenge, players have the choice of destroying checkpoints to be awarded
treasure, at the risk of being sent back" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shovel_Knight). b. The risk is the player's own: IGN found the checkpoints "rewarded cautious players though it did not
punish reckless ones" (same page). c. The crown's extra petals sit on the quickest pads, off the safe line: treasure for the brave, never needed for the goal, and a flag before each pad
field so a greedy miss costs a walk, not the level. Its pogo ("bounce upon enemies and objects", same page) is the hop-hop-hop of beat 2. d. Not copied: breaking flags (a NEW piece:
PLATFORMER-100.md lists it for later), the shovel, the knight, the 8-bit look.
**Not Frogger** (2-1's tribute): its diving turtles sink on their own clock ("sinking while on top of a diving turtle", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frogger); ours sink only under him and
always come back, and the one row that keeps its own time (beat 6) breathes in plain sight.
**The tribute rule: PASS.** Series: three (Wonder Boy, Mario, Shovel Knight). Eras: 1986, 1988, 2014 (post-2000: Shovel Knight). Pre-1990 classic: Wonder Boy and SMB3; lesser-known: Wonder
Boy, Shovel Knight; famous few: one (SMB3). Our own idea: a pond that breathes with him, and the giant lily where a sloth may finally stand still. What players loved: quoted for each (a).
The [S] line (SMB3's donut lifts) is a mechanic fact only, as the packet allows.
## Nature check (left for the story keeper)
Victoria amazonica's leaves reach "up to 3 m in diameter", float on a stalk 7-8 m long, and "are enormously buoyant if the weight is distributed evenly"; one leaf "bore the 'unprecedented'
weight of 250 lb"; its flowers "can grow up to 40 cm in diameter" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_amazonica): the crown lily holding a sloth is real. Small lily pads that sink
under him and rise again are storybook (real ones would just sink). Herons, fish rings and frogs on pads: real pond life.
