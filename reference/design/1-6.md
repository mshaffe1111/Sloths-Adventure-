## Tributes (the lead checks a/b/c before any build, D58; sources carried from lists A-C)
**Pitfall! (Activision, 1982).** a. A vine swings on its own over a pit; you press as it comes overhead, ride, let go at the far point: one clean decision, the swing free, the timing yours (https://vgpavilion.com/mags/1982/12/joystik/pitfall; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitfall!). b. The safe tool came first: the vine over the gators before gators without one (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitfall!).
c. Beat 1: the edge press waits for the liana, moss under it; tap to catch, tap on the glow; thorns only at beat 4. d. Not copied: Harry, pits, crocodiles, the yell, the look.
**Donkey Kong Jr. (Nintendo, 1982).** a. Snapjaws travel the vines: blue ones run down and fall off the end, red ones patrol both ways; loved because the danger has a readable path and you always have a move (https://mariowiki.com/Snapjaw; https://www.mariowiki.com/Vine_Scene; https://arcadesushi.com/tribute-donkey-kong-jr/). b. The first round brings the simple passers first, a few at a time, the two-way ones later (https://www.mariowiki.com/Vine_Scene).
c. Beat 6 a snake only passes; 7 one comes down HIS liana and pushes him low; 11 they rise both ways; thumb: up, down, let go, nothing new. d. Not copied: Junior, Mario, the cage, Snapjaws, fruit-drop.
**Donkey Kong Country (Rare, 1994), Ropey Rampage.** a. Ropes swing on their own and never stop; the rope keeps time, you pick the moment; a level built on one mechanic, fleshed out step by step (https://www.mariowiki.com/Ropey_Rampage; https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Donkey_Kong_Country/Ropey_Rampage). b. Single ropes before chains over gaps (first rope's exact place not confirmed by any source).
c. Matt's own self-swing (STAGE-RULES.md:380-382); 1-6 adds the grip as the one dial a thumb turns. d. Not copied: the Kongs, barrels, Kremlings, the storm, the music, the look.
**Jungle Hunt (Taito, 1982).** a. Vine to vine, pressing when two come close; the distances vary so the timing keeps changing; some vines carry a creature (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungle_Hunt; https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Jungle_Hunt). b. Near regular gaps first, then varied, then a creature on a vine.
c. Beat 4's SHORT, SHORT, LONG and the finale's mix: one tap to catch, one on the glow, a slide down only when the gap is LONG; the creature is our vine snake. d. Not copied: the explorer, the gorilla, the scenes, the look; no people in World 1.
## Nature check (left for the story keeper)
The brown vine snake (Oxybelis aeneus) lives from Mexico through Central America into South America, is thin and vine-coloured, hangs still and is taken for a vine (lists A-C:
https://reptilesofaz.org/?p=257). Shy, slow sliding and the startle-drop are storybook. Lianas off a giant fig over a creek gorge fit a Central/South American canopy.
