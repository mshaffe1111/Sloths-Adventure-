**One idea:** "The river keeps time: every snap stone bites on four, so hop on one-two-three and be off before four." A player says: "the one where the stones bite on four." **The hook**
(B): a beat you can SEE: each stone's jaw opens a notch on one, two, three and claps shut on four (a muted phone reads it; the clack is extra). The snappers keep the SAME count: bonk one's
shell right after it snaps and it ducks off downstream, huffy, never hurt. 2-2's pads LEAVE him; 2-5's stones STAY and bite (he must leave them). **The 12+ bite** (A): a WAVE, each stone a
count after the one before, so he hops right behind the snap.
**The clock and the hop, FLOWN** (rule 24; `npx tsx reference/specs/sims/snapClock.ts normal|easy` on origin/main 6e7b85f8; out/snapClock-normal.txt, -easy.txt). Pokey is the real engine
(realPlay.ts on '2-5') with the game's own InputEngine (a tap's 80 ms minimum hold, as specs/sims/flight.ts); a stone's bite each frame is the game's own beatBrick.ts brickNow('bites') of
clock.ts clockAt (a snap stone is not in play yet, padPiece.ts plays 'gone' only, so the bite is read from the functions the piece will call, along his real path). The clock is
beatBrick.test.ts's jaw row: count 0.75 s; off 1.75 s, warn 0.5 s (the jaw creaks wide: the tell), on 0.75 s (shut: it holds him and hurts); a 3.0 s bar; Easy x1.5 (4.5 s).

  moving only on the beat, the dungeon.
