## Tributes (the lead checks a/b/c before any build, D58; sources carried from lists A-C, search results only: page fetches were blocked for them)
**Red Light, Green Light / Statues (the playground game; Matt's #1, MAP-REVISIONS.md:138).** a. The leader turns away and everyone creeps; on "red light" anyone seen moving goes back: the held breath between running and freezing (https://officialgamerules.org/game-rules/red-light-green-light/; https://folklore.usc.edu/childrens-game-american/; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statues_(game)). b. It teaches itself in one round: the first turn catches the greedy runners; variants soften the cost (folklore.usc.edu).
c. The screech is the turn with a 2.0 s warn; the first pass over each new stretch is the practice round; the thumb lifts and the moss comes in 0.5 s; caught costs a heart only after the free pass; the perched eagle (S) is the game at its purest: it looks, he freezes. d. Not copied: the chant, a human "it", Squid Game's doll and stakes.
**Metal Gear (Konami, MSX2, 1987).** a. Being seen is the one danger: guards see in a line, and the box hides Snake only while he is still (moving under it in sight gets him caught); one readable rule makes every room a puzzle (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal_Gear_(video_game); https://kotaku.com/metal-gear-the-kotaku-retrospective-1828740753; https://gamingpastime.com/metal-gear-msx-review/).
b. Rule first, tool second: early floors teach sight lines with guards alone; the box comes later as the answer to a room of cameras (https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Metal_Gear/B1_Floor_1; https://retronauts.com/article/445/the-anatomy-of-metal-gear-part-1-on-site-procurement). c. Our bush is the box, taught first and safe (World 1 is for children too); the sight is a shadow on the ground, never a hidden cone; the thumb does nothing to hide, and touching it in the sweep shakes the leaves (beat 2). d. Not copied: soldiers, guns, boxes, alerts, the "!", codecs, MGS3's camo % gauge, the look.
**Super Mario Bros. 3 (Nintendo, 1988), Boo Diddly.** a. A ghost that freezes while Mario faces it and creeps when he turns: a danger controlled by a look, a tiny rhythm game (https://www.mariowiki.com/Boo_Diddly). b. Met in close quarters with room to find the rule by turning (same source). c. Turned the sloth's way: the hunter moves, Pokey freezes, and both tells show (screech and shadow; moss on Pokey). d. Not copied: ghosts, faces, any Mario name, art or sound.
## Nature check (left for the story keeper)
The harpy eagle (Harpia harpyja) lives in Central and South America and sloths are most of its prey (one review: 79%) (https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Harpy_eagle; https://www.activewild.com/harpy-eagle-facts/); real sloths survive by stillness and the algae in their fur (the moss tint is real). "It only sees what moves" is storybook (no source found). Spanish moss (Tillandsia usneoides) is native from the southern US through Central and South America (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_moss); a tree-fall gap is a real opening in a rainforest canopy.
