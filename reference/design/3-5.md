**One idea:** "The old thorn vines are asleep: a hard landing or a punch wakes the ones that hear it, so go down the quiet way (climb, hang, land on moss), and freeze if one wakes." A
player says: "the one where you have to be quiet past the sleeping vines." **The hook:** after 3-3, Pokey KNOWS noise carries; now his own noise is the danger. The slow way is the safe way:
every big drop a hurried player takes wakes a vine, every root a patient sloth climbs down keeps them snoring. The tension is held breath, not reflex. **The turn (A's twist):** a pair of
light sleepers that never both sleep long enough to pass; he wakes them ON PURPOSE from out of reach, freezes while they look, and walks past while they sleep deep: noise as a tool again,
3-3's lesson coming back. **The teach:** 3-5 teaches NOISE AS THE DANGER (3-3 used it as a CALL; one new thing a stage).

## Tributes (each opened by the pitcher who cites it; D58)
- **Mark of the Ninja** (Klei 2012; via PITCH-33A). a. Loved: Nels Anderson (Game Developer, "five stealth design rules"): the rings show "how far a sound such as a smashing light will travel
  before you destroy it", to "make the systems all very, very understandable". b. The noise is drawn from the first room. c. Ours: every loud landing shows its real radius (as a painted
  puff) and each vine it reaches opens an eye. d. Not copied: killing, the dark-vision UI.
- **Metal Gear** (Konami 1987; via PITCH-33B). a. Loved: Kojima, a "combat game without fighting ... Thus the idea of a 'hiding' game was born" (Game Developer, GDC). b. "an emphasis on
  evasion similar to Pac-Man" (Wikipedia). c. Ours: the hero who wins by not being noticed at all. d. Not copied: guns, alarms.
- **Metroid Dread** (2021; via PITCH-33B). a. Loved: "Dread captures a sense of uncertainty through its world structure and sound design" (GamesRadar). b. "Make a noise and a ring around
  Samus' waist appears ... It's coming" (GamesRadar preview): the noise is SHOWN. c. Ours: a child sees why a vine woke. d. Not copied: the chase, the instant death.
- **Pitfall!** (Activision 1982; via PITCH-33A, for its rhythm). a. Loved: one of the first running-and-jumping multi-screen games (David Crane, Game Developer's Replay interview).
  b. The crocodiles' jaws open and shut on a fixed beat [S]. c. Ours: the light sleepers' readable beat, and the joke of lining it up yourself. d. Not copied: the heads as stepping stones.
**Different:** 1-7 hid from eyes (a bush); 3-2 hid from eyes (the dark); 3-5 hides from EARS, and the sleepers cannot move: the danger is what he does, not where they walk. The hand build's
vines woke on a CLOCK and the answer was a bell or a pebble (protoStage.ts:2636-2660); these sleep until HE is loud, and the answer is how he moves. 3-8 reuses ENG-VINE afterwards.

big looks painted: the radius must read as a world thing (a puff of moss dust and spores to the radius), and the lead should rule that it is not a UI ring before ENG-NOISE draws it.
