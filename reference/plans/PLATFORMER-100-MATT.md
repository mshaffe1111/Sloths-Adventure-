# Matt's 100 side-scrollers, verbatim (Matt, 11-14 2:21 PM ET: "Took me a few tries to get everything right, but I think this is a pretty good list")
Use with reference/plans/PLATFORMER-100.md (the pick list) and INSPIRED-BY.md. Each: why people loved it / game-defining influence.

## Block 1: The Pioneers & The 8-Bit Foundation (Atari 2600, Arcade, NES, Master System)
1. Pitfall! (Atari 2600, 1982): unprecedented sense of vast, jungle-spanning adventure and treasure-hunting on primitive hardware / pioneered the multi-screen horizontal side-scrolling platformer template, establishing hazards, swinging vines, and score-chasing exploration.
2. Jungle Hunt (Arcade, 1983): the thrill of leaping over obstacles and swinging across vines with arcade joystick precision / popularized continuous scrolling movement and mixed distinct gameplay styles (swimming, running, vine-swinging) into one side-scrolling framework.
3. Pac-Land (Arcade, 1984): transforming a maze character into a running adventurer traversing scrolling maps to get home to his family / widely credited as the conceptual template that paved the way for dedicated side-scrolling platformers like Super Mario Bros.
4. Kung-Fu Master (Arcade, 1985): relentless martial arts action against waves of unique floor-by-floor enemies and towering bosses / laid the foundational blueprint for the 2D side-scrolling beat 'em up and action genres.
5. Super Mario Bros. (NES, 1985): perfect physics, magical power-ups like the Super Mushroom, a joyful quest across wildly inventive worlds / pulled home consoles out of the 1983 crash; the gold standard for momentum-based platforming level design.
6. Ghosts 'n Goblins (Arcade/NES, 1985): dark gothic atmosphere, weapon variety, the badge of honor of beating its brutal difficulty / defined punishing, skill-based arcade action platformers demanding mastery of enemy placement.
7. Castlevania (NES, 1986): universal-monster horror aesthetic, whip combat, iconic stairs-and-candle exploration / cemented dark fantasy action-platforming rooted in deliberate, strategic movement.
8. Wonder Boy (Arcade/SMS, 1986): vibrant, fast skateboarding action with a health-draining countdown that kept players moving forward / combined arcade momentum with item management.
9. Metroid (NES, 1986): isolating alien atmosphere and the thrill of power-ups (Morph Ball, Varia Suit) that unlocked blocked paths / pioneered the interconnected "Metroidvania" map, item-gated exploration over linear levels.
10. The Legend of Zelda: The Adventure of Link (NES, 1987), side-scrolling sections: intense side-view sword combat and magic / proved adventure franchises could integrate precise 2D side-view melee and RPG mechanics.
11. Bionic Commando (Arcade/NES, 1987): swinging across bottomless pits with a grappling hook instead of a jump button / replaced the standard jump with directional momentum-based swinging.
12. Contra (Arcade/NES, 1987): over-the-top co-op alien shooting, spread guns, the Konami Code / the pinnacle of 2D run-and-gun, frantic action with tight two-player dynamics.
13. Mega Man (NES, 1987): defeat bosses and take their powers for later stages / non-linear stage select and rock-paper-scissors boss weaknesses.
14. Ninja Gaiden (NES, 1988): cinematic "Tecmo Theater" cutscenes, wall-climbing acrobatics, blistering ninja action / narrative cinematic storytelling woven into demanding side-scrolling play.
15. Super Mario Bros. 3 (NES, 1988): the overworld map, suit power-ups, hidden secrets, peerless level variety / sequels could redefine creative boundaries through mechanical inventiveness.
16. Mega Man 2 (NES, 1988): flawless robot master lineup, iconic chiptunes, refined tuning / the quintessential standard of 8-bit balance, enemy design and presentation.
17. Ghouls 'n Ghosts (Genesis/Arcade, 1988): gorgeous sprite work, armor-shredding combat challenge / refined the high-difficulty loop, added up/down mid-air attacks.
18. Strider (Arcade, 1989): high-speed futuristic ninja acrobatics, massive bosses, cinematic backdrops / pioneered fast high-mobility hack-and-slash side-scrolling.
19. Wonder Boy III: The Dragon's Trap (SMS, 1989): transforming into animal forms (Mouse-Man, Piranha-Man, Hawk-Man) with unique abilities / bridged linear platforming with open exploration, shops and permanent transformation.
20. Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles (Arcade, 1989): four-player co-op, pizza health, fighting foot soldiers with friends / popularized arcade beat 'em ups for mainstream audiences.
21. DuckTales (NES, 1989): Scrooge's cane pogo to bounce on enemies and find treasure / proved licensed platformers could be legendary; brilliant mechanics, immortal soundtrack.
22. Castlevania III: Dracula's Curse (NES, 1989): branching paths, multiple playable characters, non-linear routing / replay value through character choice and structural pathing.
23. Super Mario Land (Game Boy, 1989): portable Mario with quirky shooting-stage diversions / complex 2D design miniaturized for handhelds.
24. Shadow Dancer (Arcade, 1989): fighting with a loyal attack-dog companion / companion-character mechanics in side-scrolling action.
25. Mega Man 3 (NES, 1990): Rush the robot dog and sliding / the slide and utility movement items became core DNA.

## Block 2: The 16-Bit Golden Age (SNES, Genesis, TurboGrafx-16)
26. Castle of Illusion (Genesis, 1990): fairy-tale aesthetics, bounce attack / smooth parallax and whimsical animation.
27. Super Mario World (SNES, 1990): Yoshi, secret exits, fluid momentum, a map packed with secrets / the blueprint for 2D platform perfection; save files and hidden goal loops as staples.
28. Sonic the Hedgehog (Genesis, 1991): speed, loops, pinball physics, attitude / velocity and momentum over slow careful jumping.
29. Super Castlevania IV (SNES, 1991): 360-degree whip, Mode 7 rotating rooms, haunting music / redefined whip mechanics and atmospheric hardware use.
30. Ninja Gaiden Shadow (GB, 1991): bite-sized portable ninja stages / fast action on constrained portable screens.
31. Streets of Rage 2 (Genesis, 1992): Koshiro's soundtrack, tight combat, co-op brawling / the zenith of 16-bit beat 'em ups.
32. Sonic the Hedgehog 2 (Genesis, 1992): Tails, Spin Dash, split-screen, pseudo-3D bonus stages / the Spin Dash as essential momentum mechanic.
33. Super Mario Kart (SNES, 1992): multiplayer combat racing with Mode 7 / pioneered kart racing.
34. Contra III: The Alien Wars (SNES, 1992): dual weapons, overhead Mode 7 stages, cinematic explosions / an intense co-op sensory assault on 16-bit hardware.
35. Mega Man X (SNES, 1993): dash, wall-jump, hidden armor capsules, darker tone / modernized action-platforming mobility.
36. Aladdin (Genesis/SNES, 1993): Disney animators' hand-drawn animation, an interactive cartoon / elevated sprite animation standards.
37. The Lion King (SNES/Genesis, 1993): Simba's cinematic journey, iconic music and art / high visual benchmark; famous difficulty spikes (the wildebeest stampede).
38. Gunstar Heroes (Genesis, 1993): weapon combinations, shooting chaos, sprite scaling / run-and-gun with deep customization.
39. Donkey Kong Country (SNES, 1994): pre-rendered 3D look, bass-heavy music, barrel-blasting co-op / pre-rendered CGI depth on 16-bit hardware.
40. Super Metroid (SNES, 1994): environmental storytelling, isolation on Zebes, perfect physics, wall-jumping / solidified the modern Metroidvania rules.
41. Earthworm Jim (Genesis/SNES, 1994): surreal cartoon humor, whip combat, bizarre levels / quirky western animation and eccentric puzzle-platforming.
42. Mega Man X2 (SNES, 1994): armor parts, bike riding, secret routes / chip upgrades and secret boss sub-plots.
43. Sonic & Knuckles (Genesis, 1994): lock-on cartridges to play as Knuckles / hardware innovation creating unified campaigns.
44. Yoshi's Island (SNES, 1995): crayon-and-pastel look, egg throwing, baby Mario's crying alerts / a distinct visual identity, projectile aiming and health-management innovation.
45. Donkey Kong Country 2 (SNES, 1995): pirate themes, David Wise's score, character-swapping / rhythmic, demanding secrets and precision.
46. Rayman (PS1/Saturn/Jaguar, 1995): vector art, whimsical worlds, limb-detaching powers / kept 2D sprite platforming alive into the 3D era.
47. Mega Man X3 (SNES, 1995): Zero in bursts, chip sets and ride armor / character versatility.
48. Castlevania: Rondo of Blood (PC Engine, 1993): CD audio, branching routes, Richter's whip / precursor to a celebrated masterpiece.
49. Demon's Crest (SNES, 1994): gothic action as Firebrand, hovering, elemental collection / action-platforming with light RPG exploration and flight.
50. Pulseman (Mega Drive, 1994): electric ball-dashing by Game Freak / Sonic-style velocity with vector-charging physics.

## Block 3: The Metroidvania Era & Handheld Renaissance (PS1, GBA, DS, PSP)
51. Castlevania: Symphony of the Night (PS1, 1997): RPG levels, loot, gothic art, the inverted castle / co-invented "Metroidvania".
52. Oddworld: Abe's Oddysee (PS1, 1997): cinematic puzzle-platforming with spoken commands ("Hello! Follow me!") / dynamic AI followers and environmental storytelling.
53. Klonoa: Door to Phantomile (PS1, 1997): emotional story, 2.5D, grabbing enemies with wind rings / mastered 2.5D side-scrolling.
54. Mega Man X4 (PS1/Saturn, 1997): playable X and Zero, anime cutscenes / sprite mascots into 32-bit with divergent playstyles.
55. Strider 2 (Arcade/PS1, 1999): 2.5D hack-and-slash thrills / 3D camera sweeps around 2D planes.
56. Castlevania: Circle of the Moon (GBA, 2001): the DSS card system mixing elemental powers / Metroidvania on handhelds.
57. Castlevania: Aria of Sorrow (GBA, 2003): capture enemy souls for their abilities / monster-collection integrated into exploration.
58. Metroid: Zero Mission (GBA, 2004): reimagined NES classic, fluid controls, stealth epilogue / how classics are rebuilt with modern pacing.
59. Metroid Fusion (GBA, 2002): horror sci-fi vs the mimetic X-Parasites / directed, suspenseful, narrative-driven Metroidvania.
60. Castlevania: Dawn of Sorrow (DS, 2005): touch-screen seal drawing, soul trading / dual screens for map and touch menus.
61. Mega Man Zero 3 (GBA, 2004): cyber-elf upgrades, rank systems, Zero's combat / a tight responsive mastery loop.
62. Mega Man ZX (DS, 2006): interconnected world, Bio-Metal transformations / boss powers with open-world backtracking and hubs.
63. New Super Mario Bros. (DS, 2006): classic 2D Mario with 3D models, minigames / revived the 2D style commercially.
64. Wario Land 4 (GBA, 2001): transformations (flattened, swollen Wario), frantic escape timers back to the start / puzzle-room exploration and escape sprints.
65. Muramasa: The Demon Blade (Wii, 2009): hand-drawn Japanese folklore art, fast swordplay, blade crafting / a pinnacle of hand-painted 2D.
66. Sonic Rush (DS, 2006): dual-screen momentum, Blaze, trick-jump meters / 2D speed with touch boosting.
67. Castlevania: Order of Ecclesia (DS, 2008): glyph combat, outdoor stages and hubs / tactical customization, structural shift.
68. Kirby Super Star Ultra (DS, 2008): distinct mini-games, co-op helpers, sub-adventures / compilation variety and joyful accessibility.
69. Contra 4 (DS, 2007): run-and-gun across both screens / vertical play across dual screens.
70. Donkey Kong Country Returns (Wii, 2010): silhouette stages, minecarts, demanding platforming / modern physics revival.
71. Rayman Origins (2011): hand-drawn UbiArt visuals, co-op chaos, momentum / HD hand-drawn 2D viable.
72. Kirby's Return to Dream Land (Wii, 2011): drop-in co-op, copy abilities / modern co-op design.
73. New Super Mario Bros. Wii (2009): four-player co-op chaos / couch co-op platforming at scale.
74. Sonic Generations, 2D acts (2011): classic stages in HD with classic and modern physics / honoring classic design with modern polish.
75. Bit.Trip Runner (2010): rhythm-based auto-running, every jump and slide on the beat / the rhythm-platforming subgenre.

## Block 4: The Indie Renaissance & Modern Era
76. Braid (2008): painterly look, time-manipulation puzzles, ambiguous story / sparked the indie explosion.
77. Super Meat Boy (2010): tight controls, instant respawns, precision courses / instant trial-and-error made satisfying.
78. Limbo (2010): monochrome silhouettes, eerie physics puzzles, tension / mood-driven cinematic puzzle-platformers.
79. Terraria (2011): 2D sandbox crafting, bosses, mining, building with friends / open-world survival in 2D side view.
80. Rayman Legends (2013): musical rhythm levels ("Castle Rock"), fluid art, co-op / rhythm, momentum and co-op blended.
81. Guacamelee! (2013): lucha moves in traversal, dimension shifting / beat 'em up combos inside Metroidvania exploration.
82. Shovel Knight (2014): 8-bit look, DuckTales-style pogo, modular campaigns / the retro revival with modern design.
83. DKC: Tropical Freeze (2014): David Wise score, animal buddies, momentum level variety / one of the finest modern platformers, flawless pacing.
84. Ori and the Blind Forest (2015): hand-painted storybook art, orchestral score, Bash / launch off projectiles for acrobatic momentum.
85. Undertale (2015), battle box / traversal: subversive choices, bullet-hell and pacifism / redefined indie storytelling.
86. Inside (2016): dystopian atmosphere, seamless puzzles, shocking ending / perfected cinematic puzzle-platforming, zero interface text.
87. Cuphead (2017): 1930s rubber-hose animation, punishing boss battles / hand-drawn animation to new heights.
88. Hollow Knight (2017): haunting Hallownest, tight combat, deep exploration / the modern Metroidvania gold standard.
89. Celeste (2018): tight air-dash, a moving mental-health story, Assist Mode / precision platforming with an empathetic story.
90. Dead Cells (2018): fluid combat, rogue-lite randomization, every run fresh / "Roguevania".
91. Blasphemous (2019): dark gothic pixel art, brutal executions / a lore-rich nightmare Metroidvania.
92. Ori and the Will of the Wisps (2020): grapple, epic boss chases, emotional ending / perfected the Metroidvania movement suite.
93. Katana Zero (2019): one-hit neon action, time manipulation, cinematic swordplay / action plus rewind puzzles.
94. Skul: The Hero Slayer (2021): swapping skulls for new move sets / class and movement tied to equipment swaps.
95. Metroid Dread (2021): modern speed, E.M.M.I. stealth pursuits / fast controls with survival-horror tension.
96. TMNT: Shredder's Revenge (2022): pixel art, six-player co-op / the arcade beat 'em up comeback.
97. Pizza Tower (2023): Wario Land-inspired momentum speed, chaotic cartoon art / manic momentum speedrunning.
98. Sea of Stars (2023), traversal: modern pixel art, swimming and climbing in exploration / platform traversal inside a turn-based RPG.
99. Prince of Persia: The Lost Crown (2024): combat depth, time-power navigation puzzles / a legendary franchise reinvented as a Metroidvania.
100. Super Mario Bros. Wonder (2023): Wonder Flowers that warp reality, elephant transformations, badges, surprises / proved 2D platformers can still surprise after decades.
