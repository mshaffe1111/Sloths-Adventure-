This is the one hand-off document for the polish work: the graded
review, what Build 1 changed, what comes next, and the decisions only
Matt can make. Each issue below has its file and line, the fix and any
art needed, so it can be picked up without a conversation.

Where the game stands: C against big-studio mobile platformers and B−
against independent ones. For a first game that's a strong place to
stand. The art direction, World 6 on the new generator, the wood-kit
menus and the idea of Dot are already at indie-A level. What pulls the
grade down is finish, not vision: controls that misread a few percent of
the time, backdrops blurred and outlined by an upscale, effects that are
loud and pingy, story scenes softer than the gameplay, and older stages
that break rules the new generator already follows. Matt's target is A+
against indies and B or better against big studios, ideally an A. Indie
A+ is reachable; B+ against big studios is realistic for Worlds 1-6, and
an A is possible with the hardest gaps closed (Path to the target).

What the testers show: the control-test sheet is real people from Matt's
tester link and the Facebook app, and everyone plays through now. Matt,
Jen and Steph are the experienced players. Sally had never played a
game; she finishes stages on Easy and has finished some on Normal, which
is the best evidence that the on-ramp works.

What's being worked on elsewhere: Worlds 7-10 and bosses 6-10 are being
polished by the team right now, so they're noted, not graded. The new
stage-piece generator shows from 5-7 and in all of World 6, and it's the
reference look for the older worlds.

Reference games for the two grades. Against big studios (AAA): Super
Mario Run and Rayman Adventures / Rayman Jungle Run. Against
independents: Leo's Fortune, Oddmar and Alto's Adventure. A means it
holds up next to the best of that group; B is solid and shippable in

Priorities: P1 is noticed in the first minutes or breaks play; P2 is
noticeable once you play a while or in one area; P3 is finish. Effort: S

Visuals and art: C+ against AAA, B against indies

The verdict: the art direction is the game's best asset. The painted
kits for Worlds 1-7 share one soft, hand-painted style, Pokey and every
enemy are painted and crisp, and World 6 on the new generator looks like
a real storybook game. What drags it down is mostly pipeline, not
painting: the backdrops are blurred by an upscale-on-an-upscale and
carry a dark cut-out ring, the soil seams and repeats, and the things
that matter most to play (water, flags, the exit door, ropes, pickups,
boss attacks) are still drawn in code. Rayman Adventures and Alto's
Adventure win on exactly those things: clean edges, no visible tiling,
and every interactive object painted. Most of this is coder days, not
repainting.

Grades inside: backdrops C+, ground and soil C, hero and enemies B+,
hazards and props C−, World 6 / new generator A−.

VI-1. A dark outline around every painted backdrop strip (P1, M, no new
art). Where: the near and mid strips (src/assets/**/w*-near.webp,
w*-mid.webp), loaded by paintedBackdrops.ts and drawn by scenery.ts
drawStrip (1662-1695). The outer 3 px of every leaf and trunk is 40-61%
as bright as the paint just inside (W1 near; W2, W4, W6, W7, W10 measure
0.38-0.55; W5 near 0.32-0.44). Cause, from git: commit 18bedcc upscaled
the colour 4x with the transparent areas filled black
(tools/art/upscale/up.py) and the alpha separately, so black smeared
into every edge. This is also the "jagged dark edges" on World 1's trees
(was #15). It reads as a cheap sticker outline. Fix, all scripted: start
from the originals at 18bedcc^; un-premultiply and bleed the edge colour
16 px outward into the transparent area (a normalised Gaussian fill)
before upscaling; then erode the alpha 1 px and feather it 1-1.5 px;
protect features under 4 px wide (hair vines) from the erode. Optionally
add an even 1.6 px ink line at 0.42x the local colour so the edge looks
drawn on purpose. A working prototype for World 1 is in the scratchpad
(va/defringe2.py, with before and after images); edge brightness goes
from 0.40-0.75 to 0.98-1.0. About 1.5 days for every world.

VI-2. Backdrops are blurry in portrait (P1; 10 minutes of code, then
art). On a normal phone in portrait the strips are magnified 1.7-2.5x
(tablet 2.7-3.3x), and since the real brushwork is only 1024 px wide
(the 2048 files are AI 2x upscales), the actual detail is stretched
3.5-4.5x. Portrait shows only about 24% of a strip's width. The canvas
also never sets imageSmoothingQuality, so it's plain bilinear. Next to
the crisp Pokey it looks smeared. Fix: set ctx.imageSmoothingQuality =
'high' after each resize in engine.ts resizeCanvas (785-832). The real
fix is art: repaint the near and mid strips natively at 3072-4096 px
wide (far strips can stay 2048), on flat magenta, tiling left to right.
What Matt makes, per world: one near strip 4096x1736 and one mid strip
3072x1302, transparent after keying. Gemini prompt: "Wide horizontal
side-scrolling game background layer, hand-painted storybook rainforest
style, soft gouache and watercolor brushwork, [WORLD: e.g. sunny lowland
jungle with kapok trunks, hanging lianas, broad monstera leaves].
Foreground silhouette band only: trees, vines and leaves along the top
and sides, nothing in the middle, open sky area left empty. Paint on a
flat pure magenta (#FF00FF) background so it can be cut out cleanly. No
outline glow, no drop shadows, no text, no characters. The left edge and
right edge must line up so the image tiles seamlessly side by side.
Ultra-wide 2.36:1 aspect, highest resolution." Key it with VI-1's fixed
pipeline and VI-6's spill fix.

VI-3. Backdrops pop in after the stage starts (P1, S; was #16). Where:
stagePreload.ts:137 preloads the backdrop but never adds its URLs to the
list whenSheetsReady waits on, and readyToStart gives up after 900 ms.
Meanwhile scenery.ts waitingSky (397-409) draws a gradient stand-in, and
onPaintedReady swaps the painting in with no fade. The canopy strips
load on first use, a second pop. KEEP_WORLDS = 2 counts sets, not
worlds, so World 5's dusk and twilight sets are dropped early. Fix: add
the stage's backdrop and canopy URLs to the readyToStart list, allow up
to 2500 ms for backdrops, crossfade anything still late over 250-300 ms,
and make KEEP_WORLDS count worlds.

VI-4. Mirror seams and "inkblot" twins where strips repeat (P2, S).
Where: drawStrip draws every other copy mirrored. Edge columns are much
darker or lighter than the paint 10-30 px in (w7-near right edge 0.27x,
w2-near 0.56x, w3-mid 0.71x), and mirroring puts the same tree twice
facing itself. Fix: stop mirroring. Use the seamless() crossfade that
already exists unused in scripts/backdrop-strip.py (blend a 128-256 px
band from the right edge into the left) and tile without flipping.
Delete the mirror path; new replaces old.

VI-5. A white halo on the World 1 canopy strips (P2, in VI-1's batch).
w1c-mid and w1c-near were keyed off white: edges are 2.4x brighter than
the inside. Run the same decontaminate script.

VI-6. Magenta leaking on World 5 dusk and twilight (P2, 2 hours).
2.6-4.4% of edge pixels are pink on w5dusk1-near, w5dusk2-near,
w5dusk2-mid and w5twi1-mid. The keyer in backdrop-strip.py barely
suppresses spill. Fix: after keying, clamp red and blue to no more than
green plus a small margin near the key colour (or desaturate toward the
local foliage colour), then run VI-1.

VI-7. Grey seams in the soil, including on the new generator (P1, S for
the code part). Where: World 6's kit bank piece (397x359) drawn by
drawBankArt (protoArt.ts:441) has a grey stone slab in its bottom rows
(saturation 0.06-0.10 against 0.34 for the soil above), and its height
is fixed at 110, so it's drawn only 121 wide and repeats often. In 5-7 I
saw a vertical seam (about x 963 at 1024 wide), a horizontal seam (about
y 585) and a dark sliver at the far left. Fix now: crop the slab rows
out of the source rect. Long term: VI-8's soil kit.

VI-8. Ground tiles repeat and band (P1 for Worlds 6-7, P2 elsewhere; was
#14). Where: the kit fill piece (World 6: 397x176) drawn by
drawGroundFill (protoArt.ts:471) at 230 wide, repeating every 228 x 100
units with no flip, no variation and no darkening with depth. World 6's
tile has a lit grey bevel at rows 26-34 and dark bands top and bottom,
so a stripe shows every 100 units; World 7's top/bottom mismatch is the
worst (34.8). The soil is 45% of a portrait frame (feet sit at 0.55 of
screen height; 0.68 on short landscape screens), so it matters. Code fix
(about 1 day): crop the bevel and dark rows from the source rect;
alternate horizontal flips per tile; offset each row by half a tile;
hash between 2-3 variants; overlay one large, low-frequency darkening
pass (600-900-unit blobs at 8-12%); add a vertical gradient so the soil
is 35-55% darker by the bottom of the view (groundArt.ts already does
this for the code-drawn soil; the painted fill ignores it). Art, per
world: one seamless soil-and-roots texture tiling on both axes,
1024x1024, opaque, no lit lip, no border; 2-3 variants; a separate
transparent top-lip strip 1024x160 that tiles left to right; and a bank
piece without the stone slab. Gemini prompt: "Seamless tileable texture,
hand-painted storybook style matching a rainforest platformer, rich dark
soil cross-section with tangled tree roots, small pebbles and earthy
clumps, [WORLD palette]. Even lighting, no highlight band, no border, no
horizon, no grass on top. Must tile seamlessly in all four directions.
Square, 1024x1024." Lip: "Horizontal tileable strip, hand-painted
storybook rainforest style, mossy grass edge with small ferns and roots
hanging down, on a flat magenta background, left and right edges must
line up seamlessly, 1024x160." Optional design call: feet at 0.6 of the
screen instead of 0.55 shows less soil.

leaves / W3: dark blue night water with faint glow / W4 / W5], darker
toward the bottom, soft surface highlight line, on flat magenta
background, left and right edges must tile seamlessly, 1024x256." Code:
half a day to route it through drawBadWater.

Animation and game feel: C− against AAA, C+ against indies

The verdict: the bones are better than the grade. The per-move hitstop
and knockback data, coyote time, the jump buffer, variable jump height
and the camera (dead zone, look-ahead, portrait framing) are already at
a good indie standard, so leave them alone. What's missing is the layer
of small reactions that make Rayman and Oddmar feel alive: a beat when
you die, dust when you land, sparks that pop, a background that glides
instead of stutters, and a buzz in your hand. Several of these are
already in Build 1.

AN-1. Losing a life has no beat (P1, M; was #2, in Build 1). Instant
teleport under a white flash, no KO pose, and the Game Over card takes
taps on the frame it appears. Build 1 adds a 0.6-0.8 s beat with the KO
pose or splash, a dark iris, and a 0.5 s tap guard.

AN-2. The invulnerability blink is uneven at 30 fps (P2, S; was #13, in
Build 1). Build 1 blinks on whole frames at 0.3 alpha and gives the
Solar power-up its own glow.

AN-3. Pop-up text is constant and arcade-style (P1, M; was #5, in Build
1).

AN-4. The background stutters while you run (P1, 30 minutes). Where:
drawStrip (scenery.ts 1662-1695) and drawTile (about 1615) floor x to
whole world units. At 30 fps and 200 units/s the far layer sits still
two frames then jumps 2 device pixels, while the ground snaps to device
pixels, so the layers shudder against each other. Fix: snap to device
pixels, x_dev = Math.round(x * dpr * zoom) / (dpr * zoom), or drop the
floor and draw at fractional x (fine once VI-1 removes the dark edges
that would shimmer).

AN-5. No landing dust, a five-dot take-off puff and flat circle hit
sparks (P2, M; was #12). Where: engine.ts spawnJumpDust (about 1845,
2051), the take-off puff (6901-6915), hit particles (5448-5464), defeat
debris always green (3945). Fix: a soft dust puff for landings and
take-offs scaled by fall speed; a hit-spark sprite with a brief glow;
debris coloured per creature; one pooled particle system with a cap.
Small touches, so code-drawn is fine.

AN-6. Enemy hurt flash uses canvas filters (P2, M; was #10). About 20
sites set ctx.filter = 'brightness(2.2)' (creatures.ts 187, predators.ts
163, goldSnake.ts 191): costly on phones and silently missing where
filters aren't supported. Fix: one shared helper that draws a pre-baked
white silhouette, or fills source-atop over the sprite.

AN-7. Venom Fang's lunge is a straight, stiff tube (P2, M; was #9). Draw
the body along a curved S-path with a taper, or use painted lunge
frames.

AN-8. The Tarantula Queen's animation clips are unused (P2, S-M).
queen-anim.webp exists but queenAnimData only defines a URL. Hook it up
the way croc-anim and prowler-anim are. The Sky Sovereign has pose
sheets but no clips (P3).

AN-9. Haptics only on button presses, and none on iPhone (P2, S).
TouchController buzzes 15-35 ms for jump, punch, kick and mega, and
nothing for getting hurt, a hard landing, a boss slam, a checkpoint or a
clear. Add hurt 40 ms, hard land 20 ms, boss slam a 60 ms pattern,
checkpoint two short pulses, and a clear pattern, behind a setting. iOS
Safari ignores navigator.vibrate, so on iPhone the on-screen feedback in
CT-14 has to carry it.

AN-10. Screen shake is random jitter with no setting (P3, S; was #27).
engine.ts 5098-5129: use a smooth decaying shake that moves the backdrop
too, follow reduced motion, and add a setting.

AN-11. The Stage Cleared card doesn't move (P2, see UI-1 to UI-3): no
count-up, static confetti.

Controls: C against AAA, B− against indies

The verdict: the touch rules are clever and mostly work. Across about
3,800 clean, quick up-flicks from real players, 93% jumped, and Sally,
who had never played a game, now clears stages. Testers do finish. What
holds the grade down isn't that the controls fail; it's that they
misread a few percent of the time in ways a player can feel and can't
explain, and the misreads cluster on certain grips. Super Mario Run and
Rayman Jungle Run get an A by asking the thumb for almost nothing (one
tap, auto-run). Leo's Fortune and Oddmar ask for more, like this game
does, and earn their B+ by never misreading. That's the bar here: a
gesture game can reach an A only if a clear gesture always does what the
player meant.

Grades inside the category (from code reading, scripted tests in the
engine, and the tester touches): the gesture recogniser B−; vine and
trunk climbing C−; the Pad C−; tilt F, because it doesn't exist yet;
screen edges and the home bar D; feedback in Screen mode D; reading
input at 30 fps A−.

Matt's five control rules, checked

-   On a vine, straight up or down always climbs, and only a diagonal
    jumps off. Mostly true now. In the Oct 23-25 builds, 28 of Matt's
    near-vertical strokes leapt off; since Oct 26, only 3 have. Three
    holes remain: a climbing stroke 22-25° off vertical still leaps
    (CT-3), the second thumb's straight-up flick lets go of a vine
    (CT-5), and on the Pad, up leaps off a trunk (CT-2).

-   The controls suit every finger and grip. Not yet. Missed jumps (a
    clear up-flick that didn't jump) by player since Oct 26: Mike 15%
    (two thumbs, phone in one hand), yana 9.5%, Cheddarbob 8.8% (index
    finger on a small iPhone), Jane 6.6%, Steph 4.5%, Matt 2.6%, Sasa
    2.8%, Sally 1.1%, Jen 0%. Portrait misses 3.1% against 0.9% in
    landscape. The causes are CT-9 (short flicks), CT-7 (a resting thumb
    fires the mega move) and CT-15 (the palm rule eats taps).

-   Special controls only appear in special spots. True, with one leak:
    the carve-spot flag isn't cleared, so carving can carry into the
    next stage (CT-20).

-   Tilt and slide are both selectable. Not met. There is no tilt
    anywhere in the code. CT-29 is the full specification.

-   Left-handed players can mirror the pad. Met for the Pad (Settings,
    Pad side: Right or Left). Two gaps: the Pad defaults to the right
    for everyone (CT-19, a decision for Matt), and in Screen mode the
    two halves behave differently, so a left-hander's experience isn't a
    true mirror (CT-8).

What the tester touches show

The Sloths control tests sheet is real players from the tester link and
the Facebook app: 86 sessions, decoded in full. Everything since Oct 26
(when Dot's help arrived) counts most; Matt, Jen and Steph are the
experienced baseline and Sally the never-played baseline.

-   Jumps on the ground: 154 of 5,533 clear up-flicks (2.8%) didn't
    jump.

-   Running jumps that ended in a lost life were held a median 213 ms
    against 257 ms for all jumps. That's the signature of CT-1: lift the
    thumb early on a running jump and the jump comes up short.

-   Kicks: 254 of 347 came from the steering finger (the move fires
    about 150 ms in, the kick on lift), almost all Matt's. He kicks when
    he means to steer and flick.

-   Taps: 331 of 1,559 quick taps did nothing; 329 of those were one
    Easy player tapping Dot and the cards, so that is a learning issue,
    not a control fault.

-   Swing releases: 216, with 11 hurts and 3 lost lives within 2.5 s.
    Swings are fine.

-   Where lives went (Oct 26 on): Push the log 12 (all on Easy), the
    crown finish 7, falling rocks 5, flowers over the water 5, only the
    leaf 4, run and stop 4. Most hurts: keep moving 12, hide from the
    eagle 11, snapping turtles 9.

Issues, in fix order

CT-1. A one-thumb running jump loses 40% of its distance about half the
time (P1, 3-4 hours plus a test). Confirmed by script. Where:
src/game/engine.ts 1810-1815 and 1831. A one-thumb player flicks up and
lifts quickly. If the lift ("move none", touchGestures.ts:425) reaches
the engine before the next frame, ground braking (groundDec 3400 x 1/30
= 113 px/s) runs on the take-off frame before the jump code, so take-off
speed drops from 285 to 172 px/s, and the refined air rules never give
it back. Scripted result (check/c1.test.ts): thumb still on the glass,
the jump carries 170 px; thumb lifted first, 109 px. The safe
single-jump gap the stage builder uses is SAFE.gap1 = 140 px
(physics.ts:136), so identical flicks sometimes clear a gap and
sometimes fall in. The tester data fits: running jumps followed by a
lost life were held a median 213 ms against 257 ms overall. Fix: on the
frame a grounded jump fires, skip ground braking when dir is 0 (or
decide the jump before braking). In the recogniser add an intent lock:
while the steering finger is rising (at least 0.3 px/ms over the last 60
ms, rise at least 1.5x the sideways travel) and for 120 ms after a jump
press, hold the run direction, with no stop and no reverse, until the
rise ends or the finger lifts. Turn c1.test.ts into a regression test
that requires at least 165 px.

CT-2. Pad: you can't climb a liana, and pushing up again on a trunk
leaps off (P1, 3-4 hours). Confirmed by script. Where: PadController.tsx
40-63 sends pad up as a jump (triggerTouchUp; padControl.ts:27). On a
trunk every fresh up after the 0.3 s grab grace is "Exit C: leap off"
(engine.ts 1558-1578); on a liana it's the Tarzan leap after 0.25 s
(1621, 1692). A diagonal push only steers sideways, which then triggers
the sideways hop-off from CT-4. That's the exact opposite of Matt's vine
rule. Fix: when inputEngine.onVine is true and the input is the pad, pad
up and down call triggerTouchClimb(+1/-1) and never jump. Only the Jump
button, or a pad push at least 45 degrees over with 38 px or more of up,
leaps, carrying a lean of +1/-1. Do the same for keyboard and game
controller (Up climbs on a vine, Space or A leaps).

CT-3. Ordinary climbing strokes leap off a trunk or liana (P1, about 1
day with replay). Confirmed by script. Where: touchGestures.ts
vineSteer, 727-729. A leap needs a 34 px rise within 160 ms at 20-50
degrees, but finger history is trimmed to 150 ms (line 374), so the only
real speed floor is 0.23 px/ms, a gentle climb. Scripted: a 22 degree
stroke at 0.3 px/ms and a 25 degree stroke at 0.4 px/ms both climb and
then jump. Thumbs and index fingers naturally climb 15-30 degrees off
vertical; this is Jen's index-finger complaint. Fix: angle buckets with
hysteresis plus a fresh-flick test. Leap only when the rise, measured
from the finger's last low point (riseFrom, as the ground rule does),
began within 120 ms, runs at least 0.6 px/ms, sits at least 28 degrees
off vertical (22 if it's 1.0 px/ms or faster), and the finger wasn't
already climbing in the 100 ms before. Everything else climbs. Tune by
replaying the recorded tester touches.

CT-4. Stopping on a trunk overshoots about 90 px, then a thumb left
slightly sideways drops him off (P1, 2-3 hours). Confirmed by script.
Where: engine.ts 1500-1510 and 1580-1593. climbAssist is set to 0.45 at
the grab and never decays while up is held, so he climbs 0.45 s past
where the finger stopped (88 px in check/c4.test.ts). And any climbing
stroke leaves sideways steering on: even a 10 degree, 100 px stroke
reads move right, which only releases below dx 9.6 (touchGestures.ts
601-604), while climb goes to 0 within 16 px (738). So when the player
stops, right is still held with no up or down, and Exit D hops him off
after 0.2 s. The Pad does the same whenever dx > 14 and dy < 38. Players
feel it as "I stopped to wait for the snake and he fell off." Fix: let
climbAssist decay even while up is held, and zero it on an explicit
climb(0). On a trunk, hop-off needs a fresh sideways stroke (dx at least
40 px, at least 2x dy, 0.3 px/ms or faster, held 0.35 s), or drop Exit D
for touch entirely, since Matt's rule says only a diagonal leaves.
Re-centre the sideways anchor whenever a climb starts.

CT-5. The other thumb's straight-up flick lets go of a vine (P1, 1-2
hours, needs Matt's call). Where: the vertical flick in judge
(touchGestures.ts 763-778), the jump-help slide-up (814-821) and the
palm-flick path (397-406) never check vine() when a steering finger
exists; only a lone finger is covered (756). vineFollowUps.test.ts
311-321 locks this in. Two-thumb players naturally climb with the jump
thumb and drop off instead. Fix: when vine() is true, an action finger
moving within 20-25 degrees of vertical calls setClimb(+1/-1) and never
jumps. Matt's rule says climb, so the recommendation is climb and change
the test, but it's his decision.

CT-6. A back swipe from the screen edge can leave the game (P1 on
Android with gesture navigation, P2 on iPhone; 2-3 hours). Where:
nothing listens for back navigation (no pushState or popstate anywhere),
and the touch layer covers the screen to the edges
(TouchController.tsx:156). In landscape the left thumb rests at the
edge, so a run started there is Android's back gesture: the touch is
cancelled and the browser goes back, off the game. Safari's left-edge
swipe does the same when there's page history. On iPhone a flick
starting in the bottom 20 px or so is the home-bar gesture, which eats
the jump and pops Safari's toolbar back. Fix: on stage start call
history.pushState({sloth:1}, ''); on popstate, pause, show the pause
menu and push again. Teach "thumbs a finger's width off the edges" in
the coach, and keep the Pad's ghost pad clear of the edges.

CT-7. Resting the action thumb fires a mega, and its next flick is eaten
(P1 for two-thumb players, 1-2 hours). Confirmed by script. Where:
touchGestures.ts 845-853 fires mega after a second finger is held still
460 ms within 20 px and marks the finger used up; 745-747 then ignore it
until it lifts. Scripted: rest 540 ms, flick up 60 px, result mega and
no jump. Resting the jump thumb on the glass is the normal two-thumb
posture, so every rest throws a mega (heavy shake, 30% move speed,
facing locked 0.3 s, 1 s cooldown) and the jump that follows does
nothing. This is likely part of Mike's 15% misses. Fix: never use up a
finger on mega, so a later flick still jumps; raise the hold to 550 ms;
consider hold-then-release for mega.

CT-8. The two halves of the screen behave differently (P2; 10 minutes
for the first step, about a day for all of it). Where: splitAt 0.5
(touchGestures.ts 96, 346-349); the early drag at 60 ms / 18 px,
sideways only (826); decideMs 210 (836). The early drag is wrongly gated
on assistOn() (826), so with Jump help Off a run started on the right
half waits 210 ms. The camera leads 90 px the way Pokey faces
(cameraFollow.ts CAM.lookAhead), so an index finger kept on Pokey (Jen's
grip) steers instantly one way and lags the other, and one-handed
portrait players always land on the right half. Fix: remove the assistOn
gate now. Then make any lone first finger the steering finger at once,
anywhere; its lift already handles tap to punch and flick to kick
(415-447). Keep the wait-and-see rules only for a second finger.

CT-9. Short quick flicks on the ground do nothing, or punch (P1, 3-4
hours plus replay). Confirmed by script. Where: the thresholds differ by
finger. A lone right-half finger needs 41.4 px at 0.6 px/ms (judge,
768), a second finger 30 px (slideUpPx), the steering thumb 34 px, and
on the ground nothing is checked again at lift (moverFlickOnLift,
483-498, covers only vines and air). Under 22 px is a tap, which
punches. Scripted: a 26 or 36 px flick in 56 ms from a lone right-half
finger does nothing; 18 px from either finger punches with a 60-150 px/s
lunge; the steering thumb's 30 px flick just stops Pokey. Small-phone
and landscape thumbs flick 20-35 px, which matches the higher miss rates
for Mike, yana and Cheddarbob. Fix: one rule at lift for every finger.
If the last 150 ms rose 18 px or more at 0.4 px/ms or faster, and the
rise is at least 1.2x the sideways travel, it's a jump. A tap also needs
peak speed under 0.35 px/ms or a rise under 12 px. Bring ground jump
distance to about 26 px for every finger.

CT-10. A jump near a trunk grabs the trunk (P2, 2 hours). Where: any
jump press sets upIntent for 0.35 s (engine.ts:1484), and wantGrab
(1486) grabs any trunk within 54 px (1462); holding down in the air also
grabs. Fix: a ground jump grabs only if it's straight up (jumpLean 0) or
leans toward the trunk and isn't steering away. An air grab needs up
held or movement toward the trunk.

CT-11. The thumb's arc reverses the steering at take-off (P2). Direction
stops below dx 9.6 and reverses below -16, about 46 px of travel back
from a full push. Fixed by CT-1's intent lock; no separate work.

CT-12. Jump height jitters, and a jump right after a hit comes out low
(P2, 2 hours). Where: the touch jump's 220 ms minimum hold is timed on
the wall clock from the press (inputEngine.ts 7, 289-304), not from
take-off; the cut-off is at engine.ts:1870. Take-off comes 0-33 ms after
the press, so heights range 100-108 px. During hitstop (0.045-0.11 s,
fight.ts MOVES) the engine is frozen but the timer runs, so a jump
flicked as a punch lands gives about 57 px instead of 108. Below 20 fps
the 0.05 s frame cap (engine.ts:948) does the same. Fix: keep jumpHeld
true until 0.22 s of game time after take-off, for touch and Pad.

CT-13. The Pad lesson's text is false (P2, 15 minutes for the text).
CoachOverlay.tsx:15 says "Hold Jump a little longer to go higher", but
the 220 ms minimum hold already runs past the point where the cut-off
matters, so tap and hold differ by 8 px at most, and touch has no short
hop at all. Fix: change the text, or let the Pad's Jump skip the minimum
so a tap is a hop. That's a design call for Matt.

CT-14. No feedback in Screen mode, and iPhones have no vibration (P2,
about 1 day). Where: TouchController.tsx 150-160 draws nothing; the only
feedback is haptics (39-42), which iOS doesn't support. Fix: a light
overlay with a faint ring at the steering anchor and a chevron for the
run direction, plus a 150 ms burst and label at the finger for Jump,
Punch, Kick or Mega. Make it a Show touches setting, on by default for
the first stages.

CT-15. The palm rule eats second-thumb taps (P2, 2 hours). Where: a
second finger within 70 px of the steering thumb is ignored except for a
flick up (touchGestures.ts 341, 397-406, 458). In portrait on a 320-390
px phone, running right slides the steering thumb toward the other one,
and taps and kicks vanish. Fix: palmPx 45, and ignore a finger only if
its reported radius is 22 px or more or it never moves.

CT-16. A gripping palm, or a finger whose lift was never reported, can
take over steering (P2, 3 hours). There's no rejection by touch radius
or screen edge, the finger list is never checked against e.touches, and
recovery waits for every finger to lift (TouchController.tsx 82-95). On
big phones the gripping palm touches the edge first, and the real thumb
then counts as a wobble, so Pokey won't run. Fix: ignore a touch for 150
ms if its radius is 25 px or more, or it starts within 6 px of a side
edge, unless it moves. On every touchstart, drop fingers not in
e.touches.

CT-17. Punches wait for the finger to lift (P2, half a day). Where: a
gesture tap punches on lift (touchGestures.ts 442, 471); the Pad's Punch
fires on release (PadController.tsx 164-178) while its Kick fires on
press. Fix: on the Pad, punch on press and upgrade to mega at 320 ms.
For gestures, start the jab on press for the action finger and cancel it
if the finger travels more than 22 px within 60 ms (the jab's startup is
0.06 s).

CT-18. Pad buttons have no extra touch area (P2, 2-3 hours). A touch
that misses by a few px does nothing; the gap between Jump and Kick is
15-22 px (PadController.tsx 222-224). Fix: an invisible hit area 1.3x
each button, and any touch in the buttons' half goes to the nearest
button.

CT-19. The Pad's default side (P2, decision for Matt, 5 minutes). The
movement pad defaults under the right thumb (controlPrefs.ts:37); most
mobile games put movement on the left. Recommended: left by default, or
ask once the first time someone switches to Pad.

CT-20. Circle-carving can leak out of Meeca's stage (P2, 30 minutes).
Where: carveSpot is only written when this.mc exists (engine.ts:1165);
the InputEngine lives for the whole app (App.tsx:255) and clearAll never
resets it. Leave 5-M while standing on a carving spot and later stages
read curved swipes as circles. Dot's stage (stepDot, engine.ts
5759-5763) doesn't reset onSwing, inCover or carveSpot either. Fix: set
carveSpot = !!this.mc?.carveSpot every frame, and reset those flags in
clearAll and stepDot.

CT-21 to CT-27 (P3, under 4 hours together). CT-21: the rope hop ignores
the flick's lean (engine.ts:4274); use leapDir. CT-22: a still press of
421-460 ms does nothing (tapMs 420, holdMs 460), and a left-half
steering finger can never mega by holding; set tapMs 400 and holdMs 450
and count the in-between as a punch. CT-23: two "20 degree" constants
differ (vineLeapSlant 0.36, leanSlant 0.364); make them one. CT-24:
accidental double jumps, because the one-finger wiggle accepts a rise as
slow as 0.1 px/ms (pumpSpeed, 108) and the second finger's re-jump
(airAgain, 511-528) has no speed check; set pumpSpeed 0.25, pumpDown 16,
and require 0.3 px/ms in airAgain. CT-25: the Pad re-renders React on
every touchmove (PadController.tsx:137); move the knob through a ref and
a CSS transform. CT-26: pressing Jump while the pad is held up gives no
new press because of the jumpSrc set (40-50). CT-27: a decision for
Matt: should a standing flick leaning 35 degrees or more carry forward
at 0.75 of run speed? Today it goes straight up (physics.ts 83-92).

CT-28. No sensitivity or handedness settings in Screen mode (P2, half a
day after CT-8). Add a sensitivity slider (0.75 to 1.35) that scales
every distance threshold, and "Steer with: Left / Right / Either".
Either is CT-8's unified behaviour; Left and Right flip splitAt, which
gives left-handed players a true mirror in Screen mode too.

CT-29. Tilt mode needs building (P2, 2-3 days plus phone testing).
Settings: Touch controls becomes Screen | Pad | Tilt, with Calibrate,
Sensitivity and Invert. iPhone: the first enable must call
DeviceMotionEvent.requestPermission() and
DeviceOrientationEvent.requestPermission() from inside a tap; if denied,
fall back to Screen and say so. Android needs no prompt; check that
events arrive within 1 s. Read devicemotion accelerationIncludingGravity
and rotate it into screen axes by screen.orientation.angle (left-right
is device x at 0, device y at 90 and 270 with opposite signs, -x at 180;
verify the signs on a real phone). Low-pass filter alpha about 0.25;
zero point captured at Calibrate, at each stage start and on every
orientation change; dead zone 6 degrees with 2 degrees of hysteresis;
full run at 20 degrees, well below the angle that rotates the screen. In
tilt, finger steering is off; jump, punch, kick and climb stay as
gestures and every finger is an action finger. Pause on
visibilitychange. Motion sensors may be blocked inside Matt's Claude
test link (an iframe), so test tilt on the GitHub Pages site.

Sound and music: C− against AAA, C+ against indies

The verdict: the music is ambitious and mostly works. It's composed live
from sampled orchestral instruments, with a theme for each screen and
each world, which few first games attempt. The effects are the weak
side: the default set is the "pingy" one Matt dislikes, loudness swings
20 dB between effects, several big moments are silent or borrow the
wrong sound, and nothing ducks under speech. Alto's Adventure and Leo's
Fortune are the reference here: few sounds, all from one family, all
sitting under the music. No sound was heard for this review; every
number comes from measuring the files and reading the code, so a
listening pass on a phone comes after these fixes.

Grades inside: effects C−, music B−, mix and feel C−.

SN-1. The old "pingy" effects are the default (P1, S once decided; was
#6). Where: src/audio/sfxOrch.ts:42 DEFAULT_ORCH_SFX = false, so players
hear the classic set (bells, kalimba, steelpan; the checkpoint is synth
bells, marimba and a 6.5 kHz hiss) unless they find the switch in
menuParts.tsx 288-318. The master chain in src/audio/core.ts adds a +5
dB shelf at 3.2 kHz on everything, which sharpens the ping. Decision for
Matt: make the orchestral set the only set and delete the switch
(recommended, and it matches "new replaces old"), or keep the old one.
Either way cut the master shelf to about +2 dB.

SN-2. Effects swing 20 dB in loudness (P1, S). The 151 Kenney effects
run from −25.2 LUFS (uiClick_0) to −5.5 (uiConfirm_3); variants of the
same sound differ up to 10 dB (uiGlass, swishD 9.4, uiSelect 9.2), so
one button tap is loud and the next quiet. uiClick_3 and uiGlass_3 peak
over 0 dBTP. Jump and flower, the two most frequent sounds, have the
highest trim (1.45). Fix: batch-normalise every effect to about −16 LUFS
short-term with true peak at −1 dBTP or lower (two-pass ffmpeg
loudnorm), keep variants within 1.5 dB, then reset TRIMS in sfx.ts
(about 1067) so most sit at 0.8-1.2, with jump and flower lowest so they
sit under the music. The per-effect table is in the scratchpad
(va/sfx_k.tsv).

SN-3. Silent or wrong sounds at key moments (P1 for death and respawn,
P2 for the rest; was #19). Respawn makes no sound (loseLife, engine.ts
4342, only plays playDefeat at Game Over). Falling into water plays a
whoosh and a punch hit, never a splash (2096-2103). playBossAppear,
playOneUp, playUiConfirm and playUiBack exist and are never called.
Small hops land silently (landing only sounds above vy 140). Puzzle
events borrow unrelated sounds (5643-5648, 6557-6618, 6743-6749): the
whoosh for six different actions, the checkpoint sound for reset, and
the player-hurt sound for "wrap", which tells the player they were hurt
when they weren't. Fix: add a splash (2-3 variants), a respawn pop, a
boss-appear roar and sting, a real checkpoint stinger (0.8-1.2 s,
bright, in key), a soft landing for every landing scaled by fall speed,
and one sound per puzzle action (about 12). Wire the four unused calls
or delete them. About 20 new effects, CC0 (Kenney, Sonniss GDC bundles,
Freesound with the CC0 filter), 44.1 kHz mono, normalised as SN-2, 3-5
variants each.

SN-4. Every boss tell sounds like a jaguar (P2, S; was #11, in Build 1).

SN-5. The victory sting fires in the level, so the Stage Cleared card
has only star chimes (P2, S). engine.ts 2659-2661 plays the full sting
with "SANCTUARY UNLOCKED! VICTORY!" on reaching the exit. Play a short
chime in the level and save the full sting for when the card's ribbon
lands, with count-up ticks (UI-2).

SN-6. Pausing switches to the World 1 tune (P3, S). audio/scenes.ts
168-180. Keep the current world's tune at 45% volume through a low-pass
filter while paused.

SN-7. Nothing ducks under speech, and Dot is silent in play (P2, S).
Story stings duck the music to 0.12 but speech blips never duck it. Duck
the music 4-6 dB (attack 80 ms, release 400 ms) under any speech or
story line, and give Dot a soft chirp when her bubble opens (once per
line, not per word). Her voice exists in speech.ts and the DOT motifs,
used only in the story player.

SN-8. Some instruments jump in volume at their loop point (P3, P2 if
Matt hears it; 1 day). 115 of 220 sustained samples loop over a 1.8 s
region, with level steps up to 7 dB (oboe_89 7.08, oboe_82 6.63,
flute_96 6.62). Mp3 encoder delay makes the seams worse. Re-pick loop
regions in a steady part of each note matched within 1 dB, and store
samples as WAV or Opus with exact loop points.

SN-9. Ambience is fully synthesized (P2, S plus sourcing). Add one
recorded rainforest bed per world type, CC0, 60-90 s, seamless, about
−28 LUFS.

SN-10. The boss intro card plays no sound (P3). Use the boss-appear
sting from SN-3.

Story and cutscenes: C− against AAA, C+ against indies

The verdict: the story has heart (Pokey, Dot, Meeca's trail, the
carvings in 5-M) and Matt says it's what keeps players going, so it
deserves the most polish per hour. Right now the cutscenes are softer
than the gameplay, the characters look pasted onto the paintings, and
two story threads are left hanging: the 5-6 payoff for Meeca's carvings
was never built, and the Stage Cleared clue lines repeat every world.
Oddmar's comic panels and Rayman's short intros are the bar: few frames,
every one of them crisp.

ST-1. Story scenes are blurry, stand on a code-drawn strip, and
sometimes don't match the words (P1, M; was #1). Where:
src/components/StoryPlayer.tsx:162 caps the canvas at dpr 1.25 in
landscape and 1.5 in portrait, so on a 1080p-class phone the browser
stretches the story canvas 2.1x. A standing Pokey cel is 171 px in the
atlas (storyCels.ts, CEL_K = 270/205) and ends up about 609 physical
pixels tall at the story camera's 1.35 zoom: a 3.6x upscale (3.85x on an
844x390 phone). The 1024x572 plates come out about 2.35x. paintFinish
(storyArt.ts:1181) then blends a 1/6-size bloom at 0.34 over everything.
The figures stand on a green scalloped strip drawn in code (drawBranch,
storyArt.ts 81-137) that slices through the painting, the text box
covers Pokey from the waist down in 7-1, and in 7-1 and the World 1
opening the words describe things the picture doesn't show. Fix, cheap
first (1-2 hours): raise the story dpr cap to 2 in both orientations
(about 1830x824, 6 MB), cut the bloom to 0.15-0.2 and apply it only to
highlights, cap close-ups so nothing draws above 1.5x its source, and
keep feet above the text box in both orientations. Then the art:
AI-upscale every cel 2.5x with VI-1's edge fix and hand-touch the faces
(2-3 days), or repaint them (2-3 weeks); a standing Pokey becomes about
430-450 px (CEL_K about 270/512). Plates at 2560x1430. Load atlases per
scene, not all three. Gemini prompt per plate: "Hand-painted storybook
rainforest scene, [scene description], soft gouache and watercolor, warm
cinematic lighting, clear open ground along the bottom third where
characters will stand, no characters, no text, 16:9, 2560x1440." Audit
every scene for words that don't match the picture and list the missing
pictures for Matt.

ST-2. Characters aren't lit like the scene, and their shadows are weak
(P2, about 1 day of code). storyArt.ts drawStoryScene (1281) applies the
world grade before drawing the actors, so they never take on the scene's
colour. The contact shadow (shadowFor, storyActors.ts) is one flat
cool-grey ellipse at 0.55. The folk cels have a light grey halo from the
grey key. Fix: apply the grade after the actors, or tint actors 8-15%
toward each plate's main colour with a thin rim light on the lit side;
use a two-layer shadow (a tight dark ellipse, ry about 0.12 of rx at
0.6, plus a soft broad one at 0.25) tinted from the floor colour;
defringe cels-folk with VI-1's script; replace the code-drawn branch
with the painting's own ground or a painted 2048x300 ground strip per
world.

ST-3. Meeca's carvings never pay off (P2, M-L). 5-M has Meeca carve five
messages. 5-6 "Where She Ran" was meant to be where Pokey finds them,
but meecaCarved.ts:4 says "it is not built yet" and carvingsForPokey()
is never called; 5-6 is a generic hunt for three "messages" using World

as the relics, each read in Meeca's words, and call carvingsForPokey().
Check whether meecaCarveData already has the carving plates; if not,
five painted plates.

ST-4. The Stage Cleared clue repeats every world (P2, S code, M
writing). stageClue.ts 2-18 has 9 clues keyed by stage number only, for
102 stages. 5-M reads "M" as no number, falls back to 1, and shows
"Meeca's Petal… She came this way" on Meeca's own stage; every boss

ST-5. The exit has three names and the same victory line everywhere (P2,
S; was #21, in Build 1, plus one more spot). Build 1 settles sanctuary,
Exit Shrine and shrine. Also change engine.ts 2659-2661, which shows
"SANCTUARY UNLOCKED! VICTORY!" in caps on every stage of every world
(World 10's fortress isn't a sanctuary), and stagePlan.ts:171 ("to open
the sanctuary" everywhere): one sentence-case exit line and goal noun

ST-6. Boss quotes and aim text read as placeholder (P3, S).
worldsData.ts boss quotes ("Sssilly sloth…") and all-caps aim text ("TOO
TOUGH! HIT THE GLOW"). Rewrite in the story's voice, in sentence case,

ST-7. The ending card's copy is out of date (P2, with UI-5).
StatusModals.tsx 189-192: "passed one hundred trials" (there are 102
stages), "MEECA IS SAVED!" and "VISIT WORLD MAP" in caps. Final copy in

ST-8. Placeholder stage names (P3, S). For example 1-5 "More Petals".

Dot and hints: C against AAA, B− against indies

The verdict: Dot works, and the tester data shows it. Outside testers
almost all play with Teach me, and newcomers finish stages. The system
under her is thoughtful (a calm pace in Worlds 1-4, priorities, an

Level design and difficulty: C+ against AAA, B against indies

The verdict: Worlds 1-4 are well taught and hand-built, and their
teaching is the best in the game. The new generator (5-7, 5-9 and World
6) is the best level design in the game, A− on its own: 46-57% different
on every visit, 10-18 s of dead time, checkpoints never more than about
4000 px apart, water always signposted, and every new animal introduced
safely. The rest is held back by three things: Normal's difficulty stops
rising after World 2, World 5 is three different builders stitched
together, and the older stages break rules the new generator follows
(water before it's taught, long checkpoint gaps, padding). Leo's Fortune
and Rayman are the bar for a rising curve and for never killing a player
with something they haven't been shown.

By world

-   World 1, the lowlands: well taught, B+. Look-alike pair 1-1 and 1-5
    (98% alike), checkpoints up to 8019 px apart, one look per visit.

-   World 2, the river village: mostly finished, B. Water is taught too
    late in 2-3, 2-4 runs about 8 minutes on a phone, and 2-1 on Easy
    loses more lives than on Normal (hand-check).

-   World 3, the grotto: the most finished of Worlds 2-5, B+. 3-5, 3-7
    and 3-8 have hits nothing teaches, and 3-8 can drown the player
    before the tip.

-   World 4, the Mist Cascades: lots of content but rough, B−. 4-7 is
    under 2 minutes for a fast player, a jump in 4-8 failed every
    scripted try in both modes, the eagle's first strike in 4-5 has no
    cover nearby.

-   4-D, Dot's Netting Flight: carefully designed (each flower is a
    checkpoint, every roll is proven flyable), provisionally B. The
    inspector skips it, so it needs a hand test on a phone in both
    orientations.

-   5-M, Meeca's stage: a strong idea (five puzzle rooms, none costs a
    life, each fits an upright phone), provisionally B− until its payoff
    exists (ST-3).

-   World 5, the Orchid Sanctuary: the least finished of Worlds 1-6, C.
    5-1 to 5-5 are hand-built, 97-98% the same on every visit, with
    22-32% dead time; 5-6 and 5-8 are on the old generator (5-6 even
    uses World 1-3 animals); 5-7 and 5-9 are on the new one. Three
    looks, three levels of quality, and only 6-7 Dot lines in the whole
    world.

-   World 6: the reference, A−.

-   Bosses 2-5, B−: inventive with clear tells (the Shadow Prowler's
    "glowcaps go dark" phase is the best set piece), held back by
    missing coaching (DOT-16), code-drawn lairs (VI-14) and the old
    intro card (UI-5).

Easy and Normal

Easy, C+ against AAA and B against indies. It's a real on-ramp, and
Sally is the proof: she'd never played a game and now finishes stages.
It gives longer coyote time and jump buffer (0.167 s against 0.133), a
12 px landing allowance (8 on Normal), full assist for the whole game,
softer jump heights, stepping stones over deep drops, wider lily pads
and creek stones, gentler and fewer animals, 0.6x fight goals, a flag
every 1800 px with a heart at each, slower vines with a 300 ms release
window, and bosses with 0.7x the hits and 1.3x the tells. Its gaps: the
player can't see they're on Easy (LV-1), it's kinder on new-generator
stages than on the hand-built ones (LV-4), it loses more lives than
Normal on 2-1 and 4-7 (LV-5), and some targets are hollow (LV-6).

Normal, C against AAA and B− against indies. Its assist fades from full
at 1-1 to nothing by stage 25, which is a good ramp, but the stages
themselves stop getting harder (LV-2), and checkpoint gaps of 7000-8000
px on hand-built stages mean 45-60 s of replay after a death.

decision for him (LV-3), not a settled fix:

    doubleAllowed ignores difficulty). The scripted check finds the same

-   "Normal felt too easy, collect 80 of 133." 80 is Easy's bar (2-2

Performance and tech: C against AAA, B− against indies

The verdict: Android holds 30 fps well, and input sampling at 30 fps is
solid (A−). iPhones in portrait are where it slips, and the causes are
known, ordinary canvas costs. The download is heavy for a phone game.
Super Mario Run and Alto's Adventure never drop a frame on mid-range
phones, and that smoothness is a big part of why they feel premium.

PF-1. iPhones in portrait drop frames (P1 on iPhone, M). From the
testers' own frame logs: iPhone portrait median 27.4 fps with the worst
10% of moments at 21; Android landscape 30 and 29.6; Android tablet 28.2
and 26.3. Across all 86 sessions the recorder logged 247 stalls, 226
long frames, 135 blank frames and 92 camera jumps. Known causes in the
code: shadowBlur set while drawing at about 29 sites (the swing
sweet-spot ring every frame, engine.ts:5399); gradients rebuilt every
frame for hearts, glows, rope posts and water; ctx.filter hurt flashes
(AN-6); particles and pop-ups allocating new objects every frame with no
cap. Fix: cache gradients and glow sprites once, pre-render the glows
that use shadowBlur, pool particles with a cap, and replace the filters.
Then split the recorder's blank and camera-jump events by device and
stage to find any that remain.

PF-2. The download is heavy (P3, M-L; was #28). The main JavaScript is
one 3.25 MB chunk (1.03 MB gzipped). The offline precache is 574 files,
76 MB, and every first visit downloads all of it, Worlds 5-10 included.
Precache Worlds 1-2 and the shell, cache later worlds on demand, and
split the code by world. If the story cels are upscaled (ST-1), load
atlases per scene to keep memory flat.

PF-3. Leftovers (P3, S; was #29). package.json is still named
react-example. Unused dependencies: @google/genai, express, dotenv,
@types/express, lucide-react, motion. metadata.json declares the AI
Studio Gemini capability and still describes a sloth saving "his
kidnapped girlfriend". vite.config.ts has devOptions.enabled on, which
is why dev-dist/ keeps changing. reference/HANDOFF.md is out of date
(creature sheets are now released per world; the precache is 76 MB, not
52).

The hardest gaps for one developer working with AI, in order:

1.  Feel on real phones. AI can script the engine and replay the
    testers' touches, but it can't hold a phone. Every controls build
    needs Matt and a few testers to run the phone test, with the
    recorder as the referee. This is the one place AI can't close the
    loop alone.

2.  Art consistency at high resolution. Image tools drift in style from
    piece to piece, and the targets are big (native 3072-4096 px
    backdrops for ten worlds, soil kits, about 15 World 5 pieces, 349
    story cels). Use one style reference sheet and one prompt template
    per world, generate on flat magenta, and run everything through the
    fixed edge pipeline. Upscale the cels before considering a repaint.

3.  Sound taste. Normalising and measuring is mechanical; choosing
    effects that sound like one family, and judging the mix on phone
    speakers, needs ears. Plan one focused listening session after Build
    6.

4.  Animation depth, the gap to an A against AAA. Rayman's characters
    have dozens of hand-animated frames per move. The motion model and
    the existing clips can carry Pokey and the bosses to a B+; an A here
    would take hand-animation or a hired animator.

5.  Tuning difficulty across about 100 stages. LV-2 is long, careful
    work. The bands inspector and the scripted first-timer make it
    measurable, so it's grind, not mystery.

An honest read on the AAA A: it's within reach for a polished Worlds 1-6
slice, and much harder across all ten worlds at once. If the goal is to
show what one person with AI can do, the strongest proof is a Worlds 1-6
build polished to this standard before Worlds 7-10 grow further.

Phone test you can run yourself (about 10 minutes)

Use the GitHub Pages build, landscape first, then portrait. Write down
the counts; they tell the coders which fixes landed.

1.  Distance jumps, 2 minutes. On a flat run with a gap, run right with
    one thumb and flick up and forward, lifting at once, 10 times. Then
    10 more keeping the thumb on the glass. If the lift-off jumps land
    shorter or vary more, that's CT-1.

2.  Trunk and liana, 2 minutes. Climb with relaxed, slightly slanted
    strokes, stop halfway and hold still for a second. Count unwanted
    leaps (CT-3), slides past where you stopped, and drops off the side
    (CT-4). Switch to Pad: climb, let go, push up again. If he leaps,
    that's CT-2. Try a liana with the Pad.

3.  Two thumbs, 1 minute. Steer with the left thumb, rest the right
    thumb on the glass about a second, then flick it up. A mega and no
    jump is CT-7. On a vine, flick the right thumb straight up: if he
    lets go, that's CT-5.

4.  Small flicks, 1 minute. Standing still, do 10 tiny, quick flicks up
    (about 1 cm). Count jumps, punches and nothing (CT-9).

5.  Edges, 1 minute. Start a run from the very left edge; on Android
    watch for the back arrow or leaving the page. Start a jump flick
    from the bottom edge: is it eaten, or does the toolbar appear
    (CT-6)?

6.  Finger on Pokey, 1 minute. Facing right, then left, start runs by
    touching Pokey. Does one way feel slower (CT-8)?

7.  Picture, 1 minute, portrait. In 1-1 and 6-2, look at the leaf edges
    for a dark outline (VI-1), whether the backdrop looks soft next to
    Pokey (VI-2), whether it shudders while you run (AN-4), and the soil
    for seams and repeats (VI-7, VI-8).

8.  Sound, 1 minute, phone speaker at middle volume. Collect flowers and
    jump for 30 seconds: do they poke above the music (SN-2)? Fall in
    water: is there a splash (SN-3)? Pause: does the tune change (SN-6)?

9.  Win card, portrait. Finish any stage: is Pokey on the card (UI-1)?

Optional: film your thumb and the screen together in slow motion (240
fps) and count frames from the thumb moving to Pokey moving. More than
about 20 frames (over 80 ms) feels sluggish.
