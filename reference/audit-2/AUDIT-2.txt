Sloths audit two: grades and the next level
Oct 30, 2026 · @Matt Shaffer
Start here
This is the second full audit of Sloths, in the same shape as the first one: every category graded against the big studios and against indies, both modes graded, each issue with its place in the code, its fix, effort and any art, then a path to the target and the builds in order. It's a review only. Nothing in the repo or on any test link was changed.
Where the game stands. Like for like, the worlds the first audit graded plus World 7 are now B− against the big studios and B+ against indies, up from C and B−. That's a real jump in a short time. The whole game, counting Worlds 8-10 as a player meets them today, is C+ and B, because those worlds still run on the old generator.
The biggest movers. Animation and game feel moved two steps against AAA (the death beat, dust, sparks, shake and smooth parallax all landed). Controls, story and cutscenes, and Dot each moved a step and a half. Menus, visuals and Easy each moved a step. Of the first audit's 138 issues, 94 are fixed or decided.
The three things holding it back.
Level pacing across worlds. Normal stops getting harder after World 2, World 5 is still padded, and Worlds 8-10 are old-generator stages with 29 to 38% dead time and pits in the first few seconds. Everything that could be fixed inside a stage moved; the shape of the game across worlds didn't.
Sound. It's still the lowest category. The effects are set too hot, each world shares one short loop, and respawning is silent.
The ordinary-phone experience. A 160 MB download, 16 slow seconds at the start on an older Android, no clean iPhone number, and touches that wait for slow frames on mid-range phones.
What to do first. Build 1 (Builds in order) is two or three days of code and fixes what a new player hits in the first minute: being asked Easy or Normal, the stuck 2-3 goal card, approved Dot lines that can't silently revert, a respawn sound, and the early hits in Worlds 8-10. Then your four follow-ups as Build 2, then sound, controls, picture and levels, then Worlds 8-10 and the bosses in your order.
Where I push back.
I'd build the Magma Drake before the World 9 canopy, because its design and art are ready and the canopy needs a mock-up, five art pieces and a new section type.
The hold move as drafted contradicts the vine rule every player has learned since 1-1. I recommend "freeze on the flash" instead (WIP-18).
The world branches are hundreds of commits behind and edit the same files. Merge them one at a time, World 8 first, before any new World 8-10 work goes on them (WIP-10).
Start the Play Store's 14-day closed test as early as you have a build you're happy for testers to see, because it sets the earliest launch date.
Your calls are listed at the end of Builds in order, in the order the builds need them. I'll ask them one at a time.

How this was reviewed
This audit is against main at 949abd98 (release PR #53, merged Oct 30, 5:53 PM ET) and the live GitHub Pages site. The first audit was against 02ac7f5 on Oct 29; about 1,049 commits landed in between. Nothing in the repo was changed and nothing was pushed or published.
Played: the title, How to Play, Worlds 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9 and 10, the 6-10 boss from its story scene through to Game Over, and the pause menu, in a cloud browser held at landscape phone size (1024x600) with the keyboard. Portrait can't be played in that browser, so portrait is judged from code and measurements. The Stage Cleared card can't be reached there, so it's judged from code.
Read and measured: every first-audit issue was checked against the current code, not against commit messages, with file and line. Art and sound claims come from measuring the shipped files at real phone sizes; nothing was heard.
Real players: the Sloths control tests recorder sheet where it could be read, weighted to rows from builds after the first audit.
Grades: two per category, against big-studio mobile platformers (Super Mario Run, Rayman Adventures / Rayman Jungle Run) and against independent ones (Leo's Fortune, Oddmar, Alto's Adventure). A holds up next to the best of that group, B is solid and shippable in it, C is noticeably behind, D is a clear weak spot. Easy and Normal are graded separately.
Priorities: P1 is noticed in the first minutes or breaks play; P2 is noticeable over time or in one area; P3 is finish. Effort: S is under half a day, M is 1-3 days, L is a week or more. Issue ids keep the first audit's numbering; new issues continue each category's numbers.
Worlds 7-10 and bosses 6-10 are being polished now. This time they are graded, with that marked, because they are most of what's left before launch.
Visuals and art: B− against AAA (was C+), B+ against indies (was B)
The verdict. This is the biggest visible change since the first audit. The backdrops are now native resolution, with no dark outline, no seams and no pop-in. Every world has painted soil, and the water, gates, flags, pickups, ropes and boss attacks are all painted. Worlds 7 and 8 at night look like an indie A. The reason it isn't higher is what sits right in front of the player:
In World 1, about 230 of the logs, stones and bridges you stand on are still drawn by code.
Every boss fights on code-drawn floors and ledges.
Bank ends are cut off with a hard vertical edge.
The back two backdrop layers merge into one busy plane in the dark worlds.
The canvas is capped at 2x, so on most iPhones and recent Androids everything is stretched 1.5x.
The helper who measured the art files graded this B / A−. I played it and took it down one step, because what I saw on screen (World 1's code ledges, flat teal water in 2-3, twin slabs in 4-5, code flowers in 9-3, a smeared boss sprite in 6-10) is exactly what a reviewer screenshots.
The bar: Rayman Adventures and Oddmar hand-paint every surface you touch, boss arenas included, and separate their background layers by value. Each layer back is lighter and thinner-lined, so the play layer always pops.

New issues
VI-19. World 1's thin platforms are drawn by code (P1, M, needs art).
Why P1: it's the first world and the first minutes, and the painted kit sits right beside the code pieces.
Where: protoArt.ts:773-785 paintedPlatforms. For World 1, ownKit() is false, so only branches are painted. Everything else goes to the vector painters: thinArt.ts drawLog, drawStone and drawRuin, and spanArt.ts bridge and raft.
Measured over 1-1 to 1-9: 100 logs, 54 moss stones, 69 canopy bridges, 8 ruins. That's about 231 code-drawn surfaces next to 312 painted banks.
Fix:
Give World 1 its own thin-kit pieces.
Drop the ownKit() condition.
As a same-day stopgap, send style === 'log' to the painted push-log art (drawLogArt).
Then delete thinArt, spanArt, shelfArt, columnArt and worldLedges, and the ?paint=0 switch (protoArt.ts:25-32). Matt's rule is no on/off switches.
Art (about 7 pieces, on flat green): log 512×128 (2 variants), mossy stone slab 384×128 (2), ruin lintel 384×128, rope-and-plank bridge segment 256×160 that tiles plus an end post 128×256, raft 384×128.
Prompt: "Side-view platform pieces for a 2D rainforest platformer, hand-painted storybook style with clean dark ink outlines, matching the attached World 1 kit: a mossy fallen log with a cut end, a flat mossy stone slab, a carved ruin lintel, a rope-and-plank bridge segment that tiles left to right, a log raft; flat tops lit from the upper left, darker undersides, no ground, no shadows, flat green #00FF00 background, each piece separated."
VI-20. Boss arena floors and ledges are code-drawn in all ten fights (P2, L in total, needs art).
Where: lair*Objects.ts, 36-71 vector calls each, with no images. Examples: the Croc's mud bank (lairCrocObjects.ts:31-606) and the Queen's silk branches (lairQueenObjects.ts:28-452). Even the five painted lairs have code ledges in front.
Why it matters: the camera zooms out in fights, so the floor is a big share of the frame.
Fix: one arena kit per lair, drawn through the platformSprites path, with the collision boxes unchanged.
Order: Croc and Queen first (already plated), then the rest. Bosses 9 and 10 get theirs with the redesign.
Art per lair: a floor strip 1024×384 that tiles; ledges at 256, 384 and 512 × 128; a trunk or pillar 256×512 that tiles vertically, plus a cap; a ladder or rope 64×512.
Prompt: "Side-view arena pieces for a 2D boss fight, [LAIR: moonlit mangrove mire / silk hollow / ...], hand-painted storybook style with clean ink outlines matching the attached lair painting's palette and light, a floor strip that tiles left to right, three wooden/stone ledges, a trunk segment that tiles vertically with a top cap, flat green background."
VI-21. Bank ends: one per world, a hard cut below it, a smudged inner edge (P2; code S now, art M). This is Matt's "repainted bank ends" follow-up.
Where: groundAtlasData.ts. Every world has exactly one end piece, 384×576, drawn 300 units tall (END_H, groundPaint.ts:26). The left ends are the same piece mirrored.
What's wrong:
Below the 300-unit piece, the soil is clipped to a wobbly line with no ink and no shadow (groundPaint.ts:214-235). Pit walls are often 400-900 units tall, so most of the face is the plain cut. I saw it live at the end of 7-3's walkway.
The piece's inner edge is an airbrush fade that smudges over the soil.
The lip stops 30 units short of each end (protoArt.ts:596-602) and relies on that one corner.
The repaint has started: green-key starts for gend-w1 to w10 are committed (ca781d26). Nothing is wired yet.
Fix:
Code, now: after drawSoil, stroke the face path with a 2.5-unit ink line in the world's deep colour at 0.85, and a 10-unit soft shadow band inside it.
Art, per world: 3 taller end variants (384×1152, so END_H can be 600), plus one vertically tiling face-edge strip (160×1024). Use a separate left variant, never just the mirror. The code already picks ends by hash (endFrame, groundPaint.ts:63-67).
Replace the airbrush fade with a 24 px feather painted into the art.
Prompt, bank end: "Side-view cliff edge for a 2D platformer, hand-painted storybook rainforest style with clean dark ink outlines matching [WORLD] soil (attach soil tile), a mossy grassy lip that rounds over the top-right corner and drapes down, the soil face below falling straight down with roots and small stones poking out of the broken edge, no background, flat pure green (#00FF00) background, the left edge of the soil cut straight so it can sit against a tiling soil texture, 384×1152, three different variations side by side."
Prompt, face strip: "Vertical seamless tileable strip of a broken soil cliff edge seen from the side, [WORLD] palette, ink outline along the right edge, roots and pebbles in the edge, the left side plain soil matching the attached texture, flat green background, top and bottom edges must tile seamlessly, 160×1024."
VI-22. The mid and near backdrop layers don't separate by depth in Worlds 3 and 8-10 (P2, S, no art).
Measured (median brightness L, and the share of near-black ink):
World 1: near 58, mid 66. This is right: the back is lighter.
World 10: near 37 and 30% ink, mid 37 and 31% ink.
World 3: near 34, mid 27. The mid layer is darker than the front.
Why it happens: painted sets are marked hazed: true (scenery.ts:1536), so no veil is drawn over them.
Fix: bake a per-world value lift into makeFit (scenery.ts:1938-1958): the world's haze colour at 12-20% on the mid layer, 0-6% on the near one. Target: mid at least 15 L brighter than near in every world. It's baked once, so it costs nothing per frame.
VI-23. The game canvas is capped at 2x pixel density (P2, S after the GPU move).
Where: engine.ts:974-977, cap = [2, 1.5, 1][quality]. The story player is capped at 2 too.
On 3x phones (most iPhones, Pixel 8 and 9, Galaxy S), every frame is drawn at two-thirds size and stretched.
Fix: after PixiJS, raise top quality to min(native, 3). The governor already steps down on slow phones.
VI-24. Upright story framing: the zoom is undone (P1 for story upright, M, no art). This is Matt's "upright cutscene framing" follow-up.
Where:
StoryPlayer.tsx asks for UPRIGHT_ZOOM 1.22 (up to 1.4).
capStoryZoom (storyFrame.ts:14-18) limits it to 1.5 device pixels per old cel pixel. But the cels were repainted at 2x (CEL_HD = 2.0), so the cap is twice as strict as it needs to be.
Measured with the game's own functions:
On a 412×915 phone the actual zoom is 1.00.
Pokey is 80 css px tall, which is 9% of the screen.
About 62% of the upright screen is the painted top of the plate.
49 of the 469 scenes with figures put an actor outside 15-85% of the width, so a simple zoom crops them. That's why the cap was added.
Fix:
Measure the cap against the HD cels: room = MAX_CEL_MAG / (dev * (CEL_K / CEL_HD) * actorScale).
Frame on heads: storyDirect.ts already knows speakerHeight and tallestHeight. Keep the tallest head at least 6% below the top of the band, and allow zoom up to 1.6.
Aim for Pokey at 18-22% of the screen height upright.
For wide casts, pan between speaker and listener (a 1.2-1.6 s ease, cut on each line), or squeeze positions with 0.5 + (x−0.5)·0.75.
Run the contact-sheet tool upright on all 469 scenes before and after, counting clipped heads and overlaps.
I also saw the sideways version of this in the 6-10 intro: Pokey was cut off at the waist by the words box (ST-13).
VI-25. Small code-drawn leftovers (P3, S). The shared checkpoint post, the gate's plate and motes, one rope texture, the Z marks, the vector fallbacks in vectorArt.ts and SWEEP.md's open list. Delete the fallbacks now, since sheets are preloaded.

VI-31. Soil takes about 45% of the frame in landscape (P3, S-M, check on a phone). Found live.

The soil is lovely, but it's the least interesting thing on screen, and it pushes the backdrops up.

Path to the target for Visuals (B− / B+ now; target B+ to A / A+)
Every surface you touch is painted. VI-19 first, because it's the first impression. Then the VI-20 arena kits, and WIP-1's code plants in Worlds 8-10 (which go away with the generator move).
Value-separated layers (VI-22): a one-day code change that lifts every dark world at once.
Edges and corners (VI-21): bank ends are where a painted platformer gives itself away.
Sharp on high-density screens (VI-23), once the GPU renderer lands.
Light that belongs to the scene, after the GPU move:
one light direction and colour per backdrop set;
a 1-2 px rim on Pokey and the creatures on the lit side (heroRim.ts already exists);
a colour grade per set. That's what makes Oddmar look expensive.
The hardest gap for one developer with AI is keeping AI art consistent across ten worlds and about 100 more pieces. What closes it:
one style sheet per world (the kit plus three approved pieces), attached to every prompt;
whole kits generated in one batch so they share light;
everything on flat green or magenta, through the fixed edge pipeline and artcheck.

a smooth trauma-based shake with Full, Gentle and Off;

CT-30. Letting go of a liana is now the least reliable gesture (P2, about 1.5 days).
Why P2: on a timed swing, a let-go that doesn't happen, or one that happens during a climb, is a lost life. It's the largest misread left.
Where: touchGestures.ts vineSteer (about 990-1031), the vine flick on lift (416-430, 1076), and the anti-pumping limit vineLeapMaxSlant 1.2 (151).
Replay:
12.2% of quick up-and-over flicks don't let go (88 of 724). By player: Sally 60%, Matt 9.3%, Steph 7.6%. 81 of the 88 were on a swinging liana.
28% of climbs that bend over at the end do let go.
The root cause is design, not tuning. Lianas swing themselves now, so a sideways stroke on one does nothing but set facing. The anti-pumping rule protects a stroke that no longer has a purpose, and costs real let-goes. Sally's long sweeps that end in an upward whip are read as steering.

On a swinging liana only, let go on any of:
a quick release: under 350 ms, 30 px or more, and not within 20° of straight up or down;

SN-11. Effect levels were set through the master compressor, so many takes hit it 20-40 dB too hard (P1, S-M, needs ears).
Why P1: the loudest takes are on things a player touches in the first minute (pause, back, the exit chime, heavy hits). When they fire, the whole mix, music included, ducks and recovers. That's the pumping sound big studios never ship.
Where:
tools/audio/sfxv1/calib.py, which iterates each gain until the post-compressor loudness matches the old synth effect;
src/audio/sfxRecLevels.ts, the resulting gains;

uiBack +37.1 dBFS;
leaf +32.1;
pauseIn +30.7;
hit_fur_3 +21.0;
the exit chime +17.0;
the Reaver's tell +15.5.

Normalise every take file offline: two-pass ffmpeg loudnorm to about −20 LUFS (peak-normalise short clips), true peak −1 dBTP. Drop any take that needs more than +12 dB; it's too quiet or noisy a recording.

The hardest gaps for a solo developer working with AI
Merge debt. AI helpers branch fast and don't feel the cost of drifting. Merge main into every branch daily, and rebase any branch more than about 150 commits behind before more work goes on it.
How the new moves feel on a phone. The test bots can't judge "fiddly". Play a one-room prototype of each move (hang, hold, carry under fire) before building a stage around it. The hold is the most urgent.
Boss poses drifting off model. Always attach the clip sheet itself as the reference, and re-roll before animating.
Measuring climbs. The bot can't finish any climb, so judge climbs by hand until it's fixed.
Scope. Three special stages, two boss rebuilds, three world merges and nine climbs is several weeks of work. Finish one world completely before starting the next.

The hardest gaps for a solo developer working with AI
Merge debt. This is new since the first audit, and it's now the biggest risk. Three world branches sit hundreds of commits behind and edit the same files. AI helpers branch fast and don't feel the drift. One owner per shared file per round, merging main into every branch daily, and finishing one world before starting the next closes it.
Feel on real phones. The tester sheet and the touch recorder are a real step up, but the bots still can't hold a phone, and they can't finish a climb. Every new move (hang, hold, carry under fire) needs a one-room prototype in your hands before a stage is built around it.
Sound taste. Levels and loudness are mechanical. Choosing effects that sound like one family and hearing the mix on a phone speaker needs ears. Plan one listening session after Build 3.
Art consistency on new poses. The Iron-Claw re-rolls already drift off model. Always attach the clip sheet as the reference and re-roll before animating.
Real-device speed numbers. Every speed claim waits for your phone. A nightly run on three Android test devices closes most of it; the iPhone stays a manual run each release.
Scope. Worlds 8-10 plus five bosses plus Worlds 1-5 on the new generator plus the GPU renderer is a lot of weeks. That's fine, but it means finishing in slices, each one shippable.
An honest read on the AAA A: Worlds 1-7 can get there with the builds below and your art. Getting all ten worlds there at once is much harder, and the deciding factors are sound and the two finale fights. Animation depth still caps out around B+ without hand animation or an animator.

Phone test you can run yourself (about 10 minutes)
Use the live site, upright first, then sideways. Write down the counts; they tell the coders which fixes landed. If you can, run it once on your phone and once on the oldest phone a tester has.
Fresh start, 1 minute. Clear the site's data, or use a private tab, and start a new game. Were you asked Easy or Normal? If it just started, that's LV-19.
The 2-3 goal card, 1 minute. Play into 2-3. Does the goal card leave the screen on its own? If it stays, that's UI-22.
Losing a life, 1 minute. Fall in a pit twice. Is there a sound when Pokey pops back up (SN-13)? With sound at middle volume on the phone speaker, do jumps and flowers sound too loud or squashed next to the music (SN-11)?
Liana and second thumb, 2 minutes. On a liana, let go and jump off 10 times. Count the times he didn't let go, or dropped instead of jumping (CT-30). Then rest a second thumb on the glass for about a second, 10 times, and count the megas you didn't mean (CT-31).
Climbs on every setting, 1 minute. In World 1, try the tall walls with Jump help on Off, then On. Count any wall you can't get over (LV-22).
Characters talking, 1 minute. In a World 6 or 7 stage where Pokey and Flicker talk, does every line get its reply, in order (ST-9)? Can you see Flicker while he talks (AN-12)?
Upright cutscene, 1 minute. Watch one story scene upright. Is anyone cut off at the waist or pushed off the edge (VI-24, ST-13)?
Worlds 8-10 opening, 1 minute. Start 8-2, 9-3 and 10-1 and just run right. Count the lives lost in the first 5 seconds (WIP-6).
Old phone, 1 minute. On the oldest phone, start any stage with the ?perf overlay. Note how many seconds it takes to feel smooth, and whether sounds lag behind what you see (PF-11).
iPhone, 3 minutes, when you can. On an iPhone with no profiler, play 1-1, 4-1 and one boss upright with ?perf on, and note the frame rate. That's the missing PF-5 number.
Optional: film your thumb and the screen together in slow motion (240 fps) and count frames from your thumb moving to Pokey moving. More than about 20 frames (over 80 ms) feels sluggish, and on a mid-range phone that's CT-38.
